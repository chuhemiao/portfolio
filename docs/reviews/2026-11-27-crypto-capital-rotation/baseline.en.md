- **The market is still in the later stages of liquidity contraction and deleveraging.** BTC fell **24%** in 30 days and **25.2%** in November to the observation date. It keeps ETH underweight and avoids high-beta DeFi, AI applications, and memes. Adding risk depends on stablecoin supply, sustained ETF inflows, and BTC reclaiming its **$71.9k 200-day moving average**.

### Comparison with previous cycles

- **2022.** Failures at LUNA, 3AC, and FTX damaged the credit chain. This time the sample shows ETF redemptions, stablecoin supply contraction, and futures deleveraging. Transmission is faster and more direct. The sample offers no corresponding evidence of a major crypto lender default.
- **2018.** ICO unlocks drove excess supply. In this cycle, the pressure falls more on small and mid-cap venture-backed tokens with low floats, high fully diluted valuations, and ongoing unlocks.
- **2021.** Retail leverage and DeFi yields drove altcoin gains. Perpetuals now concentrate at venues such as Hyperliquid, with wider gaps in liquidity across assets.
- **Spot ETF era.** Spot funds participate in BTC price discovery at the margin. Interest rates can affect crypto flows through creations and redemptions. Liquidity and real rates explain more of this period than a fixed four-year cycle.

## Relative strength and market breadth

In the sample, **38%** beat BTC over seven days, **29%** over 30 days, and **60%** over 90 days. The 90-day median altcoin return was **−16.7%**, ahead of BTC's **−21.1%**, over a window that includes the August–October rebound. Over 30 days the median alt fell **26.8%**, against BTC's **24.0%**, and **33%** of the sample lost more than 30%. Cross-sectional dispersion was **9.2 percentage points** over 30 days and **20 points** over 90 days. Returns were widely dispersed and usually negative, a difficult setting for long-only momentum. Median 90-day correlation with BTC was **0.59** and median beta was **1.33**.

ETH/BTC was **0.02822**, down **2.9%** in 30 days and **10.1%** in 90 days, about **3%** below its 200-day moving average. This is around the **20th percentile** of daily observations over 365 days, spanning **0.02578–0.03613**. ETH fell **29%** in 90 days. Using beta of **1.18** and BTC's return over that window, about **25 percentage points** can be attributed to BTC exposure, for a residual of about **−4 points**. The author attributes relative weakness to ETF redemptions, L2 fees taking activity away from mainnet, and the opportunity cost of staking.

In the top 200 non-stablecoin market cap, BTC accounted for **66.3%**, ETH **11.4%**, and other alts **22.4%**, subject to rounding. Stablecoins represented **13.6%** of the top 200 total. In the set of 60 comparable assets, BTC's share went from **68.7%** to **69.7%** in 90 days, a gain of **1.0 point**. BTC held up relatively well in a broad selloff, with a small increase in share. The draft sees less favorable odds for a long-BTC, short-alts pair trade than in 2022.

## Key assets

### BTC

- **Performance.** BTC fell **24%** in 30 days, **21%** in 90 days, and **33%** in the trailing 12 months, yet beat **71%** of the 30-day sample. Its 365-day maximum drawdown was **−39.6%**. Its price was about **15%** below the **$71,933** 200-day average and **18%** below the **$74,723** 50-day average.
- **Drivers and positioning.** ETF redemptions, higher real rates, and a **24%** drop in perpetual OI. OI/market cap was **3.4%**, funding **+0.0083% per eight hours**, and the long/short ratio **1.00**. Leverage has fallen and funding remains positive.
- **Valuation.** MVRV, NUPL, and SOPR are unavailable for this snapshot. The risk framework uses the draft's **$58.6k** threshold and the **$71.9k** 200-day average.
- **Conditions.** A sustained move above **$71.9k**, five consecutive sessions of ETF inflows, and stablecoin supply stabilizing would support the base case. A daily break below **$58.6k** alongside accelerating redemptions would warrant a reassessment.
- **Ranges.** Seven days **$58k–$67k**, 30 days **$52k–$75k**, and 90 days **$48k–$88k**. The **50%** base case is BTC between **$55k and $68k**.

## Conclusion

Global liquidity contraction triggered the deleveraging. Stablecoin supply, ETF flows, and perpetual OI have yet to turn together. BTC remains the benchmark, with ETH as a smaller high-beta exposure. The author keeps watching a few assets with a stronger fee-to-token link or a low beta and better downside resilience. With only **29%** of assets beating BTC in 30 days and **all 11** DeFi names lagging, evidence for a broad altcoin rotation is still missing.

**Keep stablecoins and BTC at the core, with small TRX, HYPE, and LINK positions.** Add exposure when stablecoin supply stabilizes, ETF flows turn positive, and BTC reclaims $71.9k. If BTC loses $58.6k, reduce portfolio beta first. If policy turns dovish and ETF inflows return, gradually move part of the stablecoin allocation into ETH and trading assets.
