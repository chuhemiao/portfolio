### Six Interview Tips from HR

1. What if you are late?

Although I always recommend allowing enough travel time and leaving early, delays can still be beyond your control. Adjust your mindset rather than deciding that everything is ruined or abandoning the interview. If you expect to be late, call the company in advance, say you will arrive as soon as possible, and briefly explain the reason: a school event, traffic, or a subway breakdown. Avoid saying you left home late or a previous interview overran, which may suggest this interview matters less. If the delay is long, call again after 10–15 minutes, and within 5 minutes of arrival, such as when you reach the building, to show sincerity.

As long as the interviewer has not left work, still go. On arrival, explain again and apologize. Most companies will understand.

2. Keep your thinking clear.

Companies often confirm graduates' educational dates, the year they took the CET-4 English exam, and their scores. Answer those questions promptly. Not remembering your own timeline or results creates doubts about reliability and even whether a qualification is real.

3. You can lack knowledge, but do not stay silent.

Preparation and a view on the business or role give you an advantage. Most new graduates, however, cannot know every industry deeply. If asked about an unfamiliar industry, do not freeze in silence. Say your understanding is limited and ask for an explanation. In a foreign-language interview, if you did not understand, say so and ask the interviewer to simplify.

4. Have a clear career goal even without experience.

Limited experience makes it hard for graduates to define a career goal. But someone who only wants a job or an income may appear likely to leave. Connect your strengths and personality with this industry or role. Explain how you can help the company develop, rather than only how it can develop you. After meeting candidates willing to do anything for the right salary, interviewers often appreciate a clearer plan.

5. Do not demand an unrealistic salary.

Even excellent graduates need training and adaptation before producing profit, so entry-level salaries have a relatively common market range. Asking far above it may make employers hesitate. Many high demands reflect unfamiliarity with market rates rather than arrogance. Research the range beforehand and avoid focusing only on price. Future potential is for the company to judge. You would also dislike a restaurant asking you to pay before eating.

6. Be open and optimistic; treat it as a conversation.

Many graduates make themselves feel inferior and treat an interview like an exam. It is a conversation, so avoid unnecessary tension. Looking down, staying silent, and answering every question in fewer than 10 characters gives employers little confidence about how you would handle setbacks at work.
