> **Update (02:23 UTC, 30 Oct)**: this audit runs to **00:30 UTC**. Binance then published a *Security Incident Update* giving **a root cause for the first time** (DevOps laptop → service container → unauthorised withdrawals), a $6.3B impact estimate and $3B frozen, and saying the affected addresses' authorisations have been revoked. **This piece's conclusion that no root cause had been disclosed is superseded by it** — see [Binance names a root cause](/en/blog/binance-root-cause-is-service-identity-not-7702-en). The signing infrastructure logs are still unpublished and the technical post-mortem is still outstanding, so **the missing-evidence list in section 3 still stands.**

> This piece audits public information only; it contains no non-public material. Data as of **00:30 UTC, 30 October 2026**.

## TL;DR

**No.** As of the early hours of 30 October, Binance has released no log evidence from its signing infrastructure — no signing request records, no HSM/MPC audit trail, no policy engine decision log, no third-party forensic report.

- **Binance has still not officially confirmed that the attack vector was EIP-7702.** Every technical detail about the delegation, the 50 authorisations and the malicious contract comes from on-chain analysts and third-party security firms — **not from Binance's disclosure.**
- **What is publicly visible is six statements**, all centred on "funds are safe", "withdrawals paused", "security checks under way". CZ said it "does look like a hack", while also stating plainly that he no longer manages Binance.com — **that is a shareholder's remark, not a company finding.**
- **The accounting was never defined, and it does not reconcile**: independent accounting puts outflows at **$5.643B** against **$2.62B** frozen, leaving about **$3B** — yet when CZ responded to an estimate of "about $1B", he said only "possibly a bit higher". **A threefold gap.**
- **There is no formal incident notice to users.** The announcements page still leads with listings and margin parameter changes.
- "Funds are safe" is a **solvency statement, not a security conclusion** — it answers "will users lose money", not "how was the system breached".

## 1. What was disclosed: six statements, no technical detail

Lay the official accounts' and executives' public remarks out in order and the information content is close to zero:

| UTC (29 Oct) | Source | Substance | What it does not say |
|---|---|---|---|
| 21:15 | [CZ](https://x.com/cz_binance/status/2115915423174853077) | "User funds are safe; it does look like Binance was hacked"; thanks Tether for freezing about $971M and Circle about $823M; as a shareholder expects full coverage | Intrusion path, signing method, scale of loss |
| 21:16 | [Binance](https://x.com/binance/status/2115915708504900039) | Aware of a "potential security issue", investigating, "impact on user funds not yet confirmed" | Any technical detail |
| 21:17 | [He Yi](https://x.com/heyi/status/2115915841195942020) | Reviewing all relevant information, will update through official channels | Any technical detail |
| 21:29 | [Binance](https://x.com/binance/status/2115918888429133902) | Wording changes to "user funds are completely safe" | What this rests on, how it was verified |
| 22:23 | [CZ](https://x.com/cz_binance/status/2115932500006756686) | Responding to an estimate of "about $1B lost after freezes": "possibly a bit higher, but it will be covered" | What "loss" means, where coverage comes from |
| 22:33 | [Binance](https://x.com/binance/status/2115935002139742437) | Security checks under way; withdrawals resume once fully resolved; funds safe | When, and what the problem is |

**Three shifts in wording are worth noting**: at 21:16 it was "impact not yet confirmed", at 21:29 "completely safe", and at 22:33 back to "security checks under way".

**That is a crisis communications cadence, not disclosure of investigative progress.** Thirteen minutes from "not yet confirmed" to "completely safe", with nothing verifiable in between — and if an impact assessment really was completed inside thirteen minutes, that assessment is precisely the document that should have been published.

## 2. The numbers do not reconcile: $3B vs $1B

This is the hardest contradiction in the public record, and it can be computed directly:

```text
Independent on-chain accounting, outflows   $5.643B   (21:31)
Frozen by four issuers                      $2.620B   (Tether 1.567 + Circle 0.823 + Ripple 0.2 + USD1 0.027)
                                            ─────────
Difference                                  $3.023B

The magnitude CZ accepted at 22:23          about $1B ("possibly a bit higher")
```

**A factor of three.**

The gap is not in itself an accusation — both figures may be correct and simply counting different things. At least three explanations are available: Binance itself executed partial recoveries or defensive transfers; the "outflow" figure includes consolidation between Binance's own addresses; or CZ meant net exposure after insurance and own-asset coverage.

**None of them has been stated officially.** And as long as "loss" is undefined, the promise to "cover it fully" has no verifiable object — **covering $1B and covering $3B are entirely different commitments.**

**This is the thing most worth pressing on, and the thing most easily buried under the price action.**

## 3. The missing logs

To decide between "private key leaked" and "signing system abused", the following evidence is decisive. All of it is absent:

| Evidence | What it would settle | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Signing request records** | Which service, which service account, from which environment issued the 50 authorisations; the gap between request and inclusion (20:20:59) | Not disclosed |
| **HSM/MPC audit trail** | Which key, which policy slot, whether multi-party approval was required, who approved | Not disclosed |
| **Policy engine decision log** | Why a Type-4 authorisation cleared the limit, allowlist and transaction-type checks | Not disclosed |
| **Monitoring and alert timeline** | When internal systems saw the anomaly; whether the first alert was an outflow or a code change | Not disclosed |
| **Key rotation records** | Whether `Binance 14`'s key has been replaced, when, whether the old address is retired | Not disclosed |
| **Third-party forensic report** | Independent root-cause analysis | Not disclosed |

**These are not materials that can be "assembled later". They are system logs that already existed within the first hours of the incident.**

Checking the announcements page: as of 30 October the latest entries are still the 28–29 October listing and contract notices, with one margin parameter adjustment on the 30th and **no security incident page**. That does not rule out a sync delay, but it does mean there is no formal incident notice to users — **which, in an event that suspended withdrawals, is itself anomalous.**

## 4. What this means for judgement

Without logs, public analysis can only infer from on-chain behaviour, and on-chain evidence is mute — it records what happened, not why it was permitted:

- **50 consecutive nonces, descending, exactly one effective** — more consistent with batch pre-signing than with holding a key and operating against a node transaction by transaction;
- **12 automated sweeps afterwards, several of them 23,100-gas empty polls** — the attacker held a repeatably triggerable channel, not a one-off transfer;
- **If the full private key had leaked, Binance signing a revocation would simply invite re-delegation**, yet no such tug of war has been observed — which leans weakly towards an abused signing channel rather than a key held long-term.

**But these are inferences, not evidence.**

What would settle it is exactly the signing log that has not been published: show "which service account requested 50 authorisations pointing at `0x63e842…` around 20:20" and the root cause is characterised within minutes.

**Which is why the non-disclosure is itself information**: until an investigation concludes, "key leaked" and "signing system abused" remain parallel hypotheses in the official record, not eliminated ones.

For users and counterparties, two consequences:

1. **"Funds are safe" currently rests on Binance's own statement and CZ's shareholder backing**, with no proof of reserves or third-party audit behind it.
2. **If the root cause is signing infrastructure rather than a single key, more than `Binance 14` may be affected** — exactly the risk that logs would rule out, and Binance has offered no basis for ruling it out.

## 5. Conclusion

**As of the early hours of 30 October 2026, Binance has disclosed no log evidence from its signing infrastructure, and has published no root-cause report or third-party forensic conclusion.**

The public record is six statements plus an attack chain assembled by on-chain analysts — the latter can prove *what happened*, but not *why the signature was permitted*.

**Bottom line.** Until Binance publishes signing request records and a key management audit, every conclusion about "leaked key versus abused signing system" is a hypothesis; and "user funds are safe" is a solvency promise, not a security finding.

The next signal worth watching is not the price but three things: **a formal incident notice, a proof-of-reserves update, and whether a revocation pointing at `address(0)` appears for `Binance 14`.** Until at least one of them lands, this is not technically over.
