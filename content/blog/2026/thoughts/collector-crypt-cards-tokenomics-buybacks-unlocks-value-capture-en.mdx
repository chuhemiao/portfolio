## TL;DR

**Buybacks exist, but are small and nowhere near sufficient to offset supply growth.**

- **Buyback mechanism**: Since 2026-04-19, an on-chain bot has used USDC from Gacha revenue to make frequent small $CARDS purchases. The $CARDS portion of LP fees earned by the treasury's Raydium market-making is also burned.
- **Cumulative burns**: **25.54M** tokens, or **1.28%** of total supply, primarily from two batch burns on 2026-08-29 and 2026-10-01.
- **Current buyback pace**: Approximately **$0.3M per month**, less than **4%** of average monthly company gross profit of approximately $8.5M.
- **Remaining unlocks**: **445.8M** tokens remain locked, with approximately **44.6M** unlocking monthly, worth about **$12.3M**, until 2027-07-29.
- **Conclusion**: Monthly unlock value is approximately **40 times** buybacks. The foundation and community also hold **1.04 billion already-unlocked tokens that are not circulating**, a larger supply concern. Price is mainly driven by business momentum and sentiment, with weak cash-flow support for the token.

## Comparing Value Return and Supply

**Comparing company cash flow with token supply reveals how little reaches the token.**

The comparison uses the materials' monthly estimates, preserving each metric's time window and measurement basis.

| Metric | Monthly scale | Explanation |
|---|---:|---|
| Locked-token releases | **~$12.3M** (44.6M tokens) | Team, advisors, and seed |
| CC average gross profit | ~$8.5M | Q3 2026 gross profit of $25.4M ÷ 3 |
| CC average net revenue (DefiLlama basis) | ~$11.6M | Q3 net revenue of $34.75M ÷ 3 |
| Buyback bot | **~$0.3M** | Approximately 1.1M tokens at the current pace |
| Holder points airdrop | ~$0.085M | August data |
| Buybacks / Gross profit | **~3.5%** | Buybacks to unlocks approximately **1:40** |

*All figures above are monthly estimates.*

Key assessments:

- **Most company revenue does not reach the token.** The table's monthly estimates put buybacks at 3–4% of gross profit and **2.6%** of net revenue ($0.3M ÷ $11.6M). The materials' “less than 1%” is inconsistent with these figures. Four Pillars reports cumulative withdrawals of **$45.7M USDC** from operational wallets, including $8.5M since May 2026, while the amount directed toward the token over that period was only on the order of millions of dollars.
- **The ceiling on buybacks is low to begin with.** As calculated earlier, CC's gross margin is only around 6% and is sensitive to card-buyback spreads. Allocating a large share of gross profit to token buybacks would compete with inventory replenishment and operations.
- **The largest supply concern lies outside lockups.** The foundation's 752M and community's 290M are already unlocked, totaling **1.04 billion, or 52% of supply**, versus only 445.8M still locked. Official commitments only say foundation tokens will be used in ways accretive to the token, such as partnership incentives; no lockup terms are specified.

## Conclusion

$CARDS does have buybacks: a small, high-frequency bot, LP-fee burns, and occasional batch burns. Cumulative burns total 25.54M, or 1.28%. But current monthly buybacks of approximately $0.3M are small against approximately $12.3M in monthly locked-token releases and $8.5M in company gross profit. Another 52% of supply is already unlocked and company-controlled, leaving a weak connection between the token and company cash flows.

**Conclusion**: $CARDS should currently be treated as a volatile bet on Collector Crypt's business momentum, not a cash-flow asset with a buyback-supported floor. Three signals would change that assessment:

1. An official commitment to allocate a fixed gross-profit share to buybacks, for example at least 20%;
2. Foundation-token lockups or a public usage plan;
3. Team-unlocked tokens consistently staying away from exchanges.

Until then, every monthly unlock before 2027-07-29 could create selling pressure.
