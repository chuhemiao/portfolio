## TL;DR

At **20 J/TH and $0.05/kWh**, the model puts the electricity bill for one BTC at about **$60,000**. The original draft uses **$55,000 to $65,000** as a range to watch. JPMorgan put production costs at about **$86,000** on November 18. The draft's spot snapshot of **$61,210** was about **29%** below that estimate. Difficulty was expected to rise by about **9.96%**. With equipment efficiency and electricity prices unchanged, that would lift the draft's cost scenario to about **$66,000** and bring modeled revenue per unit of hashrate to **$22 to $23 per PH/s per day** in the same sensitivity analysis. Actual revenue also depends on block timing, the BTC price, and fees.

If difficulty rises as projected and BTC stays near $60,000, the next two weeks could be among the toughest for miners this year. AI contracts can still help miners raise capital. IREN's two cases came in at about **6% and 9%**. Miners without comparable collateral or customers are more likely to sell bitcoin to raise cash. A group of miners tracked by Glassnode has cut holdings by nearly half. Miner selling can affect spot supply and the distribution of holdings. The subsidy schedule operates by block height and implies issuance of about **450 BTC per day** at the target block pace. ## $60,000 in electricity and an $86,000 production cost estimate

CryptoQuant founder Ki Young Ju used MARA's third-quarter 2026 disclosures to estimate a cost of **$62,862 per BTC**, which includes operating expenses beyond power and excludes depreciation. That provides a company-level reference. In the draft's price record, bitcoin reached about **$64,000** in February and about **$60,000** in June and July. CoinGecko's hourly series recorded **$55,711** on November 21 at 06:00 UTC. JPMorgan's broader production cost estimates were about **$77,000 to $78,000** in June, **$85,000** in September, and **$86,000** on November 18. The bank views this kind of estimate as a soft floor that helps explain when miners react. [MARA Q3 shareholder letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1507605/000150760526000026/q326shareholderletter.htm) · [Coverage of the CryptoQuant estimate](https://theinference.org/article/bitcoin-fell-below-mara-s-62-862-mining-cost-for-the-first-time-since-august) · [Coverage of CoinGecko's hourly low](https://rumos.eu/news/why-bitcoin-fell-lowest-since-september-2024) · [JPMorgan production cost estimates](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/11/324599-bitcoin-remains-well-below-jpmorgans-estimated-production-cost-intensifying-pressure-on-btc-miners/)

The bottom-up calculation uses **1.126 ZH/s** of network hashrate, a subsidy of **450 BTC per day** at the target block pace, about **3 BTC** in fees, and a price of **$61,176**. Subsidies plus fees imply normalized revenue of about **$24.6 per PH/s per day**. Electricity cost per BTC in the table divides each daily power bill by the **450 newly issued BTC**. Each row assumes one efficiency and electricity price for the whole network to show how the inputs affect the cost. A rig at **18 J/TH or better** using power at **4 cents/kWh** spends about **$43,000** on electricity per new bitcoin. At **6 cents/kWh**, older models like the WhatsMiner M66 280T, Antminer S19 XP, and Avalon A1466 are more exposed when revenue falls. The original draft cites an Antpool profitability snapshot. Actual losses vary by model, energy price, and realized revenue. **$60,000** comes from the benchmark scenario in the table. To estimate a network-wide weighted average, we would need power prices and the mix of machines at individual sites.

As mining costs approach revenue, decisions to shut down equipment or sell holdings can change. The market price can still diverge from cost estimates. ## How higher difficulty changes unit revenue

On November 14, difficulty fell **9.13%** at block 975,744, from **148.36 T** to **134.82 T**. Average block time then moved to about **9.1 minutes**, faster than the 10-minute target. The draft's mempool snapshot showed **77.1%** of the epoch complete, with **461 blocks** remaining. The readback showed **77.28%** complete with **458 blocks** left, still pointing to an increase of about **9.96%** to **148.26 T** (about 148.3 T). It put the adjustment at **22:48 UTC on November 26**, or **06:48 Beijing time on November 27**. The network will set the difficulty at block **977,760**. The estimate is close to the **149.3 T** seen on November 27, 2025. The historical series also reached **155.97 T**. The draft's price snapshot sits about **51.5%** below the record high. [mempool difficulty estimate](https://mempool.space/api/v1/difficulty-adjustment) · [Difficulty history](https://mempool.space/api/v1/mining/hashrate/3y) · [CoinGecko historical prices](https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/bitcoin/historical_data)

At a fixed price, block reward, and rig efficiency, a **9.96%** increase in difficulty divides revenue per unit of hashrate by **1.0996**. Applied to the draft's normalized baseline of about **$24.6 per PH/s per day**, that yields about **$22.4**, within the **$22 to $23** range. The modeled electricity cost per BTC also rises from about $60,000 to about **$66,000**. Under this revenue assumption, the break-even power price for a **30 J/TH** rig falls from **3.42** to **3.11 cents/kWh**. For a **13.5 J/TH** rig it falls from **7.6** to **6.9 cents/kWh**.

Calculated from difficulty, a **$61,176** price and the **3.125 BTC** subsidy give theoretical revenue of about **$28.5** before and **$25.9 per PH/s per day** after the adjustment, excluding transaction fees. The November 23 Hashrate Index roundup reported a spot index of about **$28.62 per PH/s per day**. The two calculations start from different revenue baselines. [Hashrate Index methodology](https://data.hashrateindex.com/bitcoin-compute-data/bitcoin-hashprice-index) · [November 23 weekly roundup](https://hashrateindex.com/blog/hashrate-index-roundup-november-23-2026/)

The upward adjustment reflects the block pace during the preceding epoch. If BTC stays near **$60,000** and enough miners switch off afterward, the next adjustment could be lower. At the target block pace, the next date to watch would be around **December 11** in Beijing time. A drop in difficulty could ease the pressure on miners still running. The actual direction depends on subsequent block times. [Bitcoin developer documentation](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/block_chain.html)

The latest daily mempool estimate was about **1,126 EH/s**, some **9.5%** below the **1,245 EH/s** recorded on November 27, 2025. Hashrate Index puts the 30-day average at about **1.02 ZH/s**. From an early October low, daily estimates are up over **10%**, while the draft records a roughly **25%** decline in price over the period. One possible reason is that rigs ordered when BTC was near **$87,000** are arriving. Listed miners may also be continuing with long-term hashrate investments. Both explanations need company-level evidence. A sustained change in hashrate would feed into the next retarget. [mempool hashrate history](https://mempool.space/api/v1/mining/hashrate/3y) · [Hashrate Index November 23 roundup](https://hashrateindex.com/blog/hashrate-index-roundup-november-23-2026/)

## Financing terms are diverging by miner

The draft estimates that public miner debt grew about **500%** since the second quarter of 2024. The sample and end date remain unverified. In June, VanEck compared cash against near-term construction capex for a group of companies and found a funding gap of about **$50 billion**. The draft puts IREN's estimated gap at **$21.1 billion**. The estimates apply to planned construction. Actual financing needs may change with operating cash flow, customer prepayments, and delivery schedules. [VanEck funding gap model](https://www.vaneck.com/us/en/blogs/digital-assets/matthew-sigel-a-framework-for-valuing-bitcoin-miners-as-ai-infrastructure/)

Some miners can still raise capital against signed customer contracts. The Microsoft-related GPU financing IREN announced in June was reported at **$3.65 billion** with a blended cost of about **6%**. Its fiscal year results describe it as about **$3.6 billion**. Another **$2.4 billion** GPU facility in August carried a **9%** fixed rate. In Cipher's project financing, Google agreed to backstop **$1.4 billion** of certain Fluidstack lease obligations in exchange for warrants representing about **5.4%** of equity on a pro forma basis. Access depends on the customer and the guarantees behind a deal. [June IREN financing coverage](https://www.theblock.co/news/deals/2026-06-01-iren-lands-3-65-billion-a-rated-financing-microsoft-ai-buildout-403194) · [IREN fiscal year results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1878848/000187884826000051/irenreportsfy26results.htm) · [Cipher 2025 annual report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1819989/000181998926000018/cipher2025arsvf.pdf)

In the draft's six-month equity snapshot, MARA fell from **$11.46** to **$5.58** (about **−51%**). CLSK was down **55%**, IREN **68%**, CIFR **64%**, HUT **33%**, and RIOT **7%**. The group bounced **6% to 26%** on the day, which I read as a rebound from oversold levels. At those two MARA prices, an at-the-market (ATM) offering would need to issue roughly twice as many shares at the lower price to raise the same cash. That calculation measures the shares issued.

As equity financing grows more expensive, miners without financeable AI contracts are more likely to sell BTC, especially if they depend on mining cash flow to stay in operation. MARA said in November that mining remained the foundation of its operations. Bitdeer's results in the same month disclosed investment in self-mining and cash proceeds from sales of mined BTC. The examples show why a narrower capital market window sends miners back to bitcoin cash flow. [MARA Q3 shareholder letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1507605/000150760526000026/q326shareholderletter.htm) · [Bitdeer Q3 results](https://ir.bitdeer.com/news-releases/news-release-details/bitdeer-reports-unaudited-third-quarter-2026-results-record)

## Holdings have fallen. How large is the selling pressure?

Around August 12, coverage of Blockware Intelligence figures put the monitored public miners at about **99,000 BTC**, down from **127,000 BTC** at the start of the year. The net decline of **28,000 BTC** was worth roughly **$1.78 billion** at the market price used in the report. On November 18, holdings in another group of public miners tracked by Glassnode were down **nearly half** from January, with some sales funding AI infrastructure. If that decline is applied to the earlier **127,000 BTC** starting point, a balance of **65,000 to 70,000 BTC** implies a reduction of **44.9% to 48.8%**. The samples may cover different companies. The remaining balance is a scenario in the original draft. In a separate dataset, Coinpedia cited Lookonchain tracking **108,247 BTC** worth about **$8.08 billion** in cumulative 2026 sales across 14 companies. Gross sales can include newly mined coins and differ from a net change in holdings. [Coverage of Blockware figures](https://www.chaincatcher.com/en/article/2282329) · [Glassnode's November miner analysis](https://research.glassnode.com/the-week-onchain-week-46-2026/) · [Coverage of the near-half decline](https://coinness.com/en/news/1176262) · [Coinpedia on cumulative sales](https://coinpedia.org/news/bitcoin-mining-could-become-less-profitable-as-miners-sell-btc-to-chase-ai-data-center-profits/amp/)

- **Relative to supply.** The scenario's **65,000 to 70,000 BTC** would be worth about **$4.0 billion to $4.3 billion**, around **0.33%** of the roughly **20.117 million BTC** in circulation. The network would issue the same subsidy per block if all of those coins changed hands.
- **Relative to flows.** At **450 BTC per day × 150 days**, the modeled balance resembles five months of target production. The draft records **$4.18 billion** in 30-day U.S. spot BTC ETF net outflows, about **68,500 BTC** at **$61,000 per BTC**. The 20 completed trading sessions in Farside's **October 24 to November 22** window give **$4.3394 billion** in outflows, about **71,100 BTC** at the same price. **November 19 saw $582.3 million** in net outflows and **November 20 saw $669.2 million**. These figures express the size of capital outflows using a common BTC price. [Farside U.S. spot BTC ETF flows](https://farside.co.uk/bitcoin-etf-flow-all-data/)

A month of net ETF redemptions is similar in scale to five months of target output. When demand recedes, a steady flow of miner sales can weigh on rebounds. JPMorgan said retail investors likely contributed to ETF redemptions in October and November and noted that higher-cost miners may also be selling reserves. My view is that ETF demand deserves closer attention in the near term. The effect of miner sales will depend on market depth. [Coverage of JPMorgan's November report](https://www.theblock.co/news/markets/2026-11-20-jpmorgan-bitcoin-price-correction-gap-86000-production-cost-444763)

## Issuance follows block height. Costs and holdings can change.

The current subsidy is **3.125 BTC per block**. At the 10-minute target pace, that implies **450 BTC per day** on average, or **164,250 BTC over 365 days**. Those coins would be worth about **$10 billion** at the draft's price. Actual block counts vary by day. The next halving happens at a set block height and is expected in **2028**. Miner shutdowns or sales can change short-term block timing and spot trading activity. [Bitcoin Core mainnet parameters](https://github.com/bitcoin/bitcoin/blob/master/src/kernel/chainparams.cpp) · [Bitcoin developer documentation](https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/block_chain.html)

**First, the cost curve can move.** Difficulty fell **9.13%** on November 14 and had been about **18%** below its peak in August. With equipment efficiency, power price, and block subsidy fixed, expected work per mined BTC is proportional to difficulty. A **10%** decline in difficulty lowers the modeled unit electricity cost by about **10%**. If expensive capacity drops out during a price slump, difficulty resets at the end of the epoch, potentially lowering electricity costs per BTC for the remaining miners. [mempool difficulty history](https://mempool.space/api/v1/mining/hashrate/3y)

**Second, holdings change hands.** The **127,000 BTC** to **65,000–70,000 BTC** scenario depicts miners moving treasury holdings into the market. Buyers could be spot investors or other participants. The holdings data cannot identify where all the bitcoin went. I think moving some reserves from borrowers under pressure to better-funded buyers can ease financing pressure over time. The sales still have to clear in the spot market.

**Third, miners can face different outcomes.** Operators with power below **4 cents/kWh** and rigs at **18 J/TH or better** are more likely to stay online at low BTC prices. Operators with AI or high-performance computing (HPC) contracts can find higher-value uses for some of their power. CleanSpark's chief business officer Harry Sudock argues that competition can compress mining margins over time, giving low-cost operators an advantage. [Coverage of CleanSpark management's view](https://coinpedia.org/news/bitcoin-mining-could-become-less-profitable-as-miners-sell-btc-to-chase-ai-data-center-profits/amp/)

If spot demand continues weakening, several thousand more BTC of net selling by miners per month could prolong the imbalance. Based on UTC calendar weeks, Coinbase BTC/USD closed lower for **seven weeks** in a row through **November 22**. The draft puts the ETF outflow equivalent of about **68,500 BTC** alongside that miner pressure. Around **$60,000**, the change in marginal hashrate is worth watching. Whether price stabilizes depends on buyers coming back. ## Conclusion

**Roughly $60,000 to $63,000 is a useful area for watching miner cash pressure.** The low end comes from the electricity scenario and the high end is informed by MARA's cost excluding depreciation. JPMorgan's **$86,000** estimate provides a wider measure of operating pressure. If the **9.96%** retarget expected on **November 27** in Beijing time goes through, it brings the draft's unit electricity cost to about **$66,000** and normalized revenue to **$22 to $23 per PH/s per day**. If bitcoin stays below **$65,000** and shutdowns slow block times enough, a downward adjustment around mid-December could provide relief.

**Public miners have been selling reserves, and the original scenario's holdings decline is close to half.** A remaining **65,000 to 70,000 BTC** would be worth about **$4.0 billion to $4.3 billion**. That is roughly five months of target production or one month of ETF net outflows expressed in BTC. It can add selling pressure in a weak market. Access to funding depends in part on financeable customer contracts. IREN's deals at **6% and 9%** give two points of comparison.

I'll watch three signals. **Whether the 30-day hashrate average crosses below the 60-day average** can show whether capacity is continuing to leave. **The difficulty adjustments on November 27 and around December 11** will indicate where unit costs are headed. **A turn from weekly ETF outflows to inflows** would suggest demand is recovering. I would give ETF flows the most weight. They help show whether spot demand near **$60,000** can absorb the sales.
