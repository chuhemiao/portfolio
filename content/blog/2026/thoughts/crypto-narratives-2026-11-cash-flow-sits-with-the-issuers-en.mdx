## TL;DR

- **This is a BTC-led repair phase, not an altcoin season.** BTC at `$80,393` is `-36%` from its all-time high but `+25.2%` over 90 days; ETH/BTC is `0.0301`, and BTC is `60.2%` of the top 60 by market cap.
- **The most systematic mismatch: the strongest cash flow sits with issuers and private companies, and token holders get the residual.** Three of the five strongest on fundamentals have no tradable token.
- **Three things do not add up.** DeFi missing the cut by `0.5` points is really `2.1`; the ONDO figures of `53.3%` unlocked and `2.05x` FDV to market cap are mutually exclusive; and the ranking table shows only `65` of `100` points.
- **The basis behind the strongest conclusion needs stating.** Stablecoins growing `2.5`–`2.8%` over 30 days is a Tether and Circle figure, not a whole-market one — and over the same window total stablecoin market cap was shrinking. The two largest issuers growing while the aggregate contracts means concentration is rising, which is not the same claim as money entering the narrative.
- **The most expensive asset is the one with the best fundamentals.** HYPE earns about `$792M` annualized in protocol revenue at `100x` FDV to revenue and `3.97x` FDV to market cap.
- **Attention already peaked ahead of price.** ZEC is `88%` off its attention peak, HYPE `79%`.

> This piece works from an external draft. I rechecked its 13 computable figures one by one and all are self-consistent (annualizations, FDV multiples, attention drawdowns, the implied BTC supply). The three findings in section 3 and the basis comparison in section 5 are mine; section 7 lists what cannot be computed.

Breadth is improving, but it improves through **small caps catching up**, not through narrative diffusion: the 11-05 gainers list is led by CAP `+31.1%`, DGAI `+29.4%` and FLUID `+25.9%`, all assets of `$100M`–`$400M`, while major alts remain `-80%` to `-95%` from their highs. Pressure comes from macro and on-chain supply: legislation has slowed, and MARA moved `1,650` BTC (`$132.8M`) and Trump Media `4,259` BTC (`$343.7M`) to trading venues or unknown wallets. The confirmation level technicians cite is `$82,600`.

## 3. Three things that do not add up

**First, DeFi did not miss the top five by `0.5` points — it missed by `2.1`.** Fifth place is prediction markets at `62.4`, DeFi is `60.3`. The difference matters, because the draft uses the near-miss to argue that DeFi is collateral damage from a liquidity squeeze, and a `2.1` point gap makes that much weaker. The real reason sits in its own components: flows at `10.0/25` is the bottom band of the whole table, level with Meme and DePIN.

**Second, the two ONDO supply figures are mutually exclusive.** The draft prints both `53.3%` unlocked and `2.05x` FDV to market cap, but `1 ÷ 2.05 = 48.8%`, not `53.3%`; conversely `53.3%` implies `1.88x`. Working back from the `$2.0B` market cap at `$0.4090`, the two branches differ materially:

| Taking which figure | Implied total supply | FDV/MC | Value of the 19.4pp unlock | Share of market cap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `53.3%` unlocked | `9.174B` tokens | `1.88x` | `$728M` | `36%` |
| `2.05x` FDV/MC | `10.024B` tokens | `2.05x` | `$795M` | `40%` |

The `$793M` and the `40%` the draft gives **only hold on the `2.05x` branch**, so either the `53.3%` is wrong or both conclusions revise down to `$728M` and `36%`. The unlock pressure is still real; its size depends on which figure gets corrected.

**Third, the ranking table shows only `65` of its `100` points.** Its three columns cap at fundamentals `25`, attention `20` and valuation `20`, leaving `35` points — tokenomics and risk among them — unlisted. Subtracting those three columns from each total leaves a hidden score between `11` and `27`, all inside the legal `0`–`35` range, which means **the table is unfalsifiable and the ranking cannot be reproduced from it**. Those `35` points do real work: PAXG gets `24` of them and Polymarket `15`, and that is exactly what lifts a token-less project to fourth.

One more thing that is not an error but worth knowing: in the narrative table, 6 of the 10 rows have components summing `0.1` above the stated total, a rounding artifact that does not change the ordering.

## 5. What basis the strongest conclusion rests on

The draft ranks stablecoins first on flows, fundamentals and cash flow together. The cash flow part is solid — the Tether and Circle daily revenue figures are real and the annualizations check out. **The flows part needs its basis stated.**

The TL;DR says major stablecoins total about `$280B`, up `2.5`–`2.8%` over 30 days. Its own data note already says it: the `2.5`–`2.8%` is **a Tether and Circle figure**, while the `$280B` is **a sum of major stablecoin market caps**. Those are not the same pool, and putting them in one sentence reads as the whole pool growing at that rate.

This is worth separating because there is a figure running the other way over the same window: [in another piece I put together the same day](/en/blog/ethereum-caught-the-assets-not-the-self-custody-en), total stablecoin market cap on the DefiLlama basis is `$293.8B`, **down** `$10.8B` over 30 days, with the contraction starting before the Binance incident.

**The two are not contradictory, and read together they mean something quite different**: the two largest issuers are minting while the whole market shrinks, which means **concentration is rising**, not that money is entering the narrative. For the stablecoin business that is good news; for the claim that the stablecoin narrative is attracting new capital it is evidence against.

The two figures come from different sources on different bases, and I did not divide one by the other to derive a net change for non-leading stablecoins — that would treat the difference between two methodologies as real flow. Only the direction is certain: **growing on the issuer basis, shrinking on the market basis.**

## 7. What this piece cannot tell you

- **The scoring is judgement, not measurement.** Both weightings (`20/25/25/15/15` for narratives, `20/25/20/20/15` for projects) were set by hand, and the component scores follow no reproducible rule. All I could check is whether the components sum to the total, not whether a component deserves its number.
- **The cross-sector ranking cannot be reproduced.** As above, the table exposes `65` points and leaves `35` both invisible and unfalsifiable.
- **Market caps and prices were not checked against circulating supply one by one.** I only did so where the draft gave two figures that could test each other, which is how the ONDO problem surfaced. For the record: UMA at a `$300M` market cap and `$0.3495` implies about `858M` tokens in circulation, a magnitude worth verifying, though I have no independent source for it.
- **The Variational second-place claim cannot be verified.** It has no token and no public disclosure, and the ranking comes from the draft with no independent source.
- **Attention is a single measure, weekly views on X.** The draft says Google Trends and Kaito data were not included and that confidence was lowered accordingly — worth carrying forward, so that attention down `88%` is not read as all attention down `88%`.
- **There are two BTC prices in the text.** The snapshot is `$80,393` and the sentiment passage uses `$80,676`; different moments, not interchangeable.
- **Protocol revenue is not holder revenue.** This is the easiest line in the whole piece to skip: the Tether `$5.98B` has nothing to do with USDT holders, and the Polymarket `$265M` has nothing to do with anyone, because there is no token.

---

**Conclusion.** This is a market where **cash flow sits with the issuers, narrative sits with the tokens, and liquidity sits at the top.**

Three of the five strongest on fundamentals cannot be bought; the sector with the most attention has no token; the strongest investable name on fundamentals is also the most expensive; the two cheapest both rest on rates; and the only asset with no narrative at all fell the least. These are not five separate observations but five faces of one thing — **in this cycle, narrative strength and token return have come apart.**

So the question worth asking is not which narrative is strongest, but **whether that narrative contains anything that lets you buy the cash flow**. Stablecoins are strongest, and the best exposure to them is equity like CRCL rather than any token; Perp DEX ranks third and is the only sector where a token maps directly onto the revenue.
