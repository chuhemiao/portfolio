## TL;DR

**The gap between narrative and revenue does weigh on JUP, but what mainly holds it down is the perp fee structure plus shrinking fees overall, not a missing perp product. Buybacks hold the floor, the narrative gap caps the ceiling, and in the near term JUP remains a high-beta SOL proxy.**

- **Of the four new products, only Vaults takes a real fee.** Baskets charges a `0%` basket fee, Portfolio V3 and Pulse are pure interface; Vaults opened its private beta today.
- **Perps are a JLP return product, not a JUP cash-flow product.** Perps are `46.2%` of gross fees, `75%` of which goes to JLP; the protocol keeps `$2.04M` a month and holders get only `$76K`. The aggregator is only `9.0%` of gross fees but `75.2%` of holder revenue.
- **Fees are down `84.6%`, protocol revenue only `66.6%`.** Monthly gross fees went from about `$115M` to `$17.68M`, protocol revenue from about `$15.33M` to `$5.12M` — the take rate rose from `13.3%` to `29.0%`. The draft merged these two into one sentence.
- **The buyback rate is `2.3%` to `3.5%`, not one number.** At "50% of protocol revenue" it is `$2.56M` a month, `3.5%` of market cap; at the holder revenue in the draft's own fee table it is `$1.65M` a month, `2.3%` — and the latter is exactly the Litterbox purchase for the month of May.
- **Vaults cannot take over from perps.** Matching perp protocol revenue takes `$490M` of TVL at a `5%` net take for Jupiter, and `$2.45B` at `1%`.
- **Cards are a distribution play, not a revenue line.** `$209M` is Collector Crypt volume; by the estimate in an earlier piece on this site, Collector Crypt's own true gross margin is only about `5.9%`.

> This piece is built on an external draft. Prices and fees are as of `2026-11-11`, with fees on a 30-day basis. Every computable figure was rechecked; the last row of the section 2 table, the split in section 3, the two buyback bases in section 4, the `1%` case in section 6 and the four findings in section 8 are mine.

## 1. Four new products: extending aggregation from trading to the balance sheet

The four are the same logic copied sideways, not a new revenue engine.

| Product | What it is | How it earns | Day-one size |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baskets | One deposit buys a basket of tokens, rebalanced by market cap or fixed weights | `0%` basket fee; routing fees only on rebalancing trades | About `$25.5K` deposited into the Crypto 6 basket |
| Vaults | Yield vaults run by outside curators (six from Hivemind, Bitwise and RE7 Labs) | Management fee up to `10%` a year plus performance fee up to `50%` of gains, shared between Jupiter and curators | Private beta on November 11 for `19K` waitlisted users and staked-JUP holders |
| Portfolio V3 | Holdings dashboard across `190`-plus protocols, with shortcuts into perps, prediction markets and borrowing | No direct fee; feeds other products | Live |
| Pulse plus rebuilt token pages | Research interface with tokenized stock and ETF quotes, tokenomics and a comparison sidebar | No direct fee | Rolling out this week |

Only Vaults takes a real fee, and it only opened its private beta today. [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/382836/solanas-jupiter-expands-beyond-trading-with-token-baskets-yield-vaults-and-new-portfolio) · [Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/jupiter-rolls-out-baskets-curated-vaults-and-portfolio-v3-in-push-beyond-trading)

The market treated it as a one-day event: on November 10 JUP ran from `0.2321` to `0.2558` (`+10.2%`), yet it is still down `28.6%` over 30 days. A narrative can light the fire; revenue has to keep it going.

## 2. Fee structure: 46% is gross fees, not value capture

| Business line | 30-day gross fees | 30-day protocol revenue | 30-day holder revenue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Perps | `$8.17M` | `$2.04M` | `$76K` |
| Lend | `$4.60M` | `$231K` | — |
| Staked SOL | `$2.32M` | `$170K` | — |
| Aggregator | `$1.59M` | `$1.24M` | `$1.24M` |
| Not listed (total minus rows) | `$1.00M` | `$1.439M` | `$334K` |
| **Total** | **`$17.68M`** | **`$5.12M`** | **`$1.65M`** |

The last row is not in the draft; it is the total minus the sum of the four rows, see section 8.

Three points:

1. **Perps are `46.2%` of gross fees and `39.8%` of protocol revenue, but only `4.6%` of holder revenue.** `75%` of perp fees go to JLP as real return and Jupiter keeps `25%`: `$8.17M × 25% = $2.0425M`, matching the table's `$2.04M`. [Eco](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/15083164-jupiter-perps-fees-leverage-how-jlp-works)
2. **The aggregator is only `9.0%` of gross fees but `75.2%` of holder revenue** (`1.24 / 1.65`). What actually funds buybacks is the least glamorous-looking business, routing.
3. **Of the `$2.04M` perps leave the protocol, only `$76K` (`3.7%`) reaches holders.**

So "missing perps" is not a valuation gap: perps are a JLP return product, not a JUP cash-flow product. What the four new products lack is not perps but a fee layer that improves retention.

## 3. Fee scale: fees down 85%, revenue down 67%

| | Q3 2025 (monthly average) | November 2026 (30 days) | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Gross fees | about `$115M` (`$1.38B` annualized ÷ 12) | `$17.68M` | `-84.6%` |
| Protocol revenue | about `$15.33M` (`$46M` for the quarter ÷ 3) | `$5.12M` | `-66.6%` |
| Take rate (revenue ÷ fees) | `13.3%` | `29.0%` | More than doubled |
| Aggregator spot monthly volume | about `$59B` | `$14.7B` (10/10–11/9) | `-75.1%` |

[Phemex/Coinotag](https://phemex.com/news/article/jupiter-reports-46m-q3-revenue-amid-jup-token-decline-29368) · [DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/protocol/jupiter)

The draft says "now `$5.12M` a month, about `$212M` annualized, down about 85% year over year" — `$5.12M` is protocol revenue, `$212M` is gross fees annualized (`$17.68M × 12`), and `85%` is the fall in fees. Split apart, one more fact shows up: **revenue fell less than fees, and the take rate more than doubled.** That may be the fee mix shifting toward lines where Jupiter keeps more, or partly a difference in basis between the two dates — the draft gives nothing that separates the two.

## 4. Valuation support: the buyback is real, but its rate has two numbers

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Price | `$0.2623` |
| Market cap / FDV | `$870M` / `$1.798B` |
| On annualized protocol revenue (`$5.12M × 12 = $61.44M`) | `14.2x` / `29.3x` |
| Unlocked | `3.46B` / `6.86B` JUP (`50.4%`) |
| Burned | `130M` JUP |

One cross-check that holds: market cap over price gives about `3.317B` circulating, and `3.46B` unlocked minus `130M` burned is `3.33B`, less than `20M` apart.

The buyback policy is `50%` of protocol revenue. [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/jupiter-to-buyback-jup-tokens-with-50-of-fees-starting-next-week/) But the draft actually carries two bases for the buyback amount:

| Basis | Monthly | Annualized | Of market cap | Of FDV |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Policy: `$5.12M` × `50%` | `$2.56M` | `$30.72M` | `3.5%` | `1.7%` |
| Holder revenue in the fee table | `$1.65M` | `$19.8M` | `2.3%` | `1.1%` |

Holder revenue is `32.2%` of protocol revenue, not `50%`. The draft cites Litterbox as support: `113.5M` JUP / `$24.45M` accumulated through May 18, 2026, with `8.39M` JUP, about `$1.65M`, added that month, and says this "roughly matches the estimate above" — **but what it matches is `$1.65M`, not `$2.56M`.** [Coinness](https://coinness.com/en/news/1157538) The draft does not explain the gap: the buyback base may not be all protocol revenue, or the data source may classify holder revenue more narrowly. This piece lists both and states conclusions as a range.

Supply discipline is real: `130M` JUP burned, net emissions cut to zero, the Jupuary airdrop postponed indefinitely. The remaining `3.4B` has been postponed, not removed. [BlockBeats](https://en.theblockbeats.news/flash/333126) · [TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/news/coinmarketcal:d8cfcb63f094b:0-jupiter-jup-253-47mm-token-unlock-date-28-february-2026/)

| | JUP | SOL |

| 30 days | `-28.6%` | `-26.5%` |

It is still `86.9%` below the February 2024 high of `$2.00`, and `93.2%` above the February 2026 low of `$0.1358`.

## 6. Vaults cannot take over

The fee caps are a management fee of up to `10%` a year plus a performance fee of up to `50%` of gains, shared between Jupiter and the curators. Perp protocol revenue is about `$24.48M` a year; for Vaults to match it:

| Jupiter net take (of AUM) | TVL required |
|---|---:|
| `5%` | about `$490M` |
| `2.5%` | about `$980M` |
| `1%` | about `$2.45B` |

`5%` is an aggressive assumption: it has Jupiter alone netting `5%` of AUM, already half the management-fee cap before the curators take their share. `1%` is closer to what yield vaults commonly charge, and then it takes `$2.45B`.

The draft estimates early beta TVL at `$50M` to `$150M` and Jupiter revenue at `$1M`–`$4M` a year — figures that imply a net take of `2.0%`–`2.7%`, not the `5%` used above, and come to `1.6%`–`6.5%` of current annualized protocol revenue. This is an inference from the product design, not disclosed data.

So the value of Vaults is not on the 2026 income statement but in three things: **staking JUP is the ticket into the beta** (which creates demand to hold it), keeping capital circulating inside Lend and the aggregator, and staking out the future curator economy. The risks are plain: no loss coverage, standard redemptions take up to 30 days, curators can pause deposits and withdrawals; on October 30 Lend already queued USDC withdrawals over tight liquidity. [Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/jupiter-rolls-out-baskets-curated-vaults-and-portfolio-v3-in-push-beyond-trading)

**The checkpoint is Solana Breakpoint on November 15–17**: a public launch schedule, each curator's actual fees, and the first TVL figures.

## 7. Cards: a distribution play, not a revenue line

The category is growing: onchain card gacha spending reached `$324M` in June 2026, a fourth straight monthly record; Collector Crypt alone did `$209M`, or `64.5%`; Solana carries `64%` of global onchain card volume. [Bankless](https://www.bankless.com/read/news/jupiter-partners-with-collector-crypt-on-new-pokemon-card-gacha)

The product is live: Jupiter Gacha entered beta on July 13 with Collector Crypt, every pull a PSA-graded, slabbed physical card, tokenized and tradable on Jupiter. [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/408059/onchain-pokemon-cards-come-to-solana-based-dex-jupiter) Offerbook accepts graded cards as collateral at fixed rates and fixed terms with no price liquidation; within hours of launch `$120K` of cards backed about `$75K` USDC in loans (`62.5%` loan-to-value), and loan fees go straight into JUP buybacks. [Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/jupiter-offerbook-enters-public-beta-with-fixed-rate-lending-against-tokens-nfts-and-tcg-cards)

The business logic has four parts: **acquisition** (Pokémon and One Piece bring in collectors who are not crypto-native), **frequency** (opening packs is high-frequency), **a closed loop** (opening, secondary trading through the aggregator, and collateral in Offerbook, three fees on one asset), and **differentiation** (rivals attack on trading; Jupiter defends with asset breadth). It is the same distribution playbook as bringing in tokenized stocks through Securitize.

Two layers of froth need taking off, though. The draft already removes the first: `$209M` is Collector Crypt volume, not Jupiter revenue. The second comes from an [October card study on this site](/en/blog/onchain-card-gacha-revenue-not-gmv-en): `93.91%` of card gacha volume flows back to users as buybacks, and Collector Crypt's own true gross margin is about `5.9%`. At that scale, `$209M` of volume corresponds to roughly `$12.33M` of gross profit — and that is Collector Crypt's, of which Jupiter takes a distribution cut.

## 8. Four places the draft does not reconcile

**1. The fee table total is not the sum of its rows.** The four rows of gross fees sum to `$16.68M` against a stated total of `$17.68M`, `$1.00M` apart; protocol revenue `$3.681M` against `$5.12M`, `$1.439M` apart; holder revenue `$1.316M` against `$1.65M`, `$334K` apart. In the gap, protocol revenue (`$1.439M`) exceeds gross fees (`$1.00M`) — a business line cannot earn more revenue than fees, so the total and the rows are not on the same basis. The draft's shares (`46.2%`, `39.8%`, `4.6%`, `9.0%`, `75.2%`) are all divided by the total; the arithmetic is right, but part of the denominator is not listed. Section 2 here shows the gap as its own row.

**2. The buyback has two numbers.** `$2.56M` is the policy basis; `$1.65M` is the holder revenue in the fee table and also the Litterbox pace in May. The draft says Litterbox "roughly matches the estimate above", but what it matches is `$1.65M`. So "buybacks at about `3.5%` of market cap a year" in its conclusion should read `2.3%`–`3.5%`.

**3. Revenue and fees share one sentence.** "`$5.12M` a month, about `$212M` annualized" — the first is protocol revenue, the second gross fees annualized; "down about 85% year over year" is the fall in fees, while revenue fell `66.6%`.

**4. The two Vaults estimates use different take rates.** Matching perps uses `5%`; the early beta revenue of `$1M`–`$4M` implies `2.0%`–`2.7%`.

## 9. What this piece cannot compute

- **Which products make up the fee table gap.** DCA, limit orders, Launchpad, Offerbook and Gacha could all be in it; the draft does not break it out.
- **The real buyback base.** The draft gives no formula for which revenue the `50%` applies to.
- **Actual Vaults fees and the Jupiter share.** Those wait for Breakpoint.
- **Whether Q3 2025 and now share a data source and basis.** Part of the doubled take rate may come from a basis difference.
- **The Litterbox pace after May.** The draft's data ends on May 18.

---

**Conclusion.** The valuation floor has support; the ceiling is capped for now. Buybacks at `2.3%`–`3.5%` of market cap a year, zero net emissions and a postponed Jupuary make a floor that does not fall far; but monthly gross fees `84.6%` below Q3 2025, perp revenue that depends heavily on SOL volatility, and four new products that add no near-term revenue mean the multiple cannot expand. JUP is priced more like "SOL beta plus a `2.3%`–`3.5%` buyback" than the next Hyperliquid.

Until Vaults launches publicly and reaches more than `$300M` of TVL (which even at a `5%` net take equals only `24.4%` of current annualized protocol revenue), or monthly gross fees return to `$20M`, the gap between narrative and revenue will keep holding back multiple expansion. Four things to watch: monthly gross fees, perp protocol revenue, Vaults TVL and fee disclosures, and the weekly Litterbox purchase pace.
