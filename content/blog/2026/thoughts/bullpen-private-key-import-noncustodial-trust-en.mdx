## TL;DR

Bullpen is more likely to restrict wallet imports in stages. It has permanently shut down the affected 2024 beta system. It could bring supported imports under Turnkey signing policies, strengthen passkey or two-factor authentication (2FA) by default, and use withdrawal allowlists, limits, and delays for large transfers. The incident tests what a login can authorize and whether users will still leave money in the terminal. The appeal of points and a potential airdrop depends on the same trust.

## What we know about the incident

Bullpen initially reported **18–19 affected accounts** on November 24. The accounts were opened in its early beta from **April to June 2024**. On November 25 it confirmed **19**, including co-founder Ansem and 18 other testers. The company said the preliminary group represented less than **0.005%** of registered accounts and that **99.8%** of the funds taken came from one account. If the percentage also applies to the final count of 19, it implies more than **380,000** registered accounts. The preliminary low count of 18 implies a lower bound of **360,000**. Both are lower bounds inferred from the preliminary percentage. Some early reports put losses around **$2 million**. Bitquery traced around **$2.57 million in USDC** along the path from Ansem's wallet. [Bullpen initial report](https://x.com/BullpenFi/status/2125267130870272331) · [99.8% update](https://x.com/BullpenFi/status/2125314571158073654) [Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/ansem-solana-wallet-drain)

The import date cited in the draft puts about **29 months** between **June 2024 and November 2026**. Bullpen said the point of entry was a legacy system tied to the early accounts. The public material leaves open how the key was stored or migrated and how unauthorized requests were allowed to sign. Other coverage relayed Ansem's claim that 2FA was bypassed. That claim concerns account authentication. The available evidence leaves the state of Turnkey's signing environment and cryptographic components unresolved. [Bullpen November 25 update](https://x.com/BullpenFi/status/2125585437687181318) [Coverage of the 2FA claim](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/bullpen-2fa-vulnerability-leads-to-19-account-breaches-49-ansem-supply-remains-secure)

Bankless reported sales of roughly **87.5 million ANSEM** in several hours, about **8.8% of total supply**. The draft's market snapshot showed a decline of roughly **41% over 24 hours**. Reported returns vary with the window. Bitquery traced funds from Solana to Arbitrum via Relay and found that about **$2.57 million in USDC** was later converted to **42.24 BTC**, worth around **$2.50 million** in the draft. Those BTC had remained in place at **14:40 UTC on November 25**. Bullpen said it was working with the FBI and other parties and had commissioned an independent security review. The public updates reviewed through **November 27** gave no compensation plan. Some observers have disputed who signed the transactions. The company reported unauthorized access to 19 accounts. ## How a login affects signing

Bullpen markets itself as non-custodial and promotes SVM and EVM wallet imports on its home page. Its documentation says sign-up creates a Turnkey embedded wallet, lets users export the private key, and supports importing a private key or a **12- or 24-word recovery phrase**. The current product documents describe the public offering. The storage and authorization settings of the early beta have yet to be disclosed. [Bullpen home page](https://bullpen.fi/) [Account creation](https://docs.bullpen.fi/getting-started/creating-an-account) · [Wallet management](https://docs.bullpen.fi/getting-started/wallet-management)

Importing the key leaves the original wallet able to sign. The imported wallet's signing depends on account authentication and wallet policy. During an account takeover, the question is whether login access can authorize transfers. Bullpen's terms describe a non-custodial service and assign account activity to the user. Actual control and liability depend on the wallet policies, the terms, and the findings. Stronger passkey, device binding, allowlists, delays, or hardware approval can change signing permissions. Bullpen holding keys or having the ability to freeze and recover funds would bring different operational, capital, and potential regulatory obligations. I see that outcome as less likely. [Bullpen Terms of Use](https://bullpen.fi/terms) [Turnkey policy documentation](https://docs.turnkey.com/features/policies/overview)

The CLI documentation already spells out limits. An imported EVM wallet is visible in the wallet list but unavailable for Bullpen-managed transfers, ownership-proven Polymarket recovery, managed trading, or selection as the primary EVM wallet. The documentation still covers Solana imports. That supports limits by chain and feature and leaves some Solana permissions across clients unconfirmed. [Bullpen CLI](https://cli.bullpen.fi/reference/commands/wallet/import/)

## Will private key import go away?

As of **November 27, 2026**, Bullpen had announced that the affected legacy system was permanently shut and said other accounts were unaffected. It had yet to announce a general end to imports. Its investor page lists non-custodial design as one of four investment principles. Keeping that position fits the business as it stands. [Bullpen November 25 update](https://x.com/BullpenFi/status/2125585437687181318) [Bullpen investors](https://docs.bullpen.fi/about-bullpen/investors)

Imports help acquire existing traders. Active traders and points participants can keep their addresses and save the work of creating an account and moving assets. Other products also bring external wallets into embedded systems. pump.fun plans to end web sign-in with external wallets on **November 30**. The path for keeping an old address requires an import into Privy. Mark Karpelès criticized the end-to-end encryption description because the imported key is decrypted inside Privy's trusted execution environment (TEE). Removing the import option still leaves embedded accounts exposed to takeover, so authentication and signing policy would still need attention. [Bullpen home page](https://bullpen.fi/) [pump.fun migration notice](https://pump.fun/docs/wallet-login-changes) [Privy import architecture](https://docs.privy.io/wallets/wallets/import-a-wallet/architecture) [Coverage of Karpelès's criticism](https://tech.yahoo.com/cybersecurity/articles/pump-fun-faces-pushback-asking-213700831.html)

| Scenario | Actions | Likelihood | Effect on the non-custodial business |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Tiered restrictions, base case** | Shut the 2024 beta channel, apply Turnkey policies to supported imports, strengthen default passkey or 2FA, and add withdrawal allowlists, limits, delays for large transfers, or hardware approval | Most likely | Keeps the non-custodial positioning with a near-term trust discount. The longer-term effect depends on execution |
| **End imports across the product** | Keep Turnkey embedded wallets, make external wallets read-only, and remove private key and phrase imports | Medium to low | Loses the ability to onboard users with their old addresses. Some active traders could turn to Axiom, Photon, Trojan, or GMGN |
| **Shift to custody or platform recovery** | Hold keys or gain freeze authority and offer recovery or insurance | Low | Changes the brand and adds capital, compliance, and potential licensing requirements |

## What changes in the competitive position?

DefiLlama's snapshot through November 27 showed **$15,279 in fees over 30 days**, or about **$186,000 a year** using **365/30**. October 2026 fees were **$23,982**, down roughly **85%** from **$161,631** in October 2025. Fees in the fourth quarter of 2026 were about **$36,551 through November 26**. The full fourth quarter of 2025 was about **$314,616**. Perpetual futures notional volume was **$33.66 million over 30 days** and **$2.487 billion lifetime**. The October comparison shows activity was slowing before the incident. The partial and full quarters cover different windows. The incident could deepen the slowdown. Later readings will show whether it did. [DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/protocol/bullpen)

A trading terminal competes on execution, asset discovery, distribution, switching costs, and expected rewards. Bullpen has Ansem's audience and brings Hyperliquid, Polymarket, and Solana into one interface. Third-party points coverage describes a **100 million point** program, with about **70 million** unallocated, in **Season 3**, and refers to **$BULL** as the expected ticker. Sources checked by the snapshot date did not establish a token generation event (TGE). Bullpen's investor page lists **6th Man Ventures** as lead and Delphi, Manifold, Karatage, Temporal, and Sparta as participants. The page does not state the amount or valuation. [MEXC](https://blog.mexc.com/token-reviews/bullpen-airdrop-2026-how-to-farm-bull-points-on-the-multi-chain-terminal-co-founded-by-ansem/) · [Airdrops.io](https://airdrops.io/bullpen/) [Bullpen investors](https://docs.bullpen.fi/about-bullpen/investors) [Bullpen market access](https://docs.bullpen.fi/trading)

Keeping funds in the terminal lets users trade quickly and accumulate points. Ansem publicly said he could not confirm funds were safe and advised users to withdraw first. If users leave less money in the terminal, trading activity and participation in points could fall. **The risk discount is likely to show up first in retained deposits and expected rewards.** Competitors with a route that needs no private key import could gain trust. A blanket ban on imports could also cost Bullpen traders who prefer to use their existing wallets. ## Conclusion

Bullpen first needs to clarify how authorization flows from login to signing. My base case remains tighter imports by chain, feature, and limit, with stronger authentication and withdrawal approval by default. As it keeps the non-custodial positioning, Bullpen can publish what each credential is allowed to do so users can assess the risk.

Over the next **two to four weeks**, I would look for three things. Will Bullpen publish the root cause and a compensation plan? Will withdrawals or trading be restricted for imported wallets? Will deposits and trading volume recover within **two weeks**? The first two indicate progress on authorization. The last shows whether traders are willing to keep funds in the terminal.
