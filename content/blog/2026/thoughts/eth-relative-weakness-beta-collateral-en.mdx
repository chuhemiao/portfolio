## TL;DR

The selloff looks like deleveraging in a high-beta asset. The available evidence falls short of establishing a collateral downgrade. Binance liquidated about $213 million in ETH contract longs in the hour starting at 21:00 UTC on Sunday. By 08:20 UTC on Monday, ETH/BTC had fallen from 0.0294 to 0.0284. ETH-related supply on Aave V3 still stood at **4.85 million ETH equivalent**, roughly flat from a month earlier. Its share of dollar-denominated supply fell from 52.8% to 48.7%, largely reflecting ETH’s roughly 17% price decline over the same period. The pressure on demand was clearer. Staking recorded a net outflow of **844,000 ETH** in October, and spot ETH ETFs recorded about **$1.14 billion** of net outflows from September 1 through November 6. ETH remains an important form of collateral in onchain lending. These figures tell us more about marginal buyers’ appetite for risk.

## How the liquidations were distributed

In the hour starting at 21:00 UTC on November 8, 2026, Binance liquidated **$212.6 million** in ETH contract longs and $1.4 million in shorts. ETH dropped from $2,225 to $2,122 (**−4.6%**), while BTC fell from $75,687 to $73,982 (**−2.3%**). The ratio of the two hourly declines was about 2.0, a measure of the relative size of this particular move. ETH/BTC traded around 0.029 near 22:00 UTC on Sunday. By 08:20 UTC on Monday, ETH was at **$2,100**, BTC at **$74,034**, and ETH/BTC at **0.0284**.

In the original 24-hour snapshot, ETH liquidations totaled **$494 million** (92% longs), BTC **$302 million** (92% longs), and SOL **$42 million**. The three accounted for **$838 million** together, a narrower set than the roughly $1 billion marketwide estimate. ETH’s market cap was 17% of BTC’s, yet ETH accounted for **59%** of liquidations across the three assets. ETH open interest was $26.1 billion, or **10.1%** of market cap. BTC’s was $51.3 billion, or **3.4%**. On this rough open-interest-to-market-cap measure, ETH’s leverage density was about three times BTC’s.

In the same selloff, ETH fell further, derivatives positions were more crowded, and liquidations were concentrated in longs. The pattern fits a high-beta asset undergoing deleveraging.

## Onchain lending: ETH-equivalent supply held steady

Deteriorating collateral standing would warrant checking for a sustained drop in supply, weaker borrowing demand, and tighter governance parameters. Start with these Aave V3 Ethereum market snapshots.

Supply in ETH terms fell from 4.99 million on September 7 to 4.79 million on October 7, then recovered to 4.85 million on November 7. The original figures put the two-month net decline at about **2.9%**, a small change. WETH borrowing recovered from 1.77 million to **1.82 million ETH**, and utilization stood at **85.1%**. WETH remained one of the most borrowed assets in the pool. Between the October and November snapshots, ETH fell from $2,697 to $2,236 (**−17.1%**), while the dollar value of ETH-related supply declined 16.0%. Stablecoins and BTC in the denominator held up better over the same interval, so the ETH-related share of dollar-denominated supply declined from 52.8% to 48.7%.

Aave’s daily liquidations totaled **$48.5 million** on November 4, fell to $250,000 on November 5, and were close to zero on November 6–7. The lending dataset ends on November 7, leaving onchain liquidations after 21:00 UTC on Sunday unverified. The earlier balances and liquidation data show no sign of a run on ETH collateral, and the onchain impact of Sunday’s shock remains open.

## Staking and ETFs: pressure on marginal demand

October deposits reached **739,800 ETH**, 56% of August’s 1,315,400 ETH. Withdrawals reached **1,583,400 ETH**, roughly three times August’s 506,200 ETH. The net outflow was **843,600 ETH**, worth about $1.77 billion. The outflows spanned several providers. The original breakdown lists Lido at −174,700 ETH, Coinbase −153,000, Rocket Pool −96,300, Consensys −67,500, StakeWise −46,200, Renzo −40,100, and unattributed entities −234,400. November 1–8 recorded a further net outflow of **147,800 ETH**.

The net outflows are consistent with weaker demand for staking. Withdrawals can also be reward payouts, so flows alone cannot tell us how many stakers exited. If staking exits lead to further redemptions, the supply of ETH-linked tokens such as stETH and weETH could contract, reducing collateral available to DeFi. That process takes time and has yet to show up in Aave’s ETH-equivalent supply. [Ethereum’s staking withdrawal guide](https://ethereum.org/staking/withdrawals/)

From September 1 through November 6, spot ETH ETFs saw **$1.144 billion** in net outflows, while spot BTC ETFs drew **$850 million** in net inflows. From the start of the year through November 6, both saw net redemptions, with **$477 million** flowing out of ETH funds and **$820 million** out of BTC funds. The recent window shows a relative preference for BTC, consistent with BTC taking priority as risk budgets shrink. The year-to-date figures show net outflows for both.

## Relative value: years of decline, still within this year’s range

ETH/BTC fell from a 2021 high of **0.0878** to 0.0722 at year-end 2022, 0.0543 at year-end 2023, 0.0362 at year-end 2024, 0.0336 at year-end 2025, and **0.0284** in this snapshot. ETH lost about **68%** against BTC over five years.

The 2026 range was **0.0258** on June 7 to **0.0352**. The latest 0.0284 observation sits in the lower half, above the year’s low. Over the past 12 months ETH fell 41%, BTC fell 29%, and ETH/BTC fell about 17%. The original analysis reports an ETH daily-return beta to BTC of **1.25**, a downside beta of **1.21**, and annualized volatility of **60% for ETH and 44% for BTC**. ETH was already the more volatile asset before the weekend.

The deleveraging took place within a multiyear relative decline and could amplify that trend. Assessing ETH’s collateral standing requires onchain supply, actual use as collateral, and risk parameters.

## Conclusion and what to watch

High beta describes how ETH’s price moves relative to BTC. Collateral standing depends on how ETH can be used in lending protocols, including liquidity depth, oracle coverage, reliable liquidations, and risk parameters. A falling price directly reduces collateral value. Eligibility and any tightening of risk parameters depend on protocol rules and governance.

Ethereum’s native staking accepts only ETH. Lower prices or weaker demand could shrink the staking economy, while ETH’s protocol role follows the staking rules. Lending markets offer alternatives such as stablecoins and cbBTC. Changes in price and risk appetite can gradually erode ETH’s share.

1. **Relative value.** Watch whether ETH/BTC breaks below **0.0258**, the June low. A break would reinforce the multiyear relative decline. Collateral standing still requires onchain evidence.
2. **Collateral supply.** Use Aave’s **4.85 million ETH equivalent** as a baseline. A drop below **4.50 million ETH** would warrant checking for sustained withdrawals. Also watch whether the stETH/ETH discount stays above 1%.
3. **Staking demand.** Net outflows were 844,000 ETH in October and 148,000 ETH in the first eight days of November. Three consecutive months with net outflows of at least 300,000 ETH each would make weakening demand look more persistent. The partial November window is not directly comparable with a full month.

The roughly $1 billion in weekend marketwide liquidations fits the high-beta deleveraging interpretation. Aave’s ETH-equivalent supply, roughly 85% WETH utilization, and onchain liquidation data through November 7 do not yet support a downgrade in ETH’s collateral standing. For staking, the question is whether demand keeps weakening.
