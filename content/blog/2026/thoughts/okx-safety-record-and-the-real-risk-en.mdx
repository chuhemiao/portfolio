## TL;DR

- **The claim that OKX has never been hacked largely holds, with a caveat.** As of `2026-11-05` the public record holds no theft from its own exchange wallets that cost customers their assets.
- **Yet not one of the events that actually cost its users money was the work of an attacker.** A futures blowout in `2018` triggering a `17%` socialized loss, roughly five weeks of frozen withdrawals in `2020`, an OKB flash crash of about `50%` in `2024`, and a `505` million dollar DOJ forfeiture in `2025` over anti-money-laundering failures.
- **No public incident means not disproven, not audited.**
- **One line its own numbers support and nobody draws**: `11.9%` of derivatives volume, second place, against only `5.9%` of average daily open interest, fifth place — about `2` times the market-average volume per dollar of open interest.
- **That line also explains a third number**: its liquidation share of `4.9%` sits against its open interest share rather than its volume share, because liquidations follow positions, not turnover.
- **The real risk was never a breach. It is the compliance history and what its products get used for.**

> This piece works from an external draft. Everything in it that could be checked — the share ratio, the split of the forfeiture, the annualized funding rate, the liquidation share, the drawdown from the high — is internally consistent. Two points in sections 3 and 4 are mine, derived from the draft numbers but absent from it; section 6 lists what I could not verify.

## 1. Define the theft first

Separating three kinds of event is what makes the question answerable.

| Date | Event | Nature | Outcome for customers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2018-07-30 | A BTC futures position of roughly `$416M` notional was force-liquidated; OKX put `2,500` BTC (about `$18M`) into the insurance fund and took a `17%` socialized loss from profitable traders | risk blowout, not theft | users absorbed `17%` |
| from 2020-10-16, ~5 weeks | The founder, holding a private key, was taken in to assist an investigation and withdrawals were suspended | key-person risk, not a breach | funds intact, later restored |
| 2024-01-23 | OKB fell about `50%` within minutes, setting off a liquidation cascade; OKX promised compensation | market structure, not theft | some users compensated |
| 2025-02-24 | Settled with the US DOJ for `$505M` (`$84M` penalty plus `$421M` forfeiture), admitting 2018–2024 anti-money-laundering violations across some `$5B` in suspicious flows | compliance failure, not theft | no direct asset loss |
| 2025-03 | Its Web3 DEX aggregator was used by Lazarus to launder funds stolen from Bybit (about `$100M` by the Bybit account); OKX suspended it and reopened in May | product misuse, not an OKX breach | no OKX customer loss |
| 2025-11-15 | A user alleged a backdoor in OKX Wallet and a loss of `50` ETH; OKX offered `10` BTC for evidence and open-sourced its key module, and an April 2026 third-party audit found no leakage | unproven allegation | no systemic loss |
| 2026-03-15 | A third-party attack was linked to the OKX Web3 wallet; OKX denied any wallet vulnerability | third-party attack, disputed | not a wallet flaw |

Three conclusions.

**First, there really is no record of theft from its custodial wallets.** No Mt. Gox-style insolvency, and nothing like [the five events across eight years on the Binance side](/en/blog/binance-hack-history-and-96-hour-timeline-en). OKX describes its own record as free of any major breach through 2025 ([OKX](https://www.okx.com/en-us/learn/is-okx-safe)).

**Second, that is not disproven rather than audited.** An exchange absorbing a loss internally, with the addresses never flagged on-chain, cannot be ruled out from outside. Reading cannot find it as there is none is the most common error in this kind of judgement.

**Third, none of the events that cost users money came from a breached perimeter.** `2020` came closest to a run: the key holder was taken in, withdrawals froze for five weeks, and users could only watch a balance they could not move ([Nikkei/Caixin](https://asia.nikkei.com/spotlight/caixin/china-s-okex-suspends-cryptocurrency-withdrawals), [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2020/10/16/okex-founder-star-xu-held-in-police-custody-report)). Nothing was ultimately lost, but what it exposed was key-person dependence, not the strength of cold storage. The `2024` OKB crash was a different class: the platform token was broken by a liquidation spiral on its own order book ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2024/01/23/crypto-exchange-okxs-token-suffers-50-flash-crash-amid-liquidation-cascade)).

The contrast that makes this clearest is [the Binance hot wallet failure this year](/en/blog/binance-suspected-hack-outflows-custody-risk-en): one valid signature, eight minutes, `$5.643B` out. That was a mechanism problem. The OKX events were risk, governance and liquidity problems. Both cost users money, and what you watch for is completely different.

## 2. From OKCoin to $25B

Its predecessor OKCoin was founded in `2013`, went offshore as OKEx in `2017`, and was renamed OKX in `2021`. It is now headquartered in San Jose under OK Group, with around `5,000` staff and more than `120` million registered users ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OKX)). The business stopped being only a CEX some time ago: OKX Wallet supports over 100 chains (more than `5M` monthly actives in Q2 2025), and OKX Ventures runs about `$100M` across 200-plus investments.

`2026` was the year its capital structure changed most.

| Date | Deal | Valuation and terms |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-03-05 | ICE, the NYSE parent, took a strategic stake | `$25B` valuation; reported at about `$200M` for under `1%`; a board seat; ICE licenses OKX spot prices for US regulated futures, OKX offers NYSE tokenized equities to users, and the two set up a US joint venture ([ICE](https://ir.theice.com/press/news-details/2026/ICE-Makes-Investment-in-OKX-Establishing-Strategic-Relationship/default.aspx), [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/crypto-exchange-okx-valuation-hits-25-billion-after-nyse-parent-invests-2026-03-05/), [Architect Partners](https://architectpartners.com/okx-receives-200m-investment-from-intercontinental-exchange-at-25b-valuation/)) |
| 2026-10-06 | Circle, Ripple, SC Ventures (Standard Chartered) and Qube/QRT added to it | the same `$25B` valuation, alongside the launch of the stablecoin app OKX Money ([Coinpedia](https://coinpedia.org/news/ripple-backs-okx-at-25b-valuation-in-new-funding-round-bloomberg-reports/)) |

That line about offering NYSE tokenized equities is worth noting on its own, because it makes OKX a distribution channel for tokenized US stocks — and [four completely different legal structures sit behind those products](/en/blog/tokenized-equity-four-wrappers-and-what-to-watch-en), so which one an exchange hands you is a separate question to ask.

Third parties put its `2024` revenue at about `$1.5B`, but the company publishes no audited accounts, and an IPO has been raised repeatedly since `2025` with no public filing. **It remains a private, founder-controlled company running a global business through offshore entities** — which coexists with the US headquarters and the MiCA licence, and is the central tension in reading OKX.

## 3. Second place, and a line nobody draws

Its strength is derivatives, not spot.

| Metric | OKX | Rank | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total volume share | `14.41%` | 2nd (Binance `34.51%`) | 2026 Q3, TokenInsight |
| Spot share | `7.19%` | 2nd (Binance `33.63%`, Bybit `6.70%`) | 2026 Q3 |
| Derivatives as share of own volume | `92%` | industry average `83%`, among the highest | 2026 Q3 |
| Derivatives volume | `$4.19T` | **2nd**, `11.9%` share | 2026 H1, CoinGlass |
| Average daily open interest | `$6.7B` | **5th**, `5.9%` share | 2026 H1 |
| Average user assets | `$19.56B` | 2nd, `8.4%` share | 2026 H1 |
| BTC order book depth at ±1% | `$112M` | 2nd, `20.8%` share (Binance `$236M`) | 2026 H1 |

Sources: [TokenInsight Q3 2026](https://x.com/TokenInsight/status/2117180111791567101), [CoinGlass H1 2026](https://www.coinglass.com/learn/2026h1-market-report-en), [CoinGlass Q1 2026](https://www.coinglass.com/learn/2026-q1-mktshare-report-en).

**The draft lists volume and open interest on two separate rows and never puts them together.** Together they are the most informative thing in the table: `11.9%` of derivatives volume for second place against `5.9%` of daily open interest for fifth, a ratio of `2.02`. In other words, **each dollar of open interest carries about twice the volume it does market-wide**.

That changes how to read the `92%`. It is usually read as institutional depth, but the ratio says a good part of it is **turnover** rather than positions sitting still — the same risk is traded over more times here than elsewhere.

**The same line explains a third number.** As of `2026-11-05` around 03:40 UTC, BTC/USDT perpetual open interest on OKX is `$2.69B`, funding is `0.01%/8h` (about `10.95%` annualized), and BTC spot is around `$80,755`; over the prior 24 hours OKX accounted for `$15.88M` of BTC liquidations against roughly `$327M` market-wide, a `4.9%` share, fifth place. Note that this `4.9%` **sits against its `5.9%` open interest share, not its `11.9%` volume share** — liquidations follow positions, not turnover. The two measures corroborate each other here.

On competition, what is taking share is not another CEX: [the route where Hyperliquid turns regulated distribution into a moat](/en/blog/hyperliquid-hype-regulated-distribution-as-moat-en) and the route where OKX runs global derivatives through offshore entities are competing for the same leveraged flow.

## 4. Reserves, assets and OKB

OKX was among the first exchanges to publish monthly proof of reserve after FTX. Its `49th` report, dated `2026-11-02`, puts BTC, ETH, USDT and USDC at about `$27.8B` combined with over 20 major assets all at `≥100%`, and says it has published for four consecutive years ([PANews](https://www.panewslab.com/en/articles/01a19c5c-788f-7283-a31e-5b6da68d78d1)). The earlier `41st` report gave the ratios: BTC `106%`, ETH `107%`, USDT `109%`, USDC `102%`, `$25.66B` combined.

**But that `$27.8B` and the `$19.56B` of average user assets from the previous section are not the same thing, differ by `$8.24B`, and do not corroborate each other.** The first is liability coverage for four assets at one moment; the second is a half-year daily average across everything. Nor is it an entity-level balance sheet audit. The `42nd` report showed about `120,000` BTC (`-11.93%` on the period), `1.607M` ETH (`-8.19%`) and `$10.21B` USDT (`+0.73%`) — reserve composition migrates with the market, and a single report cannot be extrapolated into money is leaving.

The platform token is the other thread.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Price | `$112.41` (2026-11-05) |
| 24h / 7d / 30d | `+5.44%` / `+2.16%` / `-14.81%` |
| Market cap / FDV | `$2.36B` / `$2.36B` |
| Total / circulating supply | `21M` / `21M` |
| All-time high | `$228.74` (2025-10-05), now `-50.9%` |
| Supply reset | a one-off burn of `65.25M` in 2025-08 fixed total supply at `21M` ([TheBlockBeats](https://en.theblockbeats.news/flash/307227)) |

OKB is worth `9.4%` of that `$25B` valuation, and it **is not equity**: no claim on exchange cash flow, no dividend, no claim on OKX. It behaves more like a high-beta option on the exchange narrative — it rose over `50%` intraday when ICE invested, which is that property showing itself. The August `2025` supply reset changed its scarcity story, and also left the price easier to drive on expectations about buybacks, burns and an IPO than on fundamentals.

## 5. The actual soft spot

Its compliance history is far more crooked than its security history. In February `2025` the operating entity Aux Cayes FinTech pleaded guilty in the US, admitting anti-money-laundering violations and unlicensed money transmission from 2018 to 2024, and paid `$505M`; the settlement requires an external compliance consultant through **February `2027`** and remediation of KYC, sanctions screening and geo-blocking ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/okx-settle-us-doj-anti-money-laundering-probe)). It then re-entered the US market in April `2025` and moved its headquarters to San Jose ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/okx-enters-the-united-states-market-following-505-m-settlement)).

The licence map is more complete than three years ago: MiCA pre-authorization via Malta in January `2025` covering the EEA, a Dubai VARA licence in October `2024`, and local entities in Singapore, Australia and the US. Hong Kong is the counterexample — in May `2024` it withdrew its own VASP application and stopped offering centralized trading to Hong Kong residents before May 31 ([OKX](https://www.okx.com/help/withdrawal-of-okx-hks-vasp-license-application)). **Its global compliance is selective coverage, not seamless passage.**

The March `2025` Lazarus episode exposed a different class of risk: its Web3 DEX aggregator was used to launder funds stolen from Bybit, EU regulators looked at its MiCA compliance, and OKX suspended the aggregator, upgraded monitoring and relaunched only in May ([The Record](https://therecord.media/crypto-okx-shuts-down-exchange)). **This was not a breach of OKX; it was its product being used as laundering infrastructure** — and for an exchange seeking US licences and institutional custody, that spillover is no less damaging than a theft.

| Risk | Level | What to watch |
|---|---|---|
| AML remediation unfinished | high | the assessment after the consultant term ends `2027-02`; US state licences and the SEC position |
| Web3 product boundary | high | whether the wallet and aggregator are used by sanctioned addresses again; cross-border enforcement |
| Reserve transparency gap | medium | whether PoR coverage widens; whether an entity-level audit appears |
| Concentration in derivatives | medium-high | blowout and compensation behavior under stress; that `92%` mix |
| Governance and key persons | medium | private, founder-controlled; whether the `2020` key-holder event repeats |
| Competitive pressure | medium | Binance derivatives at roughly `2.2`–`2.4` times; Hyperliquid taking share |
| OKB against equity | medium | the token has no claim on the cash flow behind a `$25B` valuation, so it falls harder when sentiment turns |

One aside: the semantics of the OKX API are their own subject — details like its `clOrdId` only having to be unique among live orders [become a real risk-control problem once order placement is handed to an automated system](/en/blog/llm-not-final-risk-gate-trading-agents-en).

## 6. What this piece cannot tell you

- **No public record of theft cannot be upgraded into no theft.** That is a conclusion about external visibility, not an audit, and not something OKX has evidenced.
- **Proof of reserve cannot establish solvency.** It is a point-in-time snapshot of liability coverage for some assets, with no entity-level audit and no coverage of all customer assets; the `$27.8B` and the `$19.56B` do not corroborate each other.
- **The share figures cross sources and periods and cannot be mixed.** Total and spot share come from TokenInsight for 2026 Q3; derivatives, open interest, user assets and depth come from CoinGlass for 2026 H1. My `11.9%` ÷ `5.9%` ratio is internally consistent within H1, but it cannot be divided into a Q3 figure.
- **I did not independently verify the premise of the OKB supply reset.** Burning `65.25M` and fixing supply at `21M` implies a pre-reset total of `86.25M` — that is reverse-engineered from the two figures given, and I found no primary record of the pre-reset total.
- **The `$1.5B` revenue is a third-party estimate.** With no audited accounts it cannot be used as a known quantity, and no multiple against the `$25B` valuation follows from it.
- **I did not read primary documents event by event.** Every characterization above rests on public reporting, and the parties disagree about the March 2026 episode, which I have recorded as disputed.

---

**Conclusion.** The never hacked record is credible and over-marketed at once. What supports it is cold storage, monthly proof of reserve, the compensation mechanism after the `2024` crash, and the compliance build-out since `2025`. But every real loss its users have taken came from risk control, governance or compliance, not from an attacker.

**So the question to ask is not whether it has been breached.** What to watch is the assessment after the DOJ consultant term ends in February `2027`, progress on US licences, how far proof of reserve extends, and how liquidation and compensation behave in the next violent move — because a venue ranked second in derivatives volume and fifth in open interest is one where risk turns over faster than elsewhere.
