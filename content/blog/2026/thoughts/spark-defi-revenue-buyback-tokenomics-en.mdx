## TL;DR

**The protocol fundamentals read positive. That is not the same as saying $SPK should go up — an entire supply structure sits between the two.**

- **The revenue is real**: projected annual net protocol returns of `$18.45M`, operating expenses of `$10.90M`, protocol surplus of `$7.55M`.
- **The buyback is real, and it is not a burn.** Roughly `200.46M SPK` repurchased — about 5.9% of circulating supply — for `$4.08M USDS`. On-chain the tokens move from CoW Swap into a protocol-controlled wallet and on to an operations address, and **the buyback Safe still holds 12.77M SPK**. Nothing has been burned.
- **Supply is the real tension**: 10B total, 33.96% circulating, yet 49.24% already unlocked — **roughly 1.53B tokens are unlocked but not counted as circulating**, outside the market cap and free to move at any time.
- **Revenue is concentrated**: Distribution Rewards 59.21% plus SLL 33.48% is **92.7%**. And SLL net returns in Q2 2026 were **−$0.81M**, carried by the distribution business.
- **Easy to miss**: the average buyback price of `$0.02035` is **10.7% above** the current `$0.01838`. A buyback does not guarantee the treasury makes money.

## 1. It is no longer a lending protocol

Spark now positions itself as on-chain capital allocation and liquidity infrastructure for institutions, stablecoin issuers and fintechs, across Spark Savings, SparkLend, the Spark Liquidity Layer (SLL) and Spark Prime.

The sizes on the site:

| Product | Size | What it is |
|---|---:|---|
| SparkLend | **$5.05B** | Lending market and collateralised borrowing |
| Spark Liquidity Layer | **$3.20B** | Liquidity the protocol allocates centrally |
| Spark Savings | **$4.87B** | Stablecoin yield and savings products |
| Spark Prime | **$20.51M** | Institutional capital and credit |
| Total TVL | **$8.40B+** | The site's own aggregate |

**These do not add up, and should not be added.** Capital passes through: a deposit can land in Savings, get allocated by SLL into SparkLend, and be counted once in each. They describe reach, not a total asset base.

The actual path of the money:

```text
Sky / USDS liquidity
        ↓
Spark Liquidity Layer
        ↓
DeFi lending, institutional lending, RWA / stablecoin strategies
        ↓
interest, funding spread, distribution rewards, reserve income
        ↓
operating expenses, cost of funds, risk capital, SPK buyback
        ↓
protocol surplus and treasury
```

**The strategic point is that Spark does not live on user borrowing demand alone.** It stitches Sky's stablecoin liquidity, Savings products, institutional capital and multi-chain lending markets into one allocation network.

## 2. Where the money comes from, and how good it is

Revenue splits four ways: **the SLL spread** (take Sky liquidity at a cost, allocate it into yield, keep the difference), **stablecoin distribution rewards** (sUSDS, sUSDC, spUSDT and similar), **SparkLend reserves** (a reserve factor on borrow interest), and **curation plus treasury income**.

The official reports separate three measures, and mixing them up is easy: gross protocol returns (before cost of funds), net protocol returns (after it), and net protocol surplus (after operating expenses too).

| Period | Gross returns | Net returns | Net surplus | Closing treasury | SPK buyback |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Q4 2025 | $45.4M | $9.87M | $6.57M | $43.22M | — |
| Q1 2026 | $31.5M | $6.91M | $3.46M | $46.1M | $986K |
| Q2 2026 | **$40.6M** | **$4.31M** | **$0.71M** | $48.5M | $1.31M |
| Q3 2026 | — | over $4M | not disclosed | not disclosed | not disclosed |

**The Q2 row is the one to read: gross returns rose quarter on quarter while net returns and surplus fell sharply.** Growth in size did not become profit — cost of funds and product subsidies consumed nearly all of the gross. That is the key lens for a capital-allocation protocol: scale and net income can move in opposite directions.

The current annualised view:

| Measure | Projected annual |
|---|---:|
| Net protocol returns | **$18.45M** |
| Operating expenses | **$10.90M** |
| Protocol surplus | **$7.55M** |

Broken down by source:

| Source | Amount | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Distribution Rewards | **$10.93M** | **59.21%** |
| Spark Liquidity Layer | **$6.18M** | **33.48%** |
| SparkLend | $1.32M | 7.16% |
| Curation Fees | $27.48K | 0.15% |

### Distribution has become the main engine

The important structural change through the first half of 2026 is revenue shifting from the SLL spread to stablecoin distribution:

- Q1 2026, Distribution Rewards reached **$3.31M**, passing SLL net returns for the first time
- Q2 2026, it rose again to **$4.53M**
- Over the same quarter **SLL net protocol returns were −$0.81M**; distribution carried the result
- Q3 2026, Spark says SLL returns are back above the cost of funds

**That shows a genuine hedge inside the business**: when DeFi lending spreads compress, demand for stablecoin savings may rise instead.

The other side of the same fact: **this profit does not come purely from independent market demand.** It depends on Sky's cost of funds and savings rate, on USDS, USDT and USDC balances, on the settlement rules between Spark and Sky, and on institutional and DeFi lending rates. **Distribution Rewards plus SLL account for 92.7% of projected net returns — that is a concentrated book.**

## 3. The buyback: real, verifiable, and not a burn

### Parameterised, not a fixed budget

Per the public summary of SAEP-09: hold roughly `$35M` in operating and risk reserves → allocate surplus above that parametrically → spend about **10% of monthly surplus funds** on SPK buybacks → target a roughly 12-month run → size each purchase by protocol parameter → settle on-chain rather than by hand on a centralised exchange.

```text
buyback size ≈ available treasury × protocol surplus × governance parameters × execution conditions
```

**This is a relatively healthy design**, because it avoids buying back mechanically while the protocol is losing money or short on liquidity. The cost: **you cannot extrapolate future monthly size from past buyback amounts.**

### What the chain shows

Buyback address `0x797b010e0babb493b8dedd6f6ce5cc72778c2bf3` on Ethereum, through 2026-10-28 11:32 UTC:

| On-chain | Amount | Note |
|---|---:|---|
| SPK into the Safe | **201.39M** | Mostly from the CoW Swap settlement address |
| SPK out of the Safe | **187.79M** | To a Spark operations address |
| USDS into the Safe | 4.12M | Mostly from operating wallets |
| USDS out of the Safe | **4.08M** | To the CoW Swap settlement address |
| **Safe holds now** | **12.77M SPK** | Balance snapshot |
| Safe holds now | 58.03K USDS | Balance snapshot |

The official dashboard reports **200.46M SPK** repurchased against 201.39M on-chain; the small gap is plausibly refresh timing and accounting basis.

### Three readings

**First, the buyback is real but its size is unpredictable.** Q1 and Q2 disclosed $986K and $1.31M; what comes next depends on surplus, reserve requirements and parameters.

**Second, a buyback is not a burn — the easiest thing here to misread.** The chain shows SPK moving from CoW Swap into a protocol-controlled wallet and on to operations, while the buyback Safe itself still holds 12.77M SPK. **The accurate description is "repurchased into protocol-controlled wallets and held", not "permanently removed from supply".** If those tokens are later redistributed, used as incentives or sold, the supply effect dilutes.

**Third, the average cost sits above spot.** At `$4.08M / 200.46M` the average entry is about **$0.02035** against **$0.01838** today — **10.7% higher**. That does not make the buyback a failure; its goal may be long-run float management rather than a trading return. But it does mean **the buyback itself guarantees the treasury no immediate financial gain**.

## 4. Tokenomics: the actual tension

### Allocation and release

10B SPK at genesis:

| Category | Share | Amount | Release |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Sky Farming / user rewards | **65%** | 6.5B | Distributed by Sky over 10 years |
| Ecosystem | 23% | 2.3B | 17% at TGE, the remaining 6% a year later |
| Team | 12% | 1.2B | 25% cliff at 12 months, the other 75% over three years |

Sky Farming front-loads: 1.625B in each of years 1 and 2, 812.5M in years 3–4, 406.25M in years 5–6, roughly 203M a year in years 7–10. **Heavy early incentives, declining later, with real supply pressure for several years yet.**

### The gap between unlocked and circulating

As of 2026-10-28:

| Measure | Value |
|---|---:|
| Total supply | 10.0B SPK |
| Circulating | **3.396B** (33.96%) |
| Cumulatively unlocked | **4.924B** (49.24%) |
| **Unlocked but not circulating** | **~1.529B** |

**This is the most important block in the piece.** The market watches circulating supply, but the real pressure also depends on unlocked treasury and team tokens, whether Sky Farming rewards are sold immediately, whether ecosystem tokens go to incentives, whether buyback tokens stay locked, and what the team actually does with released tokens.

**1.53B tokens that are already unlocked, excluded from the market cap, and free to move is a variable no valuation model gets to skip.**

Market snapshot: price **$0.01838**, market cap **$62.4M**, FDV **$183.8M**, 24h volume $5.56M, 30-day change about **−19.2%**, market cap to FDV about **34.0%**.

### What SPK actually captures

SPK is used for governance, Snapshot voting, staking, potential future security verification, Spark Points and Symbiotic Points incentives, and SPK Farming.

**It should not be described as a dividend token.** Spark's documentation grants holders no legal or automatic claim on protocol surplus. Value capture comes from governance rights, staking and ecosystem use, the potential supply contraction from buybacks, and demand and expectations driven by business growth.

**So SPK is closer to a governance, staking and buyback-supported token than to equity with a claim on cash flow.**

## 5. Valuation and risk

Against $62.4M market cap and $183.8M FDV:

| Multiple | Value | Reading |
|---|---:|---|
| Market cap / net returns | **3.38x** | Cheap on the surface |
| FDV / net returns | **9.96x** | Future supply changes the conclusion |
| Market cap / surplus | **8.26x** | Closer to a sustainable basis |
| FDV / surplus | **24.33x** | Not cheap fully diluted |

**None of these is a real P/E**, because the surplus does not automatically belong to holders and the buyback budget is a governance decision. What they measure is **the discount between protocol fundamentals and token value capture**.

**Catalysts**: whether Q3's return to a positive SLL spread holds; continued growth in stablecoin distribution, already 59.21% of net returns; the Stablecoin FX Layer built with Uniswap and Sky, which has $150M USDS deployed and did over $14B in its first month — **the thing to watch is whether that volume converts into fees Spark actually keeps**; and whether governance commits to locking or burning repurchased tokens.

**Risks**:

| Risk | Severity | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| SLL spread compression | High | Growth fails to become surplus |
| Revenue concentration | High | Two lines are 92.7% of projected net returns |
| Token unlocks | High | 49.24% unlocked against 33.96% circulating |
| Buyback is not a fixed budget | Med-high | Falls away quickly as surplus falls |
| No burn | Med-high | Repurchased tokens re-entering supply blunts the effect |
| Sky dependence | Med-high | Cost of funds, USDS liquidity and governance sit with Sky |
| Credit risk | Med-high | SLL touches DeFi, institutional lending and multi-chain infrastructure |
| Reporting basis | Medium | Internal, non-GAAP protocol-level estimates |

## 6. Conclusion

Spark is one of the few protocols in DeFi with a reasonably complete commercial loop: a stablecoin funding source, an allocation layer, a lending market, savings products and institutional infrastructure, earning not one fee but a mix of spread, distribution, reserves and treasury management.

**But "Spark has revenue, therefore SPK goes up" skips a step.** Five things decide the outcome:

1. Whether surplus stays positive when spreads compress
2. Whether Distribution Rewards become a stable core rather than only a hedge
3. **Whether repurchased tokens leave supply for good, or return as incentives or sales**
4. Whether governance gives SPK a clearer claim on value
5. Whether the 65% user-reward release and that 1.53B unlocked-but-not-circulating block get absorbed

**Bottom line: a positive read on the protocol, while $SPK is better seen as a high-quality business with heavy supply pressure and incomplete value capture — medium-to-high risk.** If buybacks continue, tokens are locked or burned, and quarterly surplus holds above $2M–$4M, the discount has room to close. If SLL returns to a prolonged negative spread, or unlocks outpace what buybacks absorb, **today's low market cap is not a margin of safety.**
