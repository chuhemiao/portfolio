## TL;DR

**Collector Crypt ($CARDS) generates the highest revenue in this sector.** The 23 Physical TCG protocols tracked by DefiLlama generated approximately **$23.5M in Fees** over the past 30 days. Collector Crypt contributed **$16.55M, approximately 70%**. Based on revenue over the past year, its annualized revenue is approximately **$97.3M**, about 2 times Courtyard's **$47.4M**.

This “revenue” is pack sales minus official buybacks, **without deducting card procurement costs**. It is therefore closer to net retained GMV than profit. Token value capture is also weak: only approximately **$106K** was used to buy back $CARDS over 30 days, versus approximately **59 million tokens, worth about $16.7M**, unlocked over the same period. The latter is more than 150 times the former.

Courtyard takes a different route: custody and a secondary marketplace on Polygon, backed by VCs, without a token. Monthly revenue peaked at **$6.73M** in May 2026 and fell to **$2.17M (-68%)** by September. Growth in the sector during 2026 has essentially come from Collector Crypt's on-chain Gacha model.

## Collector Crypt Operating Data

**CC revolves around a Gacha-and-instant-buyback loop.** Users pay to open a pack and receive a physical card already PSA-graded, vaulted, and minted on-chain as an NFT. They can hold it, resell it, redeem the physical card, or immediately sell it back to the platform at a specified percentage. DefiLlama calculates revenue as pack sales, both on-chain and fiat, plus royalties and LP fees, minus pack buyback spending. [DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/protocol/collector-crypt?tvl=false&events=false&revenue=true)

| Quarter | Pack sales (on-chain + fiat) | Buyback spending | Buybacks / Sales | Protocol revenue | Revenue / Sales |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Q3 2025 | $73.1M | $64.8M | 88.6% | $11.46M | — |
| Q4 2025 | $115.6M | $106.7M | 92.3% | $9.44M | ~8.2% |
| Q1 2026 | $146.2M | $133.8M | 91.5% | $12.68M | ~8.7% |
| Q2 2026 | $400.8M | $368.4M | 91.9% | **$32.75M** | ~8.2% |
| Q3 2026 | **$433.2M** | $398.8M | 92.0% | **$34.75M** | ~8.0% |
| Q4 2026 (through 10/18) | $105.1M | $94.7M | 90.1% | $10.43M | ~9.9% |

*Buyback ratios and retention rates are calculated from DefiLlama's quarterly income statement. Q3 2025 included $1.83M in royalties and $1.26M in LP fees, making that quarter unsuitable for direct retention-rate comparisons.*

The table shows several things:

- **The step change in scale occurred in Q2 2026.** Quarterly pack sales rose from $146M in Q1 to $401M in Q2, approximately 2.7 times Q1's level. Retention remained at **8–10%** over the following two quarters, suggesting pricing did not sacrifice margin to chase scale.
- **Approximately 92% of pack sales return to players through buybacks.** Much of the apparent volume is therefore the same cards circulating repeatedly. Net retained revenue is a better measure of scale; DefiLlama records a cumulative **$112.51M**.
- **October could set another record.** Revenue reached $10.43M by 10/18. At that pace, the month could approach **$17–18M**, exceeding June's $16.12M. This is a linear extrapolation, however, and daily revenue varies substantially: 10/15 and 10/16 together generated approximately $2.1M.
- **Multichain expansion contributes little so far.** Of 30-day Fees, Solana accounts for $15.56M (94%), Robinhood Chain for $972K, and Base for $12K. Fiat channels contributed $6.88M in sales in Q3 2026, playing a growing role.

**The most important accounting caveat**: DefiLlama records CC's Cost of Revenue as $0. The platform must nevertheless pay to replace cards users keep rather than sell back. Those procurement costs are not deducted. The “$97M annualized revenue” should therefore be understood as net retained GMV; actual gross margins are much lower. The available data cannot establish a precise gross margin.

## Comparison with Courtyard

**Courtyard is a better-funded custody-and-marketplace platform with broader categories, but revenue declined markedly in the second half of 2026.** It mints NFTs on Polygon representing graded physical collectibles held in storage; burning an NFT redeems the physical item. Beyond Pokémon and sports cards, it has expanded into watches and sneakers and launched iOS and Android apps. Its home page promotes the Vending Machine Gacha product. [Courtyard](https://courtyard.io/)

Key points:

- **Value capture is weak.** Since April 2026, the team has used Gacha revenue to buy back and burn $CARDS, but DefiLlama records only **$841K** in cumulative holder revenue. It was $0 during May–September 2026, returning in October at $113K. Most revenue remains with the company rather than flowing to token holders.
- **Unlock pressure persists.** The schedule shows approximately **59.26 million tokens** unlocking each month, around 3% of total supply. At current prices, that is approximately **$16.7M**, equivalent to CC's entire monthly revenue and more than 150 times the 30-day buyback amount. Recipients include the team, with 32.5 million tokens per installment, the community, Seed, and Advisors. The late-September unlock was valued at approximately $10.9M. [Odaily](https://www.odaily.news/en/post/5213160)
- **Circulating-supply figures differ.** Market data reports 902.6 million circulating tokens, while the unlock schedule gives 677 million unlocked by the end of 2026-10. These use different measurement bases. Using the former to calculate market cap is relatively conservative.
- **There are reasons for low multiples.** Revenue excludes card procurement costs, the token has almost no claim on cash flows, and supply pressure continues. A 2.6x price-to-sales ratio therefore does not simply mean cheap.

## Conclusion

Physical TCG is already one of the few crypto RWA niches with nine-figure annualized cash flow. Current growth, however, mainly comes from Collector Crypt's on-chain Gacha model, which does not fully match the physical-asset tokenization narrative.

Courtyard demonstrates that custody plus a marketplace can work and has raised $37M from institutions. Revenue nevertheless fell sharply in the second half of 2026, with no public methodology available to check. CC leads in both scale and retention, but its revenue is net retained GMV, not profit, and the token receives almost none of those cash flows.

**Conclusion**: Following CC's fundamentals makes sense for this sector, but $CARDS is better traded as a high-beta volume-and-sentiment asset than held long term as a cash-flow asset. Watch four things:

1. Whether October monthly revenue exceeds June's $16.12M;
2. Whether buybacks become routine and approach more than $1M per month;
3. Price support around monthly unlocks of approximately 59 million tokens;
4. Whether Courtyard's revenue stops falling in Q4, and regulatory developments concerning Gacha.
