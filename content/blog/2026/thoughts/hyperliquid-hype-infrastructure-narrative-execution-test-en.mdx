## TL;DR

**This project has a real business behind its narrative. But the market is already paying for the “next layer of financial infrastructure,” rather than just a high-revenue perpetuals exchange.** Hyperliquid has real trading revenue, an order book developers can access, and a mechanism that turns some fees into $HYPE purchases and burns. The question is whether the developer ecosystem can consistently generate *incremental* revenue: existing data demonstrates usage and token value capture, but does not yet establish that. My view is **conditionally bullish on business expansion, while cautious about the current valuation**.

---

## Infrastructure positioning

The repositioning has a product foundation; it is more than a name change. Hyperliquid puts HyperCore, which hosts the native order book, and the HyperEVM smart-contract environment on the same chain. Developers can build lending, trading interfaces, and other applications around order-book liquidity. Official documentation explicitly presents these capabilities as building blocks for open finance. [Hyperliquid documentation](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs) · [HyperEVM documentation](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hyperevm)

However, **being available to developers does not mean developers have already formed a sustainable distribution network**. The real transition requires third-party applications to bring retained users and fee-generating activity consistently, rather than merely redistributing existing traders. Because the supplied question does not include a link to the specific widely engaged repost, I do not treat any individual post’s popularity, reach, or strength of community consensus as verified data.

**There is evidence of user growth, but simultaneous acceleration in revenue remains unproven.** Comparing daily project-series averages over the most recent 30 complete days (**2026-09-13—10-12 UTC**) with an earlier selected 30-day period (**2025-10-13—11-11 UTC**) gives the following results. These are two observation windows, **not a strict year-over-year comparison**:

| Metric | Earlier 30-day daily average | Latest 30-day daily average | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Users | 60,380 | 84,136 | **+39.3%** |
| Protocol fees | **$3.07M** | **$3.23M** | **+5.1%** |
| Protocol revenue | **$2.77M** | **$2.63M** | **−5.1%** |
| Volume in the project data series | **$339.7M** | **$194.7M** | **−42.7%** |

The chart normalizes the earlier window to 100. It highlights **divergence between user and monetization growth**, rather than claiming that perpetual trading has contracted.

![Hyperliquid user and monetization growth diverge: the earlier window is 100; latest-window users, fees, revenue, and project-series volume are 139.3, 105.1, 94.9, and 57.3 respectively](/article/hyperliquid-growth-divergence-en.svg?v=76aba9eac3)

**Scope limitations matter:** “volume” in the project series clearly does not cover the same scope as reported platform-wide perpetual notional volume. It cannot be used to calculate the platform-wide fee rate or infer changes in total perpetual volume. “Users” also cannot directly be interpreted as new users, retained users, or unique individuals. The more defensible conclusion is: **observable usage has increased, but revenue on the same data basis has not kept pace.**

## Token cash flows

$HYPE has a more direct value-capture mechanism than a governance-only token. Official fee documentation says trading fees flow to community-related destinations, including HLP, the Assistance Fund, and deployers. The Assistance Fund automatically converts received fees into $HYPE, and its $HYPE holdings are subsequently burned. Meanwhile, deployers of some spot and HIP-3 markets may retain part of the fees generated by their deployed assets. Therefore, **not all platform trading fees can be counted as token-holder cash flow, and buybacks and burns are not dividends**. [Hyperliquid fee documentation](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/fees)

Revenue sources are also being extended beyond trading fees. Reports in early October said AQAv2 made its first payment to Hyperliquid of about **$14.58M** in yield from USDC reserves. This is a new channel worth following, but **a single receipt cannot be extrapolated into a stable long-term annuity without verification**. [PANews](https://www.panewslab.com/en/articles/01a1055b-123d-716f-a43e-bfb359db13bd)

## Pricing and validation

**The valuation already requires the expansion story to keep delivering.** In the market snapshot queried as of **2026-10-13 13:22 UTC**, $HYPE was about **$81.11**, with circulating market capitalization of about **$18.04B** and FDV of about **$77.46B**. Simply multiplying the latest 30-day daily average protocol revenue by 365 gives a static annualized amount of about **$958M**. Circulating market capitalization is about **18.8 times** that amount, and FDV about **80.8 times**. This is only a valuation stress test using a consistent data basis, **not an earnings multiple or a forecast of future revenue**.

Supply also matters. The data shows circulating supply of about **222.45M $HYPE** and total supply of about **955.31M**. The unlock schedule indicates that cumulative core-contributor unlocks at **2026-11-06 UTC** increase by about **9.92M $HYPE** from the October checkpoint. Unlocking does not mean immediate selling, and cumulative unlocked supply is different from circulating supply. It does, however, make it more important to test whether buybacks can offset newly tradable supply.

I will use three groups of signals to judge whether the narrative is delivering or the price has moved ahead:

1. **User quality:** after active usage grows, do retention and fees per user improve together, rather than merely attracting short-term trading during favorable markets?
2. **Incremental developer demand:** can third-party applications consistently bring previously absent trading, collateral, or other paid demand? Application counts or integration announcements alone are insufficient.
3. **Net token capture:** do actual purchases and burns, protocol revenue, and post-unlock tradable supply improve together, rather than relying only on total volume?

## Conclusion

Hyperliquid has a **real starting point** for expanding from an exchange into financial infrastructure, so the community narrative cannot simply be dismissed as a bubble. But there is still a gap requiring evidence between composable products with real revenue and a developer-driven flywheel of sustained growth. **The greater current risk is not that the business does not exist, but that the market has priced in expansion that has not yet materialized.**

**Core judgment:** a valuation premium gains stronger support only if user retention, fee-generating activity brought by third-party applications, and net burns rise together over several consecutive observation windows. If growth remains concentrated in publicity and user counts while revenue and net token capture lag, treat it as pricing running ahead of expansion.
