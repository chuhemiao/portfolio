## TL;DR

- **On 2026-10-29 at 21:18 UTC, CZ confirmed Binance had been robbed of about `$6.3B`**, the largest single theft in crypto history and about **`154x`** the 2019 incident (`$41M`).
- **As of 2026-11-02 05:30 UTC**: withdrawals resumed in stages on 11-01 (`15` networks, `$BTC` first), **`90–95%`** of the stolen assets have been replaced from the firm's own balance sheet, and about **`$3B`** is frozen.
- **The problem was not a leaked private key** but a compromised internal signing and approval chain, then EIP-7702 delegation used to sweep the hot wallets in bulk. The FBI, TRM Labs and Chainalysis all point to North Korea's **TraderTraitor (Lazarus)**.
- **User losses on paper are zero; the cost moved onto the exchange balance sheet and its credibility.** Net outflow in the 24 hours after withdrawals reopened was `$3.64B–6.33B` depending on the method, roughly `2–4%` of its `$171B` in customer assets.
- **The thing to watch is not whether the coins come back**, but whether the 11-02 proof of reserves and the 11-03 ZK verification turn "fully backed 1:1" from a verbal promise into a verifiable fact.

> Data as of 2026-11-02 05:30 UTC. Prices are Binance spot `$BTC/USDT` and `$BNB/USDT`. The timeline is built from the publication timestamps of public reporting and official announcements; the exact second-level ordering of on-chain actions follows the transactions themselves. Two percentages in the source material disagree with the prices it gives and were recomputed; see section 6.

## 1. The long view: eight years, five incidents, a widening attack surface

Binance's security history has one clear through-line: **every incident happened at the hot edge — hot wallets, APIs, a cross-chain bridge, the signing service. The cold wallets were never breached head-on.** The scale went from tens of millions to billions.

| Date (UTC) | Incident | Scale | Method | Handling and outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2018-03-07 | API keys abused, unauthorized sell orders on thin pairs | **No coins stolen** | Phished API credentials used to manipulate trading | Withdrawals paused, abnormal fills rolled back, user funds intact [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/03/07/bitcoin-price-drops-10-as-hackers-exploit-binances-api-keys/) |
| 2019-05-07 | Hot wallet drained of `7,000` `$BTC` | About `$41M` at the time | Virus, phishing and API data combined into one withdrawal | SAFU covered it in full, withdrawals halted for days [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/technology-48199375) |
| 2019-08 | KYC documents leaked and used for extortion | No direct loss of funds | Suspected third-party vendor | Ransom refused, public bounty opened [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2019/08/07/binance-customer-data-has-leaked-what-we-know-and-what-we-dont) |
| 2022-10-06 | BNB Chain **BSC Token Hub bridge** | `2M` `$BNB` minted out of nothing (`$570M`), about `$100M+` actually moved off-chain | Forged cross-chain proof tricked the bridge contract into paying out | Validators halted block production, hard fork upgrade; Binance put the unrecovered amount near `$100M` [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/10/07/more-than-100-million-worth-of-binances-bnb-token-stolen-in-another-major-crypto-hack.html) |
| **2026-10-29** | **Multiple hot wallets plus the signing chain** | **About `$6.3B`** | Internal network intrusion plus abuse of EIP-7702 delegation | `$6.34B` provisioned, about `$3B` frozen, withdrawals resumed in stages from 11-01 |

Two things deserve to be called out separately.

First, **those `7,000` `$BTC` from 2019 are worth about `$548M` at today's `$78,336`** — the same coins, up `13x` in seven years. That is why `$41M` sounds small in its own era and belongs to a different order of magnitude on today's balance sheet.

Second, 2022 was strictly a **BNB Chain ecosystem** bridge exploit rather than exchange accounts being drained, though the market habitually files it under Binance.

Phished APIs in 2018, stolen credentials in 2019, a contract verification flaw in 2022, compromised signing infrastructure in 2026 — **every time, the attacker went around the cold wallets and came in at the layer closest to withdrawal.**

## 3. Method: how one laptop opened nine hot wallets

Three pieces on this site already take this layer apart, so this is the skeleton; the detail is in those.

- **The entry point was a person's device, not a key.** Binance accepts the attacker may have entered its internal infrastructure through a compromised DevOps laptop. The BBC quotes a Binance co-CEO describing how one laptop opens one internal system, then another, until it reaches a service trusted to issue withdrawals — withdrawals that travel the legitimate path and look routine [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cwg7xp8kx4v5o). Blockaid put it more bluntly: **the signing infrastructure was compromised, and they do not believe keys leaked.** The full root-cause breakdown is in [Binance names the root cause: what broke was a signing-capable service identity, not EIP-7702](/en/blog/binance-root-cause-is-service-identity-not-7702-en).
- **Execution was amplified by EIP-7702.** The attacker packed `50` EIP-7702 authorizations into one transaction; one of them had a nonce matching the target account and was accepted, delegating Binance 14 to an unverified sweeper contract. Bonanza Factory estimates the abused internal signing authority touched `9` hot wallets, and TRM traces funds from `10` chains. The preparation and the on-chain evidence are in [The EIP-7702 attack chain: 18 hours of preparation, one signature, one bot](/en/blog/eip-7702-delegation-one-signature-drains-everything-en), and how the signed payload becomes account code is in [The EIP-7702 signing flow: from a hash to rewritten account code](/en/blog/eip-7702-signing-flow-from-hash-to-code-en).
- **The first cut was `$XRP`.** TRM's reconstruction shows the attacker emptied `233.46M` `$XRP` (about `$296M`) first, in `4` minutes [Bloomingbit](https://en.bloomingbit.io/feed/news/123377). That was not arbitrary: `$XRP` is deep and has many cross-chain routes, which makes it the best thing to move before anything can be frozen.

**This is why replacing the coins matters more than recovering them.** About `$1.45B` of the stolen assets is `$BTC`, which cannot be frozen at the protocol layer; the stablecoins are the interceptable part. So Binance covered the customer shortfall from its own assets while working with issuers to freeze what could be frozen — two legs at once.

## 4. Fund flows, replenishment and current status

The freezes landed fast, and that is the biggest operational difference from 2019: **in 2019 almost nothing was recovered; in 2026 nearly half was frozen within `24` hours.**

| Item | Amount / status |
|---|---|
| Total stolen | `$6.3B` (TRM counts `$6.36B`; Arkham tracked `$5.84B` early; Glassnode first estimated `$5.3B`) |
| Frozen or located | About `$3B`: Tether `$1.567B` + Circle `$800M+` + Ripple about `$200M` + project-level freezes |
| Own funds provisioned | `$6.34B`, of which `$5B` is in the matching assets and already in custody |
| Replenishment | `90%` (10-31) to `95%` (11-01) |
| Sources drawn on | SAFU, the excess of the futures insurance fund, and the corporate treasury; about `$1.85B` of trackable insurance-type funds remained afterwards [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/binance-tapped-its-insurance-fund-after-the-hack-whats-left/) |
| Attacker holdings | Reported at `18,270` `$BTC`, more `$BTC` than the Coinbase corporate treasury holds |
| Laundering | About `$242M` moved through bridges in two days (about `$140M` across `4` bridges); single transfers of `485,300` `$SOL` (`$46.35M`) and `2.57M` `$LINK` (`$27.95M`) |
| Withdrawals | Reopened in stages from 11-01 08:01, `15` networks, `$BTC` first; all withdrawals now processed from new wallet addresses |
| Proof of reserves | Full PoR scheduled for 11-02, ZK verification for 11-03; **not published as of this cutoff** |
| Investigation | The FBI has attributed it to North Korea; the Secret Service says the investigation is ongoing; Korea's FSC held an emergency meeting and is pushing for higher cold-wallet ratios |

**The outflow numbers differ by method and have to be read separately.** The Block counts `$3.64B` in net outflow after withdrawals reopened. Cointelegraph cites `$5.8B` and stresses it is a small share of roughly `$171B` in customer assets. DefiLlama reads `$6.33B` over 24 hours (`$5.86B` at another cut). The spread comes from whether the window starts when withdrawals reopened or spans a fixed 24 hours, and whether internal transfers are netted out. **They agree on direction: the outflow is large, and it did not become a bank run.**

One more figure from CZ is worth noting: `98%` of users chose to stay. That is a PR line, but it is the same order of magnitude as outflows being only `2–4%` of customer assets.

One gap in the disclosure is still open. The missing items listed in [Binance still has not published the signing logs](/en/blog/binance-has-not-disclosed-signing-logs-en) — the signing-side logs and a post-mortem — have not been filled in as of this cutoff.

## 5. The market and credibility cost

The price reaction was violent but contained, and structurally unlike the panic of 2019.

| Asset | Pre-incident high (10-28) | Lowest reading during the event | 11-02 05:30 | Drawdown from the high |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `$BTC` | `$81,400` | `$76,771` (10-30 15:03) | `$78,336` | `−5.7%` at the low, `−3.8%` now |
| `$BNB` | `$759` | `$702` (10-30) | `$710` | `−7.5%` at the low, `−6.5%` now |

- `$BTC` finished October `−7.32%`, its worst October in `12` years.
- `$BNB`, which trades as a proxy for exchange equity, fell more than `$BTC` — consistent with an event aimed at the exchange itself.
- **Leverage flush**: `$1.53–1.6B` liquidated across the market, a mid-sized washout rather than systemic deleveraging.
- **Transmission into traditional finance**: spot `$BTC` ETFs saw `$643.2M` of net outflow, and crypto-linked equities fell with it — Coinbase down over `13%` (compounded by a Q3 revenue decline), Circle over `9%`, Strategy about `8%`, IREN over `10%`.
- **Narrative**: Standard Chartered expects this to accelerate flow toward DEXes, while Bitwise's Matt Hougan says a theft this size would have sunk Binance and `$BTC` a decade ago and will not now.

Set against 2019: `$41M` then raised an existential question about whether the exchange would survive. `$6.3B` now raises an operational one about whether reserves are sufficient and whether withdrawals will clog. **The market now treats Binance as a financial institution to be stress-tested, not a website that might disappear.**

## 6. What this method cannot tell you

**Two percentages in the source material disagree with the prices in the same material and were recomputed.**

- **The `$BNB` figure of `6.2%` below the pre-incident high became `6.5%`.** Using the two prices the material itself gives, `$710 ÷ $759 − 1 = −6.46%`. For `6.2%` to hold, the pre-incident high would have to be `$757`, not `$759`. Only one of the two can stay, and this piece keeps the price and recomputes the percentage.
- **The `$BTC` claim of a `$77,331` low on 10-31 and a `−5.0%` drawdown was not used.** The same material's own timeline quotes `$76,771` at 10-30 15:03, below that supposed low. So this piece records `$76,771` as the lowest reading, which is `−5.7%` from `$81,400`, and timestamps every reading rather than asserting which was the true low of the window — the material never gives intraday lows.

**Four more limits belong to the method itself:**

- **The timeline is reporting time, not chain time.** The timestamps come from when announcements and articles were published; the actual on-chain ordering follows the transactions, and the two can differ by minutes to tens of minutes.
- **The roughly `$3B` frozen does not add up from its parts.** Tether `$1.567B` plus Circle `$800M+` plus Ripple about `$200M` is `$2.567B`, with the remainder filed under project-level freezes and no breakdown. The number is a stitch-up of separate disclosures, not a reconciliation.
- **The total itself has four versions** (`$6.3B` / `$6.36B` / `$5.84B` / `$5.3B`), differing by cutoff time and pricing method. This piece uses the Binance figure of `$6.3B`, but every ratio built on it carries that uncertainty.
- **Proof of reserves has not been published, so "fully backed" is not verifiable today.** All that can be said is that Binance claims `90–95%` replenishment, not that it has been replenished — which is exactly why the first watch item exists.

---

**Conclusion.** Across eight years, the Binance security model has worked, but it defended the wrong thing. The cold wallets were never breached, SAFU never defaulted, and compensation was never partial — **on the axis of coins being stolen, the system succeeded.** What 2026 proves is that when the attacker switches targets from the vault to the pen that signs withdrawals, hot-cold separation, multisig and the insurance fund all stop mattering, because the transactions look entirely legitimate to the system.

Three things actually changed. **First, the scale crossed the threshold of what a company absorbs and a market ignores**: `$6.3B` is `3.7%` of customer assets, covered only by stacking SAFU, the futures insurance excess and the treasury. That is not a reusable buffer. **Second, the adversary is now a state-level APT** — Lazarus has taken `$11.87B` cumulatively — so the thing being defended against is patient, funded and capable of supply-chain intrusion. **Third, trust is priced differently**: the market no longer asks whether you will pay, it asks what proof you have that you can. That is why the 11-02 proof of reserves matters more than any statement.

**Bottom line.** This will not end Binance, but it ends the old frame of an exchange hack as a black swan. From here, **an exchange is anchored on verifiable reserves and withdrawal resilience, not on volume.**

**Three things to watch over the next 72 hours:** first, whether the full 11-02 PoR covers every stolen asset and includes the `$6.34B` provision — majors only counts as incomplete; second, whether the 11-03 ZK verification lets users check for themselves, which is the line between "trust me" and "verify me"; third, whether withdrawals slip again or pick up new limits — one more delay turns that `$3.64B` outflow into the start of a second run.
