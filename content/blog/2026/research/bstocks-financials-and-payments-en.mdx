## TL;DR

**This issuer is the in-house book of Binance, and the two largest instruments in this group track two of its competitors. They hold 90.9% of the group by market cap and they are also the only two names in it that fell — but only the second fact survives a window control.**

- **COINB `$18.17M` + HOODB `$5.67M` = 90.9% of the group by market cap.** The three traditional financials (JPMB, GSB, PYPLB) hold `$2.38M` between them, 9.1%.
- **That concentration is not a demand signal.** Control for the listing cohort and COINB ranks 5th of the 10 names listed its day, HOODB 7th of 10 — **both mid-pack** — while the three small ones are small mostly because JPMB has only been listed 24 days.
- **What is genuinely concentrated is the drawdown**: COINB `−23.03%` (63rd on the book) and HOODB `−25.29%` (68th) are the only two negatives in the group; JPMB `+2.89%` (13th), GSB `−1.14%` (20th) and PYPLB `+7.49%` (9th) all rank in the best 20.
- **That one survives the window control.** GSB and PYPLB listed the same day (95-day window) and rank 4th and 2nd of the ten names in it, a cohort that also contains AMATB at `−26.30%` and DELLB at `−22.84%`.
- **Turnover runs exactly backwards**: JPMB `2.263x` (18th on the book), PYPLB `2.239x` (19th), GSB `1.436x` (31st), against COINB at `0.281x` (89th) and HOODB at `0.532x` (68th). **But the three small ones do `$4.62M` of volume between them, less than the `$5.11M` COINB does alone.**
- **Both companies that got listed are tokenized-equity issuers themselves.** Coinbase runs 10 instruments on Base holding `$7.5M` on chain; Robinhood runs 190 instruments holding `$151M`. **COINB, one token on bStocks, carries 2.4 times the market cap of the entire Coinbase product line** (different sources, see section 8).
- **This snapshot cannot produce a premium.** No underlying reference price, no dividend-reinvestment multiplier.

## 1. What these five are

bStocks is the in-house tokenized-equity issuer of Binance (BTech Holdings Limited, ADGM / FSRA listed certificates, BNB Chain only, traded on Binance only). CoinMarketCap reports 105 instruments; the snapshot behind this piece holds 100 of them, timestamped `2026-10-31T06:06:52Z`.

| Token | Underlying | Listed | Days | Price | Market cap | 24h volume | Turnover | 30d |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| [**COINB**](/en/stocks/bstocks/COINB) | Coinbase Global | 2026-07-06 | 117 | $145.73 | **$18.17M** | **$5.11M** | 0.281x | **−23.03%** |
| [**HOODB**](/en/stocks/bstocks/HOODB) | Robinhood Markets | 2026-07-13 | 110 | $85.32 | $5.67M | $3.02M | 0.532x | **−25.29%** |
| [**PYPLB**](/en/stocks/bstocks/PYPLB) | PayPal Holdings | 2026-07-28 | 95 | $56.82 | $981.5K | $2.20M | 2.239x | **+7.49%** |
| [**GSB**](/en/stocks/bstocks/GSB) | Goldman Sachs | 2026-07-28 | 95 | $892.92 | $896.8K | $1.29M | 1.436x | −1.14% |
| [**JPMB**](/en/stocks/bstocks/JPMB) | JPMorgan Chase | 2026-10-07 | 24 | $338.39 | $499.6K | $1.13M | 2.263x | +2.89% |

The five hold `$26.22M` between them, **2.0%** of the book total of `$1,318.38M`, and do `$12.74M` of volume, **1.5%** of the book total of `$873.46M`.

**What makes this group unusual is the issuance side.** The bStocks issuance model is exchange-self — the issuer and the only trading venue are the same entity, and that entity is Binance. **Two of the five underlyings here are crypto trading venues: Coinbase and Robinhood.** The two largest financial instruments on this book track direct competitors of the issuer.

## 2. 90.9% of the market cap sits in two competitors

| Category | Instruments | Market cap | Share of group | Volume | Share of group |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Crypto trading venues | COINB, HOODB | **$23.84M** | **90.9%** | $8.13M | 63.8% |

| Group total | five | $26.22M | 100% | $12.74M | 100% |

**COINB alone carries 7.6 times the combined market cap of the three traditional financials.** It ranks 16th of 100 by market cap and 25th by volume; HOODB ranks 36th and 39th. JPMB ranks 93rd by market cap, GSB 75th, PYPLB 69th.

That set of numbers invites one sentence: **on a tokenized-stock venue run by a crypto exchange, the users bought the crypto exchanges, not the banks.** The reading sounds plausible, and it fits the overall character of this book — the largest instrument on it is SPCXB over SpaceX, with Circle and MicroStrategy second and third, all names from a crypto or tech narrative.

**The next section explains why the reading does not hold up in the data.**

## 3. Counter-example: control for the listing cohort and the concentration disappears

**Market cap and listing date are tightly linked on this book.**

| Listing month | Instruments | Market cap | Turnover |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2026-06 | 10 | **$689.03M** | 0.619x |
| 2026-07 | 46 | $524.74M | 0.676x |
| 2026-08 | 12 | $38.57M | 0.537x |
| 2026-09 | 17 | $54.71M | 1.075x |
| 2026-10 | 15 | $11.34M | **1.143x** |

**COINB and HOODB both listed in 2026-07; JPMB listed in 2026-10, a month whose 15 instruments hold `$11.34M` in total.** So half the explanation for "bank tokens are tiny here" is that they listed late.

Compare each instrument against the names that listed the same day and the concentration vanishes:

| Token | Listing cohort | Cap rank inside it | Largest in cohort | Smallest in cohort |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| COINB | 2026-07-06 (10 names) | **5th** | GOOGLB $104.99M | GLWB $1.79M |
| HOODB | 2026-07-13 (10 names) | **7th** | AVGOB $17.80M | IBMB $979.6K |
| PYPLB | 2026-07-28 (10 names) | 8th | AMATB $9.02M | FLNCB $888.1K |
| GSB | 2026-07-28 (10 names) | 9th | AMATB $9.02M | FLNCB $888.1K |
| JPMB | 2026-10-07 (4 names) | 4th | SECZB $1.24M | JPMB $499.6K |

**COINB ranks 5th in its own cohort — exactly the middle. HOODB ranks 7th, below the middle of its own.** If capital on this venue really concentrated in crypto-exchange equities, these two should tower over the names that listed alongside them, and they do not. **HOODB is smaller than AVGOB, RKLBB, TSMB, BABAB, MRVLB and AAOIB from its own day.**

So where does the 90.9% come from? **From the composition of this group, not from a preference.** The grouping puts two instruments from 2026-07 next to three smaller ones, one of which listed in 2026-10, and the ratio comes out at 90.9% — **redraw the group and that number becomes something else.**

**JPMB carries one more caveat of its own: it has been listed 24 days.** Its range width of `1.100x` carries no information — the first piece in this series, [the looser the anchor, the wider the range](/en/blog/bstocks-flagship-pricing-anchors-en), established with BRKBB (`1.031x`, 12 days) that the narrow range of a new listing is an artifact of the window, and JPMB is the same case.

## 4. What is genuinely concentrated is the drawdown

**Section 2 does not survive; the comparison below does.**

| PYPLB | payments | **+7.49%** | 9 | +0.04% | −0.24% | −11.3% | 47.1% |
| JPMB | bank | +2.89% | 13 | +2.35% | −1.50% | −2.1% | **76.6%** |
| GSB | bank | −1.14% | 20 | +1.97% | −2.62% | −17.1% | 15.3% |
| COINB | crypto trading venue | **−23.03%** | 63 | **−11.29%** | **−5.53%** | −29.8% | 11.5% |
| HOODB | crypto trading venue | **−25.29%** | 68 | −9.84% | −1.82% | **−33.2%** | **6.9%** |

**The table splits the same way in every column.** All three traditional financials rank in the best 20 of the book on 30-day change (only 18 of 100 are positive) and all three are positive over 7 days; the two crypto venues rank 63rd and 68th, are down `11.29%` and `9.84%` over 7 days, sit `29.8%` and `33.2%` below their highs and 11.5% and 6.9% of the way up their ranges — **both pinned near the bottom of their own ranges, against a book median of 22.4% for that column.**

**And this one survives the window control.** GSB and PYPLB listed the same day (2026-07-28, 95 days), so all ten names in that cohort share an identical window:

| Token | Type | 30d |
|---|---|---:|
| SOXSB | inverse ETF −3x | +70.82% |
| **PYPLB** | **common stock** | **+7.49%** |
| AAPLB | common stock | +3.20% |
| **GSB** | **common stock** | **−1.14%** |
| AMZNB | common stock | −4.26% |
| SMHB | ETF | −15.27% |
| DELLB | common stock | −22.84% |
| AMATB | common stock | −26.30% |
| FLNCB | common stock | −26.55% |

**PYPLB 2nd and GSB 4th, inside a window whose worst name fell 29.11%.** The other end matches: COINB ranks 7th of 10 on 30-day change in its own cohort, HOODB 7th of 10 in its own — **both in the bottom half of their own days, so this split is not a listing-date effect.**

**But stop here and do not turn it into a cause.** This snapshot carries no reference price for the underlying, so it **cannot separate "the token followed the underlying stock" from "the token fell on this venue".** That crypto-venue equities correlate with crypto markets is common knowledge in public markets, and this snapshot has no power to test it — which is exactly the first limit in section 8.

One more counter-example kills "adjacent to the issuer business therefore fell": **PYPLB is a payments company, equally adjacent, and it has the best 30-day change in the group (`+7.49%`, 9th on the book).** So the only description that holds is narrower: **what fell in this group are the two crypto-venue tokens, not the status of being a competitor.**

## 5. Turnover runs exactly backwards

| Token | Market cap | Cap rank | 24h volume | Volume rank | Turnover | Turnover rank |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| JPMB | $499.6K | 93 | $1.13M | 85 | **2.263x** | **18** |
| PYPLB | $981.5K | 69 | $2.20M | 51 | 2.239x | 19 |
| GSB | $896.8K | 75 | $1.29M | 77 | 1.436x | 31 |
| HOODB | $5.67M | 36 | $3.02M | 39 | 0.532x | 68 |
| COINB | **$18.17M** | 16 | **$5.11M** | 25 | **0.281x** | 89 |

**The turnover ordering is almost the exact inverse of the market-cap ordering.** That does not mean the three small ones are more liquid — turnover is 24h volume divided by market cap, so a small denominator inflates it, which is what the second piece, [the denominator trap](/en/blog/bstocks-single-stock-2x-etfs-en), took apart.

**Absolute volume puts it back**: JPMB `$1.13M` + GSB `$1.29M` + PYPLB `$2.20M` = `$4.62M`, **less than the `$5.11M` COINB does on its own.** Three instruments cannot match the volume of one, while their turnover runs 5 to 8 times higher.

This group also exposes a measurement problem:

| Method | Result |
|---|---:|
| Aggregate turnover (sum of volume / sum of market cap) | **0.4859x** |
| Median of the member turnovers | **1.436x** |
| Aggregate turnover, whole book | 0.6625x |
| Aggregate turnover, the other 95 | 0.6661x |

**Same five instruments, two methods, a factor of 3.0 between them — and they land on opposite sides of the book average.** The aggregate is pushed down to `0.4859x` by the large denominators at COINB and HOODB (the other 95 instruments turn over 1.37 times faster than this group); the median is pulled up to `1.436x` by the three small ones. **Which number is right depends on the question**: how many times the capital parked in this group changed hands in a day is the first; how active a typical instrument in it is, the second. This piece gives both and labels which is which.

## 6. Structure: both companies that got listed are tokenized-equity issuers themselves

The bStocks structure, all of it from the issuer record on this site:

- **The wrapper is a tracker certificate**, issued by BTech Holdings Limited as an ADGM / FSRA listed certificate — **not equity**.
- **No voting rights. Dividends are pass-through: a 30% US withholding deduction first, then reinvested through a multiplier.**
- **Reserves are covered by attestation, not an on-chain proof of reserve.**
- **Redemption is available, requires KYC, minimum $5. US persons are blocked.**
- **BNB Chain only, Binance only, BSC deposits only — a wrong-network transfer loses the asset.**
- **The issuance model is exchange-self.**

**And the underlyings of COINB and HOODB are tokenized-equity issuers in their own right.** Lined up side by side:

| Issuer | Issuance model | Instruments | On-chain value | Wrapper | Chain | Venues |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|---|---|
| bStocks (this piece) | exchange self | 105 (100 in snapshot) | $914M | tracker certificate | BNB Chain | Binance only |
| Robinhood Stock Tokens | broker self | 190 | $151M | debt claim | Robinhood Chain | Robinhood app only |
| Coinbase (Base) | exchange on-chain | 10 | $7.5M | direct security | Base | Uniswap, Aerodrome, Aave, Morpho |

**The most striking ratio in that table: COINB, one token on bStocks, carries a market cap of `$18.17M`, while the entire on-chain tokenized-stock line of Coinbase holds `$7.5M` — a factor of 2.4.** (The two figures come from different sources at different times, see point five in section 8, so read it as an order of magnitude, not a precise ratio.)

The structural differences are worth a line each, because these are three different approaches:

- **Robinhood wraps its tokens as a debt claim**, not a tracker certificate; voting is marked "planned", the only one of the three not marked "none"; but whether redemption is available and what the proof of reserve is are both unknown. One chain, one venue — the same shape as bStocks.
- **Coinbase wraps its tokens as a direct security**, with shares held 1:1 by Alpaca. It launched on 2026-08-24 with NVDAc, METAc, AAPLc and GOOGLc, and **13 contracts are deployed with only 4 carrying supply**. It is the only one of the three that does not trade inside its own exchange — tradable on Uniswap and Aerodrome, usable as Aave collateral, borrowable against on Morpho.
- **bStocks is the largest by value, the second-largest by instrument count, and the narrowest by venue**: one chain, one exchange.

**This is not an accusation, it is a structural description.** But it turns the question in section 2 into something observable: **an exchange tokenized the equity of its competitors while those competitors ship the same product — and on this venue, the certificate over one competitor is larger than that competitor entire product line.**

## 7. This snapshot was taken on a Saturday

The timestamp `2026-10-31T06:06:52Z` is a Saturday morning UTC. The US cash market closed at 20:00 UTC on Friday and does not open again until Monday.

This has already been quantified once on the same issuer: [one stock, four order books](/en/blog/aapl-four-books-tokenized-equity-liquidity-en) lined up four tokenized versions of AAPL on a Saturday and concluded that **a quote outside trading hours is a market maker opinion, not a market price**.

So read the 24-hour changes accordingly: that window (Friday 06:06 UTC to Saturday 06:06 UTC) **contains a full Friday US session**, so it is not a pure closed-market quote; but the snapshot itself lands inside the closure, and **for the two-plus days until Monday, these token prices are the only prices these names have.**

**There is an extra implication for this group.** The underlyings of COINB and HOODB are crypto trading venues, and **crypto markets do not close on weekends**. So in the two days after this snapshot, crypto kept trading while the underlying shares of these two names sat at a Friday close. **What the price of COINB is tracking across those two days is something this data cannot answer** — it can only tell you what the quote was at that moment.

## 8. What this method cannot compute

**First, no premium or discount, and no verdict on whether the token tracks its underlying.** The snapshot holds only the price of the token and **no reference price for the underlying**. More decisively, dividends take a 30% US withholding deduction and are then reinvested through a multiplier — **without the current multiplier, token unit price and share price are not comparable in the first place.** Which also means the split in section 4 can only be described, not attributed: **there is no way to tell how much of the `−23.03%` at COINB came from the underlying and how much from this venue.**

**Second, no shares per token.** The snapshot carries no conversion ratio, so circulating supply converts into no claim at all.

**Third, the windows behind range width are not equal.** These five run 117 / 110 / 95 / 95 / 24 days. Every book-wide width rank quoted here is restricted to the 46 instruments listed 100 days or more — **only COINB and HOODB qualify** (13th and 18th narrowest of the 46), and the `1.100x` of JPMB is already void under this rule.

**Fourth, for an instrument listed under 30 days, the "30-day" field does not cover 30 days.** Fifteen instruments on the book have been listed under 30 days and 11 under 20 — **JPMB has 24, so its `+2.89%` does not correspond to 30 days.** The proof sits on another instrument: APPB has been listed 5 days and its 7-day and 30-day fields are identical (`−16.11%` both), so the field is clipped to available history. **In the section 4 table, the complete windows are COINB, HOODB, GSB and PYPLB; discount the JPMB row.**

**Fifth, market cap is the CMC definition**: price times circulating supply. The book total of `$1,318.38M` here is the sum of these 100 rows. **The "on-chain value" column in section 6 is a different source at a different time** — the `$914M` for bStocks, the `$151M` for Robinhood and the `$7.5M` for Coinbase all are, and none of them reconciles with a CMC market cap. **So read the 2.4x between COINB and the Coinbase product line as an order of magnitude only.**

**Sixth, 100 of 105.** CMC reports 105 instruments; this snapshot holds 100. The missing 5 are in none of these figures.

**Seventh, one chain.** All 100 exist only on BNB Chain — no cross-chain liquidity fragmentation, **and no redundancy either.**

---

**Conclusion.** This group contains two concentrations that look alike and are not.

**The first one is false.** COINB and HOODB hold 90.9% of the group by market cap, but control for the listing cohort and COINB ranks 5th of 10 while HOODB ranks 7th of 10 — **both mid-pack, with no evidence at all that capital here concentrated in crypto-exchange equities.** That 90.9% is an artifact of the grouping: two instruments from 2026-07 next to three smaller ones, one of which listed only in 2026-10.

**The second one is real.** These two are the only names in the group down over 30 days (`−23.03%`, `−25.29%`), while the other three at `+2.89%`, `−1.14%` and `+7.49%` all rank in the best 20 on the book; and the GSB and PYPLB results come from an identical 95-day window whose worst name fell `29.11%`. **The drawdown split is not a listing-date effect.** But this snapshot has no underlying price, so it can tell you how far the token fell and **not whether the underlying fell or the venue did** — and the `+7.49%` at PYPLB incidentally proves that being adjacent to the business of this issuer explains nothing by itself.

One last thing worth keeping: **both companies that got listed ship tokenized equity themselves.** Coinbase runs 10 instruments on Base holding `$7.5M` on chain, with 13 contracts deployed and only 4 carrying supply; Robinhood runs 190 instruments holding `$151M` on one chain and one venue. **And the certificate over Coinbase on bStocks is 2.4 times the size of the whole Coinbase line.**
