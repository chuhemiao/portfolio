## TL;DR

**These five instruments share a label. They share no behaviour.**

- **AMCB is one of only 18 instruments on the book that are positive over 30 days** (`+2.27%`), against a book median of `−16.71%` and 82 instruments negative.
- **Only 18 instruments sit above the midpoint of their own since-listing range, and both GMEB (`62.3%`) and AMCB (`52.4%`) are among them** — two of this group of five, a ninth of that entire list.
- **DJTB, in the same group, is the 3rd-widest instrument on the book and the widest common stock (`3.212x`).** Only two 2x leveraged ETFs are wider. Its 30-day figure is `−38.12%`, it sits `−67.5%` from its high, and it is pinned at `2.0%` of its range.
- **The methodological limit comes first**: GMEB (2026-08-11) and DJTB (2026-08-25) were each the only instrument listed on their day, **so the two most important members have no same-batch control at all** — the window-controlled comparison every prior report in this series leaned on is simply unavailable for them.
- **In all three batches where a control does exist, the narrative stock is narrower or level**: HIMSB `1.197x` against CRMB (Salesforce) `1.186x`, 0.9% apart; GPROB `1.519x` against RDDTB (Reddit) `1.834x`, 17% narrower; AMCB `1.249x` against AGPUB `1.945x` and CYPHB `1.835x` over an identical window, the narrowest of the three. **Three for three, all pointing the other way.**
- **Strip GMEB and group turnover goes from `0.5495x` to `1.375x`.** GMEB alone is 68.0% of group market cap but does only 19.8% of group volume, and its `0.160x` turnover is the 3rd-lowest on the book.
- **This snapshot cannot compute a premium.** There is no underlying reference price and no dividend reinvestment multiplier, **so this report gives no premium or discount figure** — that is not an omission, it is not computable.

## 1. What these five are

The underlyings are the US equities that have been traded on story rather than on filings for the past few years: two 2021 meme stocks, one politically themed stock, one pandemic-era telehealth name, one hardware company whose turnaround has been announced repeatedly.

## 2. Both ends: GMEB and AMCB in the upper half, DJTB pinned at the bottom

Of the 100 instruments, 82 are negative over 30 days, with a median of `−16.71%`. This group spreads across the whole distribution:

**Two things hold at the same time, and they point in opposite directions.**

**First: GMEB and AMCB are in a book-wide minority.** Only 18 instruments on the book sit above the midpoint of their own since-listing range, and **this group contributes 2 of them** — GMEB `62.3%` and AMCB `52.4%`. Separately, only 18 instruments are positive over 30 days, and AMCB at `+2.27%` is one of those.

The top four of those 18 positive names are **SOXSB `+70.82%`, SQQQB `+24.94%`, LLYB `+21.31%` and UVXYB `+20.12%`** — two inverse ETFs, one pharmaceutical stock, one volatility ETF. **In other words, on this snapshot the property of not having fallen over 30 days belongs mostly to short instruments and defensive assets, and AMCB entered that list as a meme stock.**

**Second: DJTB is at the other end, and the other end here is book-wide.** Its range width of `3.212x` ranks 3rd among all 100, behind INTWB `5.583x` and SNXXB `3.243x` — **both 2x leveraged ETFs. DJTB is therefore the widest common stock on the book.** It is `−67.5%` from its since-listing high and sits at `2.0%` of its range, the deepest of the five and the deepest of any ordinary instrument this series has examined.

**Both of those facts live inside one five-instrument group.** So the sentence *retail narrative stocks collapse together once the narrative ends* is false for this group: **two of them are in the upper half, one is at the widest point on the book, and the other two are in between.**

## 3. The counter-example: the two most important members have no control, and the three that do all point the other way

Every prior report in this series ran the same machine: **compare instruments listed on the same day, so the window is identical and window length stops being a variable.** This section has to start by saying that **the machine runs three times here out of three available, and the two runs that matter most cannot be made at all.**

**GMEB and DJTB have no same-batch control.** Only GMEB listed on 2026-08-11, and only DJTB on 2026-08-25. **So DJTB's `3.212x` has no window-controlled reference** — it cannot be shown to be wide because of a political theme, and it cannot be shown not to be. The 67-day window is neither long nor short on this book, **but there is no other instrument with a 67-day window to compare it against.**

This has to be written down plainly, because the widest common stock on the book, from section 2, reads very easily as this group's conclusion. **It is a fact, but it is not a controlled fact.**

**The other three do have controls, and all three go the other way:**

| Listed | Window | Group member | Width | Same-batch control | Width | Result |
|---|---|---|---:|---|---:|---|
| 2026-09-08 | 53 days | HIMSB | 1.197x | CRMB (Salesforce) | 1.186x | 0.9% apart, **level** |
| 2026-09-15 | 46 days | GPROB | 1.519x | RDDTB (Reddit) | **1.834x** | narrative stock **17% narrower** |
| 2026-09-22 | 39 days | AMCB | 1.249x | AGPUB 1.945x / CYPHB 1.835x | **1.835x+** | AMCB the **narrowest** of the three |

**Three batches, the same direction three times: the narrative stock is not wider than its same-day control.**

Item by item:

- **HIMSB against CRMB is the cleanest pair.** Same listing day, same 53-day window. One is a telehealth narrative name, the other is Salesforce — **widths `1.197x` and `1.186x`, 0.9% apart.** The narrative label buys no extra volatility here.
- **GPROB against RDDTB is more interesting, because RDDTB is arguably a narrative stock itself.** Reddit is where the 2021 episode happened. Over the same 46-day window, **RDDTB at `1.834x` is 17% wider than GPROB at `1.519x`**, and RDDTB is `−36.40%` over 7 days, 2nd-worst on the book. **It is not in this group, and it fits the description of a violently moving narrative stock better than this group's members do.** Which says the grouping is a human choice — drawn by someone else, RDDTB might be inside it.
- **AMCB is the narrowest of its own batch of three.** Its same-day peers AGPUB `1.945x` and CYPHB `1.835x` are both of the type covered in [the group with no public anchor](/en/blog/bstocks-no-public-anchor-en) (listing status unclassified). **A 40-year-old cinema chain moved less than two instruments whose underlying listing status has not even been confirmed.**

**So this section ends in two sentences.** First, **on the three members where a window-controlled comparison is possible, the claim that narrative stocks are more volatile is rejected three times.** Second, **on the member that supports that claim most (DJTB), the comparison cannot be run** — it was the only instrument listed that day, so there is no control with a matching window.

## 4. Strip GMEB and this group's turnover doubles

Group turnover is `0.5495x`, below the other 95 instruments' `0.6634x`. **That number is almost entirely decided by one instrument.**

| Basis | Market cap | 24h volume | Turnover |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| All 5 | $9.73M | $5.35M | **0.5495x** |
| Without GMEB | $3.12M | $4.29M | **1.375x** |
| Other 95 | $1,308.65M | $868.12M | 0.6634x |
| All 100 | $1,318.38M | $873.46M | 0.6625x |

**GMEB alone is 68.0% of group market cap but does only 19.8% of group volume.** Strip it and the remaining four turn over at `1.375x` — **from below the book to 2.07x the book.**

GMEB's own numbers are extreme: **turnover `0.1604x` is the 3rd-lowest on the book** (above only AMATB `0.1297x` and COHRB `0.1412x`), while its $6.61M market cap ranks 34th of 100. **An upper-middle market cap paired with the 3rd-lowest turnover on the book.**

The other four sit at the opposite end: GPROB `2.167x`, AMCB `2.011x`, DJTB `1.392x`, while HIMSB's `0.245x` is the 10th-lowest and its `$183.2K` of volume ranks 96th.

**So the internal shape of this group is: one large instrument being held, one small instrument being held, one collapsing, and two being turned over fast.** Any group average describes none of them.

One basis reminder: **turnover = 24h volume ÷ market cap, so a small denominator mechanically inflates the ratio.** GPROB's `2.167x` is built on $489.9K of market cap with `$1.06M` of absolute volume — **the same order of magnitude as GMEB's `$1.06M`. Two instruments traded the same dollar amount that day and their turnover ratios differ by 13.5x.**

## 6. Structure: five instruments, one wrapper

- **The wrapper is a tracker certificate**, not equity. Issuing entity BTech Holdings Limited, ADGM / FSRA listed certificates.
- **No voting rights.**
- **Dividends pass through**: a 30% US withholding deduction first, then reinvestment through a multiplier (see section 8).
- **Reserves are attested, not proven on chain.** For comparison, xStocks uses Chainlink proof of reserve — updated daily or when reserves move more than 10%, verifiable on chain. An attestation is not.
- **Redemption requires KYC**, with a $5 minimum.
- **US persons are blocked.**
- **Single chain.** BNB Chain only, BSC deposits only; sending on the wrong network loses the assets.

That is a structural description, not an accusation. It carries one specific implication for this group, tied directly to these underlyings: **no voting rights.** Several of these five US stocks had, in the years after 2021, a direct relationship between retail ownership and shareholder votes, annual meetings, and corporate governance itself. **The tokenized version moved the price exposure on chain and moved none of the shareholder rights** — holding GMEB is not holding GameStop shares, there is no vote, and dividends arrive after a 30% US withholding deduction and reinvestment through a multiplier. **For a narrative whose core claim was that retail ownership changed the company, that is a structural difference, not a detail.**

## 7. This snapshot was taken on a Saturday

The timestamp `2026-10-31T06:06:52Z` is a Saturday morning UTC. The US cash market closed Friday at 20:00 UTC and does not reopen until Monday. **All five underlyings here are US-listed, so at the instant of this snapshot not one of the five reference prices exists.**

This has already been quantified once on the same issuer: [one stock, four books](/en/blog/aapl-four-books-tokenized-equity-liquidity-en) laid AAPL's four tokenized versions side by side on a Saturday and concluded that **a quote outside trading hours is a market maker's opinion, not a market price.**

The 24-hour change window used here (Friday 06:06 UTC to Saturday 06:06 UTC) **contains a full Friday US session**, so it is not purely a closed-market quote; but the snapshot itself lands inside the closure, **and for the two-plus days until Monday these token prices are the only prices these names have.**

One extra layer for this group: **the intraday moves of these five underlyings have historically tracked the rhythm of social media, and social media does not stop at the weekend.** This snapshot cannot test that (no tick data, no underlying reference price), **but it means the point about token prices being the only price during a closure matters more here than it does for a broad-market ETF.**

## 8. What this method cannot compute

**First, no premium or discount.** The snapshot holds only the token's own price and **no underlying reference price**. More importantly, the bStocks dividend mechanism is a 30% US withholding deduction followed by reinvestment through a multiplier — **without knowing the current multiplier, token unit price and share price are not comparable quantities.** So this report gives no premium figure; it is not computable, not forgotten.

**Second, no shares per token.** The snapshot holds no conversion ratio. GPROB's 510,896 circulating units cannot be converted into any share count without it.

**Third, GMEB and DJTB have no same-batch control, and that is this report's largest methodological hole.** Each was the only instrument listed on its day, **so the widest common stock on the book from section 2 is an uncontrolled fact.** This report does not attribute it to narrative, because there is nothing to control the window against.

**Fourth, the range-width windows are unequal.** This group runs 39 / 46 / 53 / 67 / 81 days. Book-wide width comparisons are restricted to the 46 instruments listed 100 days or more — **none of these five is in that 46** — so section 2's widths enter no statistic beyond their book-wide rank, and section 3 uses same-day listings throughout.

**Fifth, market cap is the CMC definition**: price × circulating supply. The book total of $1,318.38M here is the sum of these 100 rows, while [this site's /stocks page](/en/stocks) shows bStocks at $914M of on-chain value — **two figures from different sources at different moments, and they will not reconcile.** This report uses the former throughout and states the timestamp everywhere.

**Sixth, 100 of 105.** CMC reports 105 instruments; the snapshot in hand holds 100. The missing 5 are in no statistic here.

**Seventh, retail narrative stocks is a layer this report drew itself, and section 3 has already exposed the problem with that.** The snapshot has no such field. RDDTB qualifies under the same standard and is wider than four of this group's five members — **drawn by someone else, the numbers in sections 2 and 3 would change.** This is not a classification that came from the data.

---

**Conclusion.** The most defensible finding here is a negation: **retail narrative stocks does not correspond to one behaviour.** Only 18 instruments on the book sit above the midpoint of their own range, and both GMEB (`62.3%`) and AMCB (`52.4%`) are among them, while AMCB's `+2.27%` puts it in the book's 18-name positive list — a list headed by two inverse ETFs, a pharmaceutical stock and a volatility ETF. **DJTB, in the same group, is the 3rd-widest instrument on the book and the widest common stock (`3.212x`), pinned at `2.0%` of its range.**

**And the claim that narrative stocks are more volatile is rejected everywhere it can be tested.** HIMSB and Salesforce are 0.9% apart on width, GPROB is 17% narrower than Reddit, and AMCB is the narrowest of its own batch of three. **On DJTB, the member that supports the claim most, the test cannot be run** — it was the only instrument listed that day, with no window-matched control.

One more point about group averages: **strip GMEB and this group's turnover goes from `0.5495x` to `1.375x`.** An instrument holding 68.0% of group market cap while doing 19.8% of group volume can decide the appearance of the whole group on its own — **when you see a group average, first ask whether one member is deciding it.**
