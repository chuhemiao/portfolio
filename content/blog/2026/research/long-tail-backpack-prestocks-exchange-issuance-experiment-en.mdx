## TL;DR

**This piece treats the long tail together, because writing them separately would repeat "I cannot verify this" twice.**

- **Backpack Securities**: $31M, Solana, exchange-issued. Every other field unknown.
- **ST0x / PreStocks**: PreStocks at $20M, ST0x on Base. Every other field unknown.

Together about **$51M**, under **1.5%** of the market. Separately, each would only restate the Remora piece.

**But Backpack earns its place inside a different sequence.** In 2026 four venues ran the same experiment — an exchange issuing its own tokenized stocks — and the ranking inverts the intuition:

| Exchange | Token size | Launched |
|---|---:|---|
| Binance (bStocks) | **$914M** | 2026-06-11 |
| Robinhood | **$151M** | — |
| Backpack | **$31M** | — |
| **Coinbase** | **$7.5M** | 2026-08-24 |

**The exchange with the most users finishes last.** Not an execution failure — its core users are American, and the product is closed to US persons.

**Size is decided by reachable users, not by users.**

## Four runs of one experiment

This is the closest thing to a controlled variable in the whole series. Four venues do the same thing: issue tokenized equities themselves and distribute them themselves. Similar product shape, similar time window, identical regulatory environment.

**The only variable left is distribution.**

| Exchange | Token size | Reachable users |
|---|---:|---|
| Binance | $914M | An enormous non-US global base |
| Robinhood | $151M | An EU entity, European users |
| Backpack | $31M | Small but crypto-native and global |
| Coinbase | $7.5M | **Very large base, concentrated in the US — which is excluded** |

**Coinbase makes the point.** It is among the largest regulated exchanges in the world, posting $1.22B of quarterly revenue. If exchange flow determined token size it should rank near the top.

But its tokens are **closed to US persons**, and the US is its home market. **Its single largest distribution asset has an effective value of zero for this product.**

Backpack is four times Coinbase's size, not because Backpack is stronger, but because its users are already global and crypto-native — **a group Coinbase also has, in far smaller proportion.**

**The conclusion from this experiment: in tokenized equities, "how many users you have" matters far less than "how many of them may legally buy".** It is also why everyone is waiting on TSV approvals — that is the only switch that converts US users from unreachable to reachable.

## Backpack: right model, small book

What is verifiable:

- Size: **$31M** (2026-10-16)
- Chain: **Solana**
- Model: **exchange-issued**
- Distribution: its own venue

**Unknown**: issuing entity, legal wrapper, custody, redemption terms, proof of reserve, voting, dividend handling, US-person policy, instrument count.

**Nine dimensions, nine unknowns.** As with Remora I mark it **unrated** rather than D — insufficient information to support any conclusion, including a negative one.

But there is one meaningful difference from Remora: **Backpack's issuer identity is clear (its own exchange); Remora's is not.** A named exchange issuing for itself and a brand with no traceable entity carry different kinds of risk — the first at least has someone to hold accountable.

**That is not a recommendation, only a note that unrated has gradations inside it.**

## What the long tail tells us

Two things worth recording.

**First: the barrier to issuing is low.**

Any exchange with a custody relationship can do it. Backpack, ST0x and PreStocks prove it is neither technically nor commercially hard — the hard part is getting anyone to buy.

**Which means issuer count will keep rising, and most new entrants end up in the tail.** When a new name appears, the default assumption should be "it stops around $20M", not "the next Ondo".

**Second: the tail's risk structure differs from the head's.**

The leaders at least have a verifiable issuing entity and legal documents — xStocks' Jersey SPV, Ondo's BVI entity, bStocks' ADGM certificates. **The tail generally does not.**

And small size does **not** reduce risk: if a $20M issuer defaults, holder recourse depends on the legal structure exactly as it would at $900M — there is simply nobody writing research about it.

**Put differently: the tail's risk is not "small, so it does not matter" but "small, so nobody checked".**

## How I treat all three

| Issuer | Size | Grade | Reason |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Backpack | $31M | **unrated** | Issuer identity clear, nine other fields unknown |
| ST0x / PreStocks | $20M | **unrated** | Not even a complete size split |
| Remora | $132M | **unrated** | Ten unknowns including the issuing entity |

**All three are unrated and should not be treated as equivalent.** Under the veto checklist on `/stocks`, Remora fails the first item (verifiable issuer); Backpack clears that one and fails later ones.

**The practical advice is one line**: none of the three belongs on a whitelist today — not because something is wrong with them, but because **there is not enough information to judge whether anything is wrong** — and in financial products those two states should produce the same action.

## What I will watch

1. **Whether Backpack publishes offering documents** — it has a clear corporate entity, so disclosure is cheapest for it
2. **Whether new long-tail issuers appear** — a test of the low-barrier, rising-count thesis
3. **Whether the tail's combined share rises** — a thickening tail means fragmentation rather than consolidation
4. **Whether Coinbase's tokens open to US persons** — the one variable that could step-change the reachable-users term
5. **Whether any long-tail issuer fails** — the first default will reprice the entire tail

**Bottom line.** Backpack and ST0x / PreStocks do not warrant individual research, and this piece does not pretend otherwise.

**What is worth the words is placing Backpack inside the four-sample exchange-issuance sequence, where it exposes something counter-intuitive: Coinbase holds the largest user base and the smallest book, because its users are precisely the ones this product excludes.**

**In this category distribution has never meant user count. It means legally reachable user count.** The whole field waiting on TSV approvals is waiting on exactly that variable.
