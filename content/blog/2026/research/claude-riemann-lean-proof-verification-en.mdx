## Independent checks have been published

On November 20, Levent Alpöge released a note claiming that he and Claude had found a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis and its Dirichlet generalization. The Lean source came in 14 archive parts. MIT mathematician Andrew Sutherland then published independent checks conducted with Claude Code. His page lists **2026-11-20 at 22:05 UTC** as its latest update. It reports that 175,187 modules compiled without errors, that five theorems passed an axiom audit, and that independent kernels accepted the proof export. The status below refers to that version of the report. [Alpöge’s announcement](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) · [Sutherland’s verification log](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)

## Identify the exact problem being checked

Sutherland points to `riemannHypothesis : RiemannHypothesis` in `Main.lean`. The proposition on the right comes from Mathlib. It says that any complex zero of ζ has real part 1/2 if it is outside the negative even trivial zeros and is different from 1. The formal statement explicitly handles 1, a pole in the mathematics, because Mathlib represents ζ as a total function from complex numbers to complex numbers. Connecting the Lean statement to the classical problem requires checking the function, the domain of the zeros, and the quantifiers. [Mathlib’s ζ and RH definitions](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/LSeries/RiemannZeta.html#RiemannHypothesis) · [Sutherland’s verification log](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)

The other four targets are the expanded RH statement, nonvanishing of Dirichlet L-functions in the specified right half-plane, and two statements about zeros on the critical line, one general and one for primitive characters. In this verification, GRH covers **Dirichlet L-functions**. Alpöge expresses hopes for a broader result separately. The five targets have a defined scope. Look closely at the range of `s`, the moduli, the characters, and the exception for the trivial character at `s = 1`. [Independent challenge file](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/main/src/Challenge.lean) · [Alpöge’s announcement](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf)

## Read the axiom report for the actual theorem

`#print` shows a declaration in the current environment. `#print axioms` reports the axioms used by its proof, including indirect dependencies. The three reported axioms are standard Lean logical axioms. A dependency on `sorryAx` exposes an unfilled proof obligation. A custom axiom or one introduced by native computation calls for further scrutiny of the assumptions involved. Reviewers examine the target theorem’s dependency chain. The following queries identify a declaration from the public report. [Lean’s proof validation guide](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/ValidatingProofs/) · [Sutherland’s axiom audit](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/audit.log)

```lean
#print riemannHypothesis
#print axioms riemannHypothesis
```

Reviewers also check that the definition of `RiemannHypothesis` expresses the intended quantifiers and exceptions. Writing the target in an independent environment that imports only trusted Mathlib, then comparing it with the submitted proof, addresses that question. The placeholders in the challenge give the specification. The verifier still needs to establish that the submission supplies proofs of the targets. ## What makes a check reproducible

Source, compiled `.olean` files, and the export passed to other kernels each need their own version and digest. Sutherland published SHA-256 hashes for the source archive and the challenge and solution exports. A matching digest shows that reviewers are using the same bytes. The mathematical check is a separate step. The report’s source was compiled file by file with `lean` on unsandboxed cloud machines. A 77-module sample matched a conventional Lake build byte for byte. A comparison of the entire set remained outstanding, and the solution export inherited the same provenance caveat. [Sutherland’s provenance and caveats](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)

A stronger reproduction would pin the toolchain and dependencies, record hashes of the original archive and the exports, rebuild the solution from source in isolation, and keep the independent challenge in an environment that the submission cannot modify. It would then check the result with independent kernels and publish target names, versions, exit statuses, and unfinished checks. This lets later reviewers distinguish a rebuild of the source, replay of compiled files, and checks of the same export by several kernels. This article examined public reports and statements. It did not independently run the archive. [Lean’s proof validation guide](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/ValidatingProofs/) · [Independent Comparator setup](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis)

## The mathematical work still ahead

The independent verification report says that the five formal targets match the challenge and pass the listed kernel checks. Number theorists can use the dependency graph to find the decisive lemmas, understand how iterated theta lifts transport estimates, and see how far the argument can be simplified. In his November 20 post, Terence Tao describes verification and reconstruction of the proof strategy as ongoing work. [Tao’s November 20 post](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/11/20/unforced-euler-singularity-unforced-navier-stokes-singularity-and-the-generalized-riemann-hypothesis/)

The Clay Mathematics Institute has its own Millennium Prize rules. A proposed solution must appear in a qualifying outlet, at least two years must pass after publication, and the solution must gain general acceptance in the global mathematical community. Prize eligibility, mathematical consensus, and a machine accepting a particular formal proof each have their own evidence. The next update is easier to read once you identify which evidence it adds.
