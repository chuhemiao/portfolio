## TL;DR

**The only genuinely diversified instrument in this group came through almost untouched, while the basket diversified across a single sector fared no better than a single stock — the level of diversification decided it, not the ETF wrapper.**

- **SPYB is down `1.36%` over 30 days against a book median of `−16.71%`, with 82 of 100 negative.** Its range since listing is `1.074x`, the narrowest of the 46 instruments listed 100 days or more.
- **SMHB is the same wrapper from the same issuer, diversified across semiconductors only: down `15.27%` over 30 days.** The single equipment name ASMLB is down `14.76%`, AMATB `26.30%`. **A sector basket and a single stock land in the same band.**
- **The four mature ETFs split themselves cleanly in two**: broad mandates SPYB `1.074x` and QQQB `1.158x`; narrow mandates DRAMB `1.543x` and EWYB `1.569x`. That `1.350x` ETF median is dragged down by the two broad funds.
- **AMATB has the lowest turnover of all 100 instruments: `0.130x`.** It carries a `$9.02M` market cap (26th on the book) and does `$1.17M` a day (83rd) — **top quartile by size, bottom fifth by volume.**
- **TMFB does `$91.6K` a day, the lowest on the book.** Both the lowest turnover and the lowest volume on the entire book sit in this group.
- **The group turns over at `0.4007x`, second-lowest of the 20 groups** (the five flagships are `0.3759x`), against `0.6689x` for the other 95 instruments.
- **This snapshot cannot produce a premium.** It carries no underlying reference price and no dividend-reinvestment multiplier, **so this piece publishes no premium or discount figure at all.**

## 3. Counter-example: a basket is not necessarily narrower than a single name

That ETF table invites the reading "the wider the spread, the narrower the range". **Compare inside one window and it breaks.**

SMHB and AMATB listed the same day (2026-07-28, 95 days), so all ten names in that cohort share an identical window. By range width:

| DELLB | common stock | 1.651x | −22.84% |
| AMATB | common stock | 1.518x | −26.30% |
| AMZNB | common stock | 1.295x | −4.26% |
| **SMHB** | **ETF** | **1.286x** | **−15.27%** |
| PYPLB | common stock | 1.272x | +7.49% |
| GSB | common stock | 1.253x | −1.14% |
| AAPLB | common stock | **1.189x** | +3.20% |

**AAPLB and GSB, both single names, are narrower than that sector basket.** And ASMLB in this group — one company, via an ADR, 88-day window — is `1.232x`, also narrower than the `1.286x` of SMHB. **"A basket moves less than its constituents" is not a rule you can lean on here.**

The same table draws the boundary around section 2: **SMHB ranks 7th of 10 on 30-day change, behind four single stocks** (AMZNB, PYPLB, GSB, AAPLB). So what section 2 established is not "wider is better" — it holds across exactly one step, **whole market versus one sector. Past that step, sector baskets and single names are mixed together.**

**Skip the TMFB row entirely.** Its `1.081x` width carries no information: 12 days is not enough to print an extreme. The first piece established that caveat with BRKBB (`1.031x`, 12 days), and the 7 leveraged ETFs listed 100 days or more have a median width of `2.892x` — **a 3x product cannot genuinely have a 1.081x range; that is the window, nothing else.**

## 6. Structure: what these five tokens actually are

All of it from the issuer record on this site, none of it invented:

- **The wrapper is a tracker certificate**, issued by BTech Holdings Limited as an ADGM / FSRA listed certificate — **not equity**.
- **No voting rights.**
- **Dividends are pass-through: a 30% US withholding deduction first, then reinvested through a multiplier.** This is what drives the first point in section 8 — without the current multiplier, token price and share price are not comparable.
- **Reserves are covered by attestation, not an on-chain proof of reserve.** Among peers, xStocks uses Chainlink proof of reserve, updated daily or on a 10% move, verifiable on chain; an attestation is not.
- **Redemption is available, requires KYC, minimum $5.** (Primary redemption at xStocks has a $5,000 minimum.)
- **US persons are blocked.**
- **BNB Chain only, Binance only, BSC deposits only — a wrong-network transfer loses the asset.**
- **The issuance model is exchange-self**: the issuer and the only trading venue are the same entity.

**One row needs saying separately: TMFB is a 3x leveraged ETF inside a tracker certificate.** The underlying fund is itself a derivative built from daily rebalancing, then wrapped in a certificate — **the certificate does not track Treasuries, it tracks a fund that resets its 3x exposure every day.** The decay of holding it long-term comes from the fund mechanism, not from this wrapper, but with both layers stacked, "what does this token represent" takes two steps to answer.

By the same token, **the underlyings of SPYB and SMHB are ETFs and the underlying of ASMLB is an ADR** — a certificate tracking an ADR, where the ADR is already one layer of proxy over a foreign share. **Of the five, only AMATB has a US-listed common share underneath it.**

## 7. This snapshot was taken on a Saturday

The timestamp `2026-10-31T06:06:52Z` is a Saturday morning UTC. The US cash market closed at 20:00 UTC on Friday and does not open again until Monday.

This has already been quantified once on the same issuer: [one stock, four order books](/en/blog/aapl-four-books-tokenized-equity-liquidity-en) lined up four tokenized versions of AAPL on a Saturday and concluded that **a quote outside trading hours is a market maker opinion, not a market price**.

So read the 24-hour changes in this piece accordingly: that window (Friday 06:06 UTC to Saturday 06:06 UTC) **contains a full Friday US session**, so it is not a pure closed-market quote; but the snapshot itself lands inside the closure, and **for the two-plus days until Monday, these token prices are the only prices these names have.**

There is one extra wrinkle for this group: **TMFB is exposure to the bond market, and the US Treasury cash market does not keep the same hours as US equities.** The snapshot lands on a Saturday with both closed, so the difference is invisible in these numbers — but it exists intraday on a weekday.

## 8. What this method cannot compute

**First, no premium or discount.** The snapshot holds only the price of the token and **no reference price for the underlying**. More decisively, dividends take a 30% US withholding deduction and are then reinvested through a multiplier — **without the current multiplier, token unit price and share price are not comparable in the first place.** The absence of any premium figure here is an impossibility, not an omission.

**Second, no shares per token.** The snapshot carries no conversion ratio. The circulating supply of SPYB cannot be converted into any claim without it, **and even less so for a two-layer structure like TMFB, a certificate over a leveraged fund.**

**Third, the windows behind range width are not equal.** These five run 117 / 95 / 95 / 88 / 12 days; the shortest on the book is 5 days. Every book-wide width comparison here is restricted to the 46 instruments listed 100 days or more, and even those windows differ by more than a month. **The `1.081x` of TMFB is already void under this rule.**

**Fourth, for an instrument listed under 30 days, the "30-day" field does not cover 30 days.** Fifteen instruments on the book have been listed under 30 days and 11 under 20 — **TMFB has 12, so its `+4.81%` does not correspond to 30 days.** The proof sits on another instrument: APPB has been listed 5 days and its 7-day and 30-day fields are identical (`−16.11%` both), so the field is clipped to available history. **The book median of `−16.71%` and every ranking quoted here include those 15 clipped values.**

**Fifth, market cap is the CMC definition**: price times circulating supply. The book total of `$1,318.38M` here is the sum of these 100 rows, while the bStocks row on [the /stocks page of this site](/en/stocks) shows an on-chain size of `$914M` — **two numbers from different sources at different times, and they do not reconcile.**

**Sixth, 100 of 105.** CMC reports 105 instruments; this snapshot holds 100. The missing 5 are in none of these figures.

**Seventh, one chain.** All 100 exist only on BNB Chain — no cross-chain liquidity fragmentation, **and no redundancy either.**

---

**Conclusion.** The variable that mattered in this group is not "ETF or single stock", it is **how wide the spread goes**. SPYB, spread across 500 constituents, is down `1.36%` over 30 days, has the narrowest range of any mature instrument at `1.074x`, and tops its own listing cohort. SMHB, spread across one sector, is down `15.27%`, lands in the same band as the single equipment name ASMLB at `14.76%`, and has a wider range than ASMLB and than two single stocks in its own cohort. **The ETF wrapper bought nothing; the broad mandate bought everything.**

The other half of this is engagement. **This group holds both the lowest-turnover instrument on the book (AMATB at `0.130x`, 26th by market cap) and the lowest-volume one (TMFB at `$91.6K`)**, and its combined turnover of `0.4007x` is second-lowest of the 20 groups. The AMATB row is the one to remember: **on this book, how large an instrument is and how much it gets traded are nearly unrelated** — top-quartile market cap can sit next to bottom-fifth volume.
