## TL;DR

**One country's exposure is listed four times on this book — single name, single name at 2x, broad index at 1x, broad index at 3X. But this group holds only two of them, and the label "Asia" itself ties together two regions that moved in opposite directions.**

- **Korea is bet on four times across the book, totalling `$50.76M`, `3.85%` of it**: [SKHYB](/en/stocks/bstocks/SKHYB) (SK Hynix spot, `$29.12M`), [SKUUB](/en/stocks/bstocks/SKUUB) (SK Hynix 2x, `$434.0K`), [KORUB](/en/stocks/bstocks/KORUB) (MSCI South Korea 3X, `$10.72M`), [EWYB](/en/stocks/bstocks/EWYB) (MSCI South Korea 1x, `$10.48M`). **Only `41.8%` of it is in this group** — the other two sit in group 3 and group 2.
- **On the same index over the same 30-day window, the realized 3X-to-1x ratio is `2.795`, below the nominal `3`.** KORUB `−49.66%` against EWYB `−17.77%`; a static 3x would give `−53.31%`, so the actual fall is `3.65` percentage points shallower.
- **SKUUB's 30-day field covers 5 days.** It listed 2026-10-26, and both its `30d` and `7d` fields read `−9.85%` — the same truncated window. **So this four-rung leverage ladder cannot be compared across on 30-day returns.**
- **Counter-example: KORUB ranks only 4th on range width in its own listing cohort.** Among the ten that listed the same day, INTWB `5.583x`, SNXXB `3.243x` and MVLLB `3.075x` all exceed its `2.669x`, and all three are 2x single-stock leveraged ETFs. **The leverage multiple does not determine range width; the underlying does.**
- **The harder counter-example: the plain common stock AXTIB, listed the same day, has a width of `2.658x`, `99.6%` of KORUB's.** A plain common stock and a 3X country ETF travelled nearly identical range widths over the same 103-day window.
- **KORUB is not the worst 30-day performer on the book either.** It ranks 6th, and four of the five ahead of it are leveraged ETFs on US semiconductors (INTWB `−78.03%`, SNXXB `−61.18%`, SOXLB `−51.90%`, MVLLB `−51.66%`).
- **The label "Asia" ties two opposite things together.** The Korean half of this group, `$21.20M` (`64.0%`), is `−17.77%` and `−49.66%` over 30 days; the Chinese half, `$11.93M`, is `−14.78%`, `−9.19%` and **`+0.57%`** — a spread of `50.23` percentage points inside one group.

**But the ladder cannot be compared across on 30-day returns, because of SKUUB.** It listed 2026-10-26, giving it 5 days to the snapshot, and in the snapshot both its `7d` and `30d` fields read `−9.85%` — **the same truncated window reported twice.** So reading "2x fell less than 1x" is simply wrong: its window is a sixth of the others'. **For any instrument live less than 30 days, the `30d` field covers the whole span from listing to snapshot, not 30 days.** PDDB (32 days) just clears it; KSTRB (12 days) does not — its `−14.78%` covers 12 days.

**What can be compared is the same-index, same-window pair.** The underlying names of KORUB and EWYB both state the same index (MSCI South Korea), and both have been live more than 30 days:

**Same index, same 30-day window, and the realized 3X ratio is `2.795`, below the nominal `3`.** That direction is the one daily-reset leverage should produce in a **sustained one-directional decline**: resetting exposure to net asset value each day means the principal exposed shrinks as the asset falls, so cumulative loss comes in under a static 3x. **Had the month been choppy rather than steadily down, the sign would reverse.** To be clear about the measure: **the snapshot has no daily path, so this piece cannot decompose those `3.65` percentage points into how much came from the reset and how much from each token's own pricing deviation** — what is compared here is two token price changes, with the 1x token standing in for the index rather than being it.

This measures the same mechanism as [the directional 3X group](/en/blog/bstocks-directional-3x-and-volatility-en) from the other side: that piece held the multiplier fixed (two ±3X pairs) and attributed the difference to the index; this one holds the index fixed and attributes the difference to the multiplier. **Together they say that on this book "3X" is a statement about path, not a multiplication of outcomes.**

## 6. Structure

All of this comes from this site's issuer record, the same data behind [/stocks/bstocks](/en/stocks/bstocks):

- **Legal wrapper**: tracker certificate, ADGM / FSRA listed certificates, issuing entity BTech Holdings Limited. The holder receives a certificate tracking the underlying price, not a registration of units.
- **Voting rights**: none.
- **Dividends**: passed through, but after 30% US withholding, then reinvested via a multiplier.
- **Redemption**: supported, KYC required, `$5` minimum.
- **Proof of reserve**: attestation, not live on-chain proof.
- **US persons**: blocked.
- **Chain**: BNB Chain only. BSC deposits only; sending on the wrong network loses the assets.
- **Issuance model**: exchange self-issued; the only venue is Binance, operated by the issuer.
- **Grade on this site**: B.

Three of those carry concrete meaning for this group.

**The "30% US withholding first" line deserves separate attention, because none of these five underlyings is a US company.** The dividend mechanism this site records is pass-through, then 30% US withholding, then reinvestment via a multiplier. **But BABAB and PDDB are ADRs, and EWYB, KORUB and KSTRB are US-registered ETFs.** The dividend character of those three instrument types differs under US tax law, while the issuer record gives one uniform 30% figure — **this piece cannot verify how that figure actually applies to non-US underlyings**, only note that the record states it, and that this is one of the reasons section 8 gives no premium number.

**The underlying is already a wrapper in its own right.** BABAB's underlying is Alibaba's ADR, itself a US depositary institution's receipt against foreign shares; the underlyings of EWYB, KORUB and KSTRB are ETF units. **So buying BABAB is a certificate over a certificate, and buying KORUB is a certificate wrapped around units of a daily-reset leveraged ETF** — in section 2's `2.795x` realized ratio, the daily reset happens inside the underlying ETF and has nothing to do with the bStocks layer, but once the two layers stack the holder sees only the final price.

**"No voting rights" means little for the ETF underlyings and something for the two ADRs.** An ETF unit does not give its holder votes in the constituents anyway, so the clause adds no new forfeiture for EWYB, KORUB or KSTRB; but the ADRs underlying BABAB and PDDB typically do carry voting arrangements in public markets. **One issuer clause means different things inside this group, which is one cost of grouping five heterogeneous instruments together.**

## 7. This snapshot was taken on a Saturday

The timestamp `2026-10-31T06:06:52Z` is Saturday morning UTC. The US cash market closed Friday at 20:00 UTC and does not reopen until Monday — **for more than two days after this snapshot, these token prices are the only prices these five instruments have.**

This group carries a wrinkle the others do not: **the markets where these underlying assets live are not in the same time zone as the US market that prices them.** Korean and Chinese exchange hours barely overlap with US hours, and EWYB, KORUB and KSTRB are themselves US-traded ETFs — which means **these instruments already sit behind one time-zone layer on a weekday, and on the weekend the US layer closes too.** This piece draws no quantitative inference from that (the snapshot is a single moment and cannot see session structure), and notes only that KORUB's 24-hour change of `−3.59%` occurred along a chain where both the underlying Korean market and the US market were closed.

The same issuer has already provided a quantified version: [one stock, four books](/en/blog/aapl-four-books-tokenized-equity-liquidity-en) laid AAPL's four tokenized versions side by side on a single Saturday and concluded that **a quote during non-trading hours is a market maker's opinion, not a market price**.

The 24-hour window in this piece runs Friday 06:06 UTC to Saturday 06:06 UTC, **which includes a full Friday US trading session**, so these are not purely closed-market quotes.

## 8. What this method cannot compute

**First, the `30d` field is not 30 days for any instrument live less than 30 days.** This is the most important measurement note in this piece. SKUUB has been live 5 days and its `7d` and `30d` both read `−9.85%`; KSTRB has been live 12 days and its `−14.78%` covers 12 days. **So section 2's four-rung leverage ladder can only be set side by side structurally, never compared on returns** — only SKHYB (114 days), KORUB (103 days) and EWYB (132 days) are comparable.

**Second, the range-width windows are unequal, and unusually scattered here.** The five are 103 / 132 / 110 / 32 / 12 days. **Only KORUB, EWYB and BABAB clear 100 days**, so PDDB and KSTRB do not enter the comparison across the 46 instruments live 100 days or more. The 12-day cohort table in section 4 already demonstrates the point: all five land between `1.031x` and `1.242x`, **narrow not because they are calm but because there has not been enough time.**

**Third, it cannot compute index constituents.** Section 2 compares KORUB and EWYB as two leverage rungs on one index on the strength of both `underlying.name` fields stating MSCI South Korea. **But the snapshot has no index constituent or weight field**, and no quote for the index itself — so that `2.795` is the ratio of two token price changes, not a leveraged ETF's realized multiple against its index. **The 1x token stands in for the index here, and that proxy carries its own premium or discount and its own liquidity.**

**Fourth, it cannot compute the daily path, so leverage decay cannot be decomposed.** Section 2's `3.65` percentage point gap (actual `−49.66%` against a static triple's `−53.31%`) matches the direction a daily reset should produce in a sustained one-directional decline, but **the snapshot has only four point-in-time fields (24h / 7d / 30d / high and low since listing) and no daily series** — so this piece can compute no realized volatility and cannot say how much of those `3.65` points came from the reset versus each token's own pricing deviation.

**Fifth, it cannot compute premium or discount.** The snapshot carries only the token's own price, with no reference price for the underlying. More fundamentally, the bStocks dividend mechanism is "30% US withholding, then reinvested via a multiplier" — **without knowing the current multiplier, the token price and the underlying unit price are not comparable in the first place**. This group adds a further problem: the five underlyings are ADRs and units of US-registered ETFs, and **how that uniform 30% figure lands on them is not stated in the snapshot and has not been verified here.**

**Sixth, it cannot compute units per token.** The snapshot has no conversion ratio. KORUB trades at `$10.61` and its circulating supply is in the snapshot, but without the ratio those numbers cannot be converted into any count of ETF units. **Every market cap in section 2's table is a comparison between token floats.**

**Seventh, market cap is the CMC measure** (price times circulating supply), and the book total of `$1,318.38M` is the sum of these 100 rows; the bStocks line on [this site's /stocks page](/en/stocks) shows an on-chain size of `$914M` — **two numbers from different sources at different moments that will not tie.** This piece uses the former throughout.

**Eighth, 100 of 105, and this directly affects two ratios in this piece.** CMC reports 105 instruments and the snapshot in hand holds 100. **So the accurate phrasing of "the Korea trade has four instruments" is "four among these 100 rows," and `$50.76M` and `3.85%` are on that same basis** — if any of the missing 5 is a Korean or Chinese instrument, both section 1's `46.2%` and section 2's `41.8%` change.

**Ninth, turnover is 24h volume divided by market cap.** A small denominator inflates the ratio. These five span `$833.9K` to `$10.72M`, a factor of `12.9` — though this group happens to run the other way: **the largest, KORUB, has the highest turnover, while the smallest, KSTRB, at `0.560x` is among the lowest**, so the turnover differences here are unlikely to be mostly a denominator effect.

Only one pair in that ladder can actually be computed: **same MSCI South Korea index, same 30-day window, KORUB `−49.66%` against EWYB `−17.77%`, a realized ratio of `2.795` below the nominal `3`, falling `3.65` percentage points short of a static triple** — the direction a daily reset should produce in a sustained one-directional decline, and as far as it can be taken, since the snapshot has no daily path. **SKUUB cannot enter that comparison: it has been live 5 days, and its `30d` and `7d` fields are the same truncated window.**

Nor is it the worst faller on the book: **6th over 30 days, with four of the five ahead of it leveraged ETFs on US semiconductors.**
