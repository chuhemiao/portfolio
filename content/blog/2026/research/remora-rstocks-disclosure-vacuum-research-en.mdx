## TL;DR

**This is not research on Remora. It is a record of why Remora cannot be researched.**

It ranks fifth among tokenized equity issuers at **$132M**, behind Robinhood's $151M and seventeen times the size of Coinbase.

And that figure is **the only thing I can verify**.

**Under the veto checklist on my `/stocks` page, Remora fails the first item.** The market put it fifth regardless. **That conflict is worth stating on its own — rather than guessing my way to a piece that merely looks complete.**

## Why the absence is itself the finding

The first item on the `/stocks` veto checklist reads:

> **Issuer** — Pass: verifiable legal entity, site and offering documents. Veto: anonymous team, social accounts only, no issuing entity.

**I cannot establish what Remora's issuing entity is called, so it fails there.** By that framework it should be excluded and graded D — "issuer, underlying, contract or legal rights cannot be confirmed".

I marked it **unrated** instead, and the distinction is deliberate:

- **D** means I looked and concluded it does not qualify
- **unrated** means I lack the information to conclude anything, including that it does not qualify

**Treating missing information as evidence of a problem is the same error as treating it as evidence of safety.** All I can say is: until the offering documents can be read, it does not belong on anyone's whitelist.

## Size is not evidence

The reasoning to resist is: "it holds $132M, surely it cannot be fake."

The earlier pieces in this series keep showing that size establishes very little:

- **Ondo** is first by size, yet CRCLON turns over under 1% a day — balance is not turnover
- **bStocks** is second, yet SPCXB turns over 90% daily and fell 20% in a week — turnover is not quality
- **Coinbase** is near the bottom, yet is the only issuer accepted by lending protocols — size is not utility

**$132M tells you that much value is locked somewhere. It does not tell you who custodies it, in what legal form it exists, or who can reach it if the issuer defaults.**

Which is where this whole series started: **a tokenized stock's identity is underlying + issuer + legal wrapper + chain + contract address + venue. Remora has supplied one number that is none of those six.**

## If you hold it

I do not know what you hold, so I can only offer the part that is answerable. These five questions should have answers in any issuer's formal documents, and an absent answer is itself the signal:

1. **What is the issuing entity's legal name and domicile?**
2. **Is the underlying common stock, an ETF, or a derivative — and who custodies it?**
3. **Do you own a security, a certificate, a debt claim, or only price exposure?**
4. **Can you redeem? Cash or in kind? Minimum, KYC, settlement time?**
5. **Where is the official contract address published, and who holds mint / freeze / upgrade rights?**

**Two or more unanswerable means it should not be treated as a safe asset.** That standard is not aimed at Remora; it applies to every issuer here — several of the top four cannot answer all five either.

## What I will watch

1. **Whether formal offering documents or a legal overview appear** — the only route out of unrated
2. **Whether structured issuer fields appear on rwa.xyz or CoinGecko** — right now there is only a size number
3. **Which chains and contracts hold the $132M** — publishing that alone would make on-chain verification possible
4. **Whether any venue lists or delists it** — distribution tends to react faster than an issuer's own statements

---

**Bottom line.** I reach no negative conclusion about Remora, because I have no basis for one; I also will not put it on any usable list, for exactly the same reason.

**$132M paired with zero structural disclosure is a combination worth stopping at, in any asset class.** Ranking fifth means a substantial amount of capital made a judgement I cannot make — either they hold information I do not, or they never asked the five questions above.

**Of those two, the second is far more common.**
