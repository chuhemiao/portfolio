## TL;DR

**An exchange issues equity tokens for companies whose main asset is cryptocurrency, and the largest of them holds the exchange's own coin. Yet this group is not hot — it is the coldest group by turnover in this series so far.**

- **BNCB alone is `76.3%` of this group's market cap** (`$37.38M` against `$48.98M` for all five) and `70.4%` of its volume. It **listed 2026-09-14, has been live only 47 days, and is already the 10th largest instrument on the book** — by far the youngest name in the top 15.
- **It is `68.3%` of its own listing month.** September 2026 brought 17 instruments totalling `$54.71M`; BNCB by itself is `$37.38M`.
- **Four layers of exposure stack on one token.** The snapshot gives BNCB the CMC slug `bnb-standard-corporation-tokenized-bstocks`: the underlying company is a BNB treasury, the issuer is Binance's own BTech Holdings, the only venue is Binance, and the only supported chain is BNB Chain. **These four layers are not inferred — they are four fields in the data.**
- **And the group is cold.** Group turnover is `0.4682x` against `0.6700x` for the other 95 — **the rest of the book turns `1.43x` faster than this group.** Both large members sit at the back of the turnover ranking: BNCB 78th, BMNRB 88th.
- **Counter-example one: BNCB is not the worst performer.** Its 30-day `−14.61%` beats the book median of `−16.71%` and is the second best of these five. **If stacked exposure were the dominant risk here, BNCB should have been the worst.**
- **Counter-example two: these five are not one factor.** Over 7 days CYPHB is `+7.21%` while the other four fell `4.86%` to `17.17%`; the 30-day spread runs `−11.91%` to `−39.32%`, a gap of `27.4` percentage points.
- **Four of the five carry no underlying symbol in the snapshot, two are unclassified by kind, and two have derived company names.** Reference-data quality in this group is visibly below the book average.

## 6. Structure: four layers of the same exposure

All of this comes from this site's issuer record and the snapshot metadata, the same data behind [/stocks/bstocks](/en/stocks/bstocks):

- **Legal wrapper**: tracker certificate, ADGM / FSRA listed certificates, issuing entity BTech Holdings Limited. The holder receives a certificate tracking the underlying price, not a share registration.
- **Voting rights**: none.
- **Dividends**: passed through, but **after 30% US withholding, then reinvested via a multiplier**.
- **Redemption**: supported, KYC required, `$5` minimum.
- **Proof of reserve**: attestation, not live on-chain proof.
- **US persons**: blocked.
- **Chain**: BNB Chain only. BSC deposits only; sending on the wrong network loses the assets.
- **Issuance model**: exchange self-issued; the only venue is Binance, operated by the issuer.
- **Grade on this site**: B.

Put those fields next to BNCB and a structure appears that is worth writing out.

**In the snapshot BNCB's CMC slug is `bnb-standard-corporation-tokenized-bstocks`, while its `underlying.name` field reads "CEA Industries Inc."** Two fields naming the same entity two ways, and the slug side says BNB explicitly. That is the basis for grouping it as a treasury company here.

So someone buying BNCB on Binance holds all four of the following at once:

```text
Layer 1  Underlying company   main asset is BNB
Layer 2  Legal wrapper        tracker certificate issued by BTech Holdings Limited (Binance owned)
Layer 3  Settlement network   BNB Chain (the only supported chain)
Layer 4  Trading venue        Binance (the only venue, operated by the issuer)
```

**All four layers are written in the data, not inferred.** `issuanceModel` is `exchange-self`, `venues` is a single `{ Binance, operatedByIssuer: true }`, `chains` is a single `BNB Chain`, and the nature of the underlying company comes from that slug.

What is worth spelling out is the concrete consequence of this structure, rather than attaching a label to it:

**The redemption path never leaves this set.** Redemption converts the token back into the certificate (KYC, `$5` minimum), and the certificate's issuer and the trading venue belong to the same group. **If the underlying exposure goes wrong, the redemption exit sits inside the same structure as the exposure that went wrong.** That is the difference between having a redemption channel and having an independent one.

**Proof of reserve is an attestation, not live on-chain proof.** This site records `proofOfReserve.kind` as `attestation`. For comparison, xStocks uses Chainlink proof of reserve, updated daily or whenever reserves move more than 10%. **In a structure where issuance, settlement and trading all belong to one company, the third-party-verifiable link happens to be the weakest one.**

**Voting rights are none, so holders have no channel at all on the underlying company's treasury decisions.** For a company whose main asset is cryptocurrency, the most consequential decisions are exactly those about buying, selling and staking those coins — and that layer is entirely closed to token holders.

Finally, to connect back to section 4: **all of the above is structural description, not a price forecast.** In this snapshot BNCB's 30-day decline of `−14.61%` beats the book median of `−16.71%`, and the stacked exposure has so far not shown up as a worse outcome. **Structural risk is about what happens if something goes wrong, not a claim that something is going wrong now.**

## 7. This snapshot was taken on a Saturday

The timestamp `2026-10-31T06:06:52Z` is Saturday morning UTC. The US cash market closed Friday at 20:00 UTC and does not reopen until Monday — **for more than two days after this snapshot, these token prices are the only prices these names have.**

That carries a particular meaning for this group: **the underlying companies' main assets keep trading over the weekend.** Crypto markets never close, so a coin holding company's net assets continue moving on Saturday and Sunday while its stock does not. **The token-side quote is, for those two days, the only place that can reflect both "the crypto asset is moving" and "the stock cannot trade"** — but this snapshot is a single moment and cannot see that process.

The same issuer has already provided a quantified version of this: [one stock, four books](/en/blog/aapl-four-books-tokenized-equity-liquidity-en) laid AAPL's four tokenized versions side by side on a single Saturday and concluded that **a quote during non-trading hours is a market maker's opinion, not a market price**.

The 24-hour changes in this piece (USDEB `−7.28%`, FWDIB `−4.47%`) cover Friday 06:06 UTC to Saturday 06:06 UTC, **which includes a full Friday US trading session**, so they are not purely closed-market quotes.

## 8. What this method cannot compute

**First, it cannot tell you how much crypto these companies hold.** The snapshot has only token prices and market caps — **no balance sheet for any underlying company**. So this piece cannot say how much BNB corresponds to BNCB, nor compute a discount to net asset value. The metric most often discussed for these companies in public markets, the ratio of share price to crypto net asset value per share, is entirely unsupported by this data.

**Second, the "crypto treasury" classification comes from this site, not from the issuer.** What these five share is this site's own grouping judgement; the snapshot supplies only company name, kind and the CMC slug. **BNCB's BNB nature has data support — that slug — while the other four have no crypto-asset category in the data**: the snapshot does not say BMNRB holds bitcoin, or which stablecoin USDEB relates to. Readers who need that layer have to go to each company's public disclosures.

**Third, it cannot compute premium or discount.** The snapshot carries only the token's own price, with no reference price for the underlying stock. More fundamentally, the bStocks dividend mechanism is "30% US withholding, then reinvested via a multiplier" — **without knowing the current multiplier, the token price and the underlying share price are not comparable in the first place**. So no premium figure appears anywhere in this piece.

**Fourth, it cannot compute shares per token.** The snapshot has no conversion ratio. BNCB has 7,364,066 tokens circulating at `$5.08`, and without the ratio those two numbers cannot be converted into any share count.

**Fifth, the range-width windows are badly unequal, especially here.** The five are 47 / 88 / 39 / 24 / 32 days, and **not one of them has been live 100 days**. So none of the five enters the comparison across the 46 instruments live 100 days or more, and their widths (`1.635x` / `1.680x` / `1.835x` / `1.711x` / `1.419x`) are not comparable with each other either — BMNRB at 88 days has three times as long as USDEB to reach an extreme. **The two same-day cohort tables in section 4 work precisely because cohort windows are identical.**

**Sixth, market cap is the CMC measure** (price times circulating supply), and the book total of `$1,318.38M` is the sum of these 100 rows; the bStocks line on [this site's /stocks page](/en/stocks) shows an on-chain size of `$914M` — **two numbers from different sources at different moments that will not tie.** This piece uses the former throughout.

**Seventh, 100 of 105.** CMC reports 105 instruments and the snapshot in hand holds 100; the missing 5 appear in no statistic here.

**Eighth, turnover is 24h volume divided by market cap.** A small denominator inflates the ratio, so USDEB's `2.681x` (10th on the book) and FWDIB's `2.620x` (12th) are first of all the result of `$518.7K` and `$501.2K` market caps, **and must not be read as these two being the most liquid in the group.**

---

**Conclusion.** This is a set of equity tokens an exchange issued for companies whose main asset is cryptocurrency, and the largest of them tracks a company holding that exchange's own coin. **Four layers of exposure stack on one token — the underlying company holds BNB, the certificate is issued by a Binance-owned entity, the only settlement network is BNB Chain, and the only venue is Binance — and all four are written in four fields of the data.** The concrete consequences: the redemption exit sits inside the same structure as the exposure it would exit, and the third-party-verifiable link, proof of reserve, happens to be an attestation rather than live on-chain proof.

But the numbers do not cooperate with any dramatized version of that story. **This is the coldest group by turnover in this series so far: `0.4682x` against `0.6700x` for the other 95, with BNCB and BMNRB — which carry 96.1% of the group's market cap — ranking 78th and 88th on turnover.** And **BNCB's 30-day decline of `−14.61%` beats the book median of `−16.71%`, second best within its own group.**

Three counter-examples belong together. **These five are not one factor**: over 7 days CYPHB is `+7.21%` while the other four lost `4.86%` to `17.17%`, and the 30-day spread is `27.4` percentage points. **They are not the most violent either**: BMNRB ranks only 6th of ten on width in its own cohort, and IRENB, another crypto mining company that listed the same day, is 27% wider and 17.4 percentage points deeper. **The structural risk is nameable, but it has not yet appeared in the prices** — both sentences are required, and dropping either one distorts the picture.
