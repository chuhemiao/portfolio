## TL;DR

**The long and the short 3X of the same index are both listed here, but the money is almost entirely on the long side — and the last 30 days belonged to the short side.**

- **The two long tokens hold `$40.72M` together, the two short tokens `$6.12M` — long is `6.65 times` short.** Pair by pair: SOXLB over SOXSB is `5.44 times`, TQQQB over SQQQB is `15.01 times`.
- **And the short side was the right side over these 30 days**: SOXSB `+70.82%`, SQQQB `+24.94%`, against SOXLB `−51.90%` and TQQQB `−22.36%`. **In a month when the short side returned 70%, the long tokens are still the ones being held.**
- **Same plus or minus 3x structure, different index, 2.59 times the magnitude.** The 30-day average magnitude of the semiconductor pair is `61.36%`, of the Nasdaq pair `23.65%`. That advances the finding in [the previous piece](/en/blog/bstocks-single-stock-2x-etfs-en) that the multiplier does not set the range: **when the multiplier is fixed, the index accounts for the entire difference.**
- **"Hold both sides and decay eats both" does not hold in this window.** Holding the semiconductor pair in equal dollars for 30 days nets `+18.92` percentage points and the Nasdaq pair `+2.58` — in a one-way trend, compounding sits with whichever side is winning and the net is positive. **A choppier month can give the opposite result from the same two pairs.**
- **Counter-example: a plain common stock listed the same day is wider than the −3x SOXSB, and turns over faster.** FLNCB range width `3.044x` against SOXSB `2.695x`, turnover `2.197x` against `1.361x`, over the same 95-day window. **An inverse 3X is not guaranteed to be the most violent instrument on the book.**
- **Only two of the five have been listed 100 days**; SQQQB has 60 days and UVXYB 19, so their range widths (`1.368x` / `1.341x`) carry no information.

## 3. Same multiplier, two indices, 2.59 times the magnitude

[The previous piece](/en/blog/bstocks-single-stock-2x-etfs-en) used TQQQB as a counter-example: among the ten instruments listed the same day, the 3x TQQQB has a range width of `1.512x`, narrower than two plain common stocks (AXTIB `2.658x`, CRWVB `2.274x`), with the conclusion that the underlying own volatility explains the range better than the leverage factor. **This section does not repeat that observation but pushes it one step**: here are two structurally identical plus-or-minus-3X pairs, so the multiplier variable is held fixed and every remaining difference can only come from the index.

| Index | Long 30d | Short 30d | Average magnitude |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Semiconductors (SOX) | −51.90% | +70.82% | **61.36%** |
| Nasdaq 100 (QQQ) | −22.36% | +24.94% | **23.65%** |
| Ratio | — | — | **2.59 times** |

**Same plus-or-minus 3x daily-reset structure, same 30-day calendar window, and the semiconductor pair averages `2.59 times` the magnitude of the Nasdaq pair.** Same multiplier, same window, same issuer, same chain, same venue — the index is the only variable.

Range width gives an independent confirmation. Among the 46 instruments listed 100 days or more, SOXLB width `2.892x` ranks 43rd (4th widest), while TQQQB `1.512x` ranks 14th. **The five widest on the book are INTWB `5.583x`, SNXXB `3.243x`, MVLLB `3.075x`, SOXLB `2.892x` and KORUB `2.669x` — the first three are the single-stock 2x set from the previous piece, and the fourth is this group SOXLB.** Leveraged instruments do cluster at the wide end (median width for leveraged ETFs is `2.892x`, n=7, against `1.768x` for common stocks, n=28), **but SOXLB and TQQQB differ `1.91 times` within this group, and that gap is larger than the gap between the two type medians.**

So the reading this group supplies is: **ask which index the underlying is first, and what the multiplier is second.** Reverse the order and TQQQB and SOXLB look like the same class of risk.

## 4. "Hold both sides and decay eats both" does not hold in this window

The most repeated claim about daily-reset leveraged products is that holding both sides means decay eats both. **This snapshot tests it directly, because the same issuer on the same chain lists both sides of two indices and the 30-day window is one calendar window.**

| Index | Long 30d | Short 30d | Net on equal dollars |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Semiconductors (SOX) | −51.90% | +70.82% | **+18.92 points** |
| Nasdaq 100 (QQQ) | −22.36% | +24.94% | **+2.58 points** |

**Both pairs net positive.** Buying SOXLB and SOXSB in equal dollars 30 days ago and holding would, on these two figures, have left the combination up.

**This should not be read as a strategy, nor as "decay does not exist".** A daily-reset product resets exposure back to plus-or-minus 3x every day, so a multi-day outcome depends on the path: **in a one-way trend, compounding favours the winning side and the winner gains more than the loser loses**; in a choppy range both sides get cut. Both indices trended one way over these 30 days — visible in the sign consistency, with both shorts up and both longs down and no side reversed — so the outcome landed on the trend side.

**A choppier month can give the exact opposite net from the same two pairs.** What this table falsifies is only the absolute claim that holding both sides must lose in any window; it does not prove the converse.

One more detail that captures path dependence: **the SOXSB 7-day move is `−0.79%` while its 30-day move is `+70.82%`.** The same token, up seventy percent in a month and slightly down in the past week — almost all of the return in products like this comes from a handful of trend days rather than spreading evenly across the window. The SQQQB equivalents are `+4.09%` over 7 days against `+24.94%` over 30, equally concentrated.

**Second, three members have windows too short for range width.** SQQQB has been listed 60 days, UVXYB only 19, SOXSB 95. Their widths are `1.368x` / `1.341x` / `2.695x` — the first two are narrow because the window is short, not because they are calm. The narrowest on the book, BRKBB (`1.031x`), has likewise been listed only 12 days; this pattern repeats throughout the snapshot. **Only SOXLB and TQQQB enter the comparison across the 46 instruments listed 100 days or more.**

## 7. Structure: a directional tool stacked on a tracker certificate

All of this comes from the issuer record on this site, the same data behind [/stocks/bstocks](/en/stocks/bstocks):

- **Legal wrapper**: tracker certificate, ADGM / FSRA listed, issued by BTech Holdings Limited. The holder gets a certificate that tracks a price, not a fund unit.
- **Voting**: none.
- **Dividends**: pass-through, but **a 30% US withholding deduction comes first, and the remainder is reinvested through a multiplier**.
- **Redemption**: available, KYC required, `$5` minimum.
- **Proof of reserve**: attestation, not a live on-chain proof.
- **US persons**: blocked.
- **Chain**: BNB Chain only. BSC deposits only, so a wrong-network transfer loses the asset.
- **Issuance model**: exchange self-issued; the only venue is Binance, operated by the issuer.
- **Grade on this site**: B.

Three of those matter extra for this group.

**All five are tokens over certificates, and the underlying is itself a fund.** The chain is: the index, a leveraged ETF (a fund that resets exposure daily and charges a management fee), an ADGM tracker certificate, a BNB Chain token. **Three layers, and the daily reset in the middle layer is the source of every return characteristic these products have.** The token layer resets nothing; it only tracks the fund price.

**UVXYB carries one layer more.** Its underlying ETF holds VIX short-term futures contracts and has to roll them to maintain exposure. So its chain is: the VIX index, futures contracts, a 2x leveraged fund (reset plus roll), a certificate, a token. **Four layers, with roll cost and daily reset as two independent sources of decay** — this data records neither, see section 9.

**The `$5` redemption minimum means something particular here.** These five trade at `$77.31` / `$63.12` / `$51.41` / `$40.93` / `$18.98`, so a `$5` floor is no obstacle. But redemption is the path back from token to certificate, and **behind the certificate sits a fund that resets daily** — the end of the redemption path is not "some number of shares", which separates these from a common-stock token.

## 8. This snapshot was taken on a Saturday

The timestamp `2026-10-31T06:06:52Z` is a Saturday morning UTC. US cash equities closed on Friday at 20:00 UTC and do not reopen until Monday — **for more than two days after this snapshot, these token prices are the only prices these names have.**

For this group that definition matters more than for any other: **the "day" in daily reset is the trading day of the underlying fund.** While the underlying ETF is closed no reset happens at all, and the token quote keeps moving. So **over a weekend the relationship between the token price and the plus-or-minus-3x-daily-return product definition is looser than it is intraday** — hold one of these across a weekend and at the product level it has not been through a single reset, while its on-chain price has already moved.

This has already been quantified once on the same issuer: [one stock, four books](/en/blog/aapl-four-books-tokenized-equity-liquidity-en) lays the four tokenized versions of AAPL side by side on a Saturday and concludes that **a quote outside trading hours is a market maker opinion, not a market price**.

The 24-hour moves here (SOXLB `−9.88%`, SOXSB `+6.83%`) run from Friday 06:06 UTC to Saturday 06:06 UTC and **contain one complete US session**, so they are not pure closed-market quotes. Note that the pair has opposite signs and unequal magnitudes (`−9.88%` against `+6.83%`), which is consistent with what plus-or-minus 3x on one index does in a single day, but **the single-day asymmetry cannot be attributed from this data**.

## 9. What this method cannot compute

**First, no premium or discount.** The snapshot holds only the token price, with no reference price for the underlying ETF. More to the point, the bStocks dividend mechanism is "deduct 30% US withholding, then reinvest through a multiplier" — **without knowing the current multiplier, the token unit price and the fund unit NAV are not comparable in the first place.** So no premium figure appears here.

**Second, no decay attribution.** The table in section 4 can only compare the period returns of two tokens; **it cannot split the difference into daily-reset decay, management fee, roll cost and tracking error.** That needs a daily NAV series, which the snapshot does not hold. The UVXYB roll cost in particular is entirely invisible.

**Third, no way to say how much of the direction tilt is the listing date.** The `6.65 times` in section 2 and the listing-date gaps in section 6 (the short side later by 22 and 43 days) are two entangled factors. **This snapshot is a single moment with no historical market-cap series, so it cannot answer what the tilt would be if SOXSB and SOXLB had listed on the same day.** This piece gives the magnitude of the tilt, not a decomposition of its cause.

**Fourth, the range-width windows are unequal, and badly so for this group.** The five are 117/103/95/60/19 days. The book comparison uses only the 46 instruments listed 100 days or more, and only two members qualify. **The SQQQB `1.368x` and the UVXYB `1.341x` carry no information** — the narrowest on the book, BRKBB (`1.031x`), has likewise been listed only 12 days.

**Fifth, market cap is CMC's definition** (price times circulating supply), and the book total of `$1,318.38M` is the sum of these 100 rows; the bStocks row on [this site's /stocks page](/en/stocks) shows an on-chain size of `$914M` — **two figures from a different source and a different moment, which will not tie out.** This piece uses the former throughout.

**Sixth, 100 of 105.** CMC reports 105 instruments; the snapshot holds 100. The missing five appear in no figure here.

**Seventh, turnover is 24h volume divided by market cap.** A small denominator inflates the ratio, so the UVXYB `3.732x` (5th on the book) is first of all a consequence of a `$463.1K` market cap and **must not be read as the best liquidity in this group**. Its absolute volume of `$1.73M` is 5.1% of SOXLB.

---

**Conclusion.** bStocks lists the bull and bear 3X of the same index together, which makes something normally hard to observe directly countable: **after a month in which the short side returned `+70.82%` and `+24.94%` and the long side lost `−51.90%` and `−22.36%`, the long tokens still hold `6.65 times` the market cap of the short ones.** Pair by pair that is `5.44 times` and `15.01 times`. Part of it comes from the short side listing later (by 22 and 43 days), and this data cannot separate the two factors.

With the multiplier held fixed, this group gives a clean comparison: **same plus-or-minus 3x daily-reset structure, same 30-day window, and the semiconductor pair averages `61.36%` against `23.65%` for the Nasdaq pair, a factor of `2.59`.** That turns the previous piece finding that the multiplier does not set the range into something stronger: **with the multiplier identical, the index accounts for the entire difference.** SOXLB and TQQQB differ `1.91 times` on width within this group, more than the gap between the leveraged-ETF and common-stock type medians.

Two counter-examples stand. **FLNCB, a common stock listed the same day over the same 95-day window, beats the −3x SOXSB on both range width `3.044x` and turnover `2.197x`** — inverse triple leverage is no guarantee of being the most violent instrument. **And "this channel prefers leverage" does not hold either**: on semiconductors the 3X token is `15.37 times` the plain index token, while on the Nasdaq the plain index token is `8.46 times` the 3X. The two lines point opposite ways.
