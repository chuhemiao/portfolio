## TL;DR

**Robinhood is the most profitable company in this category, the least invested in it, and the only one seriously fixing the rights problem.** All three are true at once, and each explains the others.

Its stock tokens total **$151M**. Against a company posting **$1,308M** of quarterly revenue and **$561M** of net income, that is a rounding error — **less than a thousandth of the business.**

Yet it publicly plans **1:1 redemption into real shares, plus voting rights**. If that ships, it becomes the first large issuer to hand shareholder rights back to holders.

**My read: at today's size it does not warrant attention; for what it is building, it carries the highest option value in the category.** After the 2026 regulatory changes, "identical rights" stopped being a moral bonus and became the hard gate on US liquidity.

---

## What it actually is today

The issuer is **Robinhood Assets (Jersey) Limited**, and the instruments are **tokenized debt securities** — not shares. Same family as xStocks' tracker certificate and Ondo's issuer claim: you hold a claim against the issuer, not ownership of the company.

- Chain: **Robinhood Chain** (ERC-20)
- Instruments: **190+**
- Size: **$151M** (2026-10-16)
- US persons: **blocked** (EU entity)
- Distribution: the Robinhood app

It also runs **2,000+ CFD-style products in the EU that cannot be withdrawn on-chain**. Those are not the tokenized stocks discussed here, and the distinction matters: **a token you can move to self-custody and a CFD that lives inside an app are different instruments entirely** — the first is composable, the second is a brokerage product.

## It is the fastest grower in the field

An easily missed fact:

| Date | Robinhood stock tokens |
|---|---:|
| 2026-09-04 | **$39.1M** |
| 2026-10-16 | **$151M** |

**Nearly four times in 30 days.** Over the same window the market went from $2.77B to $3.5B (about +26%), and Ondo from $858.6M to $984M (about +15%).

**Robinhood is the fastest-growing issuer in the category**, and it has barely tried. Which says something: **when distribution is strong enough, a small supply-side effort moves real size.** Its main app flow is not connected at all yet.

## The real story: 1:1 redemption and voting

This is the heart of it.

Robinhood plans to support **redemption into real shares** and to **add voting rights**.

Why that matters far more than $151M:

Two 2026 rules changed the game:

- **SEC Innovation Exemption (2026-09-17)**: qualifying tokenized securities venues may trade tokenized NMS stock through permissioned AMM pools without registering as an exchange or ATS
- **Nasdaq same-order-book rule approved**: qualifying tokenized stocks and ETFs may trade in the same order book, at the same execution priority, as the traditional share — **provided the two are fungible and carry identical rights**

**Those last three words shut the entire offshore route out.** A tracker certificate does not, by definition, carry the same rights as the share; an issuer claim carries even fewer. No matter how large xStocks, Ondo or bStocks grow, their structures cannot earn that ticket.

If Robinhood actually ships a version redeemable 1:1 into real shares and carrying votes, **it moves from the offshore-wrapper race into the onshore-licensed one** — alongside Dinari, Securitize and Superstate, but carrying Robinhood's user base.

A caveat: **the reported five-year term and equal-rights conditions attached to the Innovation Exemption are secondary readings and should be checked against the SEC order itself.** Detail at that level decides who gets through.

## Two different routes back to rights

Only two issuers are working this seriously, and their methods differ completely:

| | Robinhood | Ondo |
|---|---|---|
| Route | **1:1 redemption into shares + voting** | **Broadridge proxy voting** |
| Nature | Changes the token's legal character | Layers proxy machinery on an existing claim |
| Difficulty | High — requires restructuring issuance | Medium — integrate infrastructure |
| Ceiling | Could satisfy "identical rights" | Proxy voting is not direct holding |

**Robinhood's path is harder with a higher ceiling.** Ondo's Broadridge route passes votes through while leaving "token ≠ share" intact; Robinhood's redemption attacks that premise directly.

**Whichever ships first may reset the pass mark for the category.**

**It sits at B for the same reason as bStocks and Dinari: not a weak structure, but insufficient verifiable information.** The difference is that their gaps are "not disclosed" while Robinhood's is "not built yet".

## What I will watch

1. **When the redemption terms are published** — minimum, KYC scope and settlement time decide whether it is a real channel or another $5,000 gate
2. **How voting is implemented** — direct registered holding, or a proxy arrangement like Ondo's
3. **Whether it appears on the first TSV approval list** — the line between a plan and a channel
4. **When main app flow is connected** — $151M came with almost no effort; connected, it is another order of magnitude
5. **When a US entity launches** — an EU entity today; moving onshore is what reaches the largest market

---

**Bottom line.** On today's numbers Robinhood is a placeholder rather than a competitor: $151M is a rounding error for it, and 190+ instruments is unremarkable. **But it is the only large player attacking the premise that a token is not a share — exactly as the 2026 regulatory changes turned that premise into the entry requirement.**

I would not follow it for its current size. **I follow it because if 1:1 redemption and voting ship, the size advantage held by the three offshore wrappers stops mattering overnight — their structures can never reach US liquidity.**
