## TL;DR

**This is the most heavily funded theme on the book, and almost all of the attention inside it sits on one name.**

- **SNDKB did `$97.80M` in 24 hours, the largest of all 100 instruments on the book**, while ranking only 6th by market cap (`$76.98M`). It is `11.20%` of the whole book volume on its own.
- **The five tokens did `$147.51M` together and SNDKB is `66.3%` of that.** The other four add up to `$49.71M`, so SNDKB alone is `97%` more than their combined total.
- **Remove SNDKB and the group stops being unusual.** Group turnover is `0.7723x`; without SNDKB it is `0.4360x`, below the `0.6625x` of the whole book. **The gap between this group holding 14.5% of the value and doing 16.9% of the volume comes entirely from one instrument.**
- **The thematic ETF is the coldest large instrument on the book.** DRAMB holds `$19.64M`, 14th by market cap, and turns over `0.186x`, 96th. SNDKB turns `6.83 times` faster, does `26.9 times` the volume, and is only `3.9 times` larger.
- **But "single names trade more than baskets" fails once market cap is held constant**: AVGOB is a single stock worth `$17.80M` — almost exactly DRAMB size — and turns over `0.224x` against DRAMB `0.186x`. **The real regularity is that large positions turn slowly, not that baskets do.**
- **Four of the five fell further than the book median over 30 days** (`−35.29%` / `−20.60%` / `−21.49%` / `−20.43%`); only MUB at `−16.31%` did slightly better than the `−16.71%` median. **This theme fell harder than the book this month.**

## 3. The thematic ETF is the coldest large instrument on the book

DRAMB is something this snapshot rarely offers: an ETF covering the exact industry the other members sit in, wrapped as a token by the same issuer. Its `$19.64M` makes it the **14th** largest instrument on the book — slightly under TSLAB (`$23.68M`), slightly over SPYB (`$19.08M`), squarely in the upper middle of this snapshot.

Its turnover of `0.186x` ranks **96th**. The five lowest turnovers on the book are DRAMB (`0.186x`), CRMB (`0.183x`), GMEB (`0.160x`), COHRB (`0.141x`) and AMATB (`0.130x`) — **the other four hold `$501.9K`, `$6.61M`, `$8.44M` and `$9.02M`, so DRAMB is 2.2 times the largest of them.**

## 4. The counter-example: hold market cap constant and the single-name story collapses

That 6.83-fold gap reads easily as "this channel buys names, not themes". **Two findings in this snapshot contradict it directly.**

**Counter-example one: AVGOB is a single stock, almost exactly DRAMB size, and turns over almost exactly as slowly.**

| Token | Kind | Market cap | 24h volume | Turnover | Book turnover rank |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| [AVGOB](/en/stocks/bstocks/AVGOB) | common stock | $17.80M | $3.98M | 0.224x | 92 |
| [DRAMB](/en/stocks/bstocks/DRAMB) | ETF | $19.64M | $3.64M | 0.186x | 96 |

Broadcom is the second largest chip designer in the world and an unambiguous single name. **Market caps differ by 10%, turnover by 20%, and both sit at the bottom of the book.** If "nobody wants baskets" explains DRAMB at `0.186x`, it does not explain AVGOB at `0.224x`.

Going further down: among the ten lowest turnovers on the book, five hold more than `$5M` — AMATB (`$9.02M` / `0.130x`), COHRB (`$8.44M` / `0.141x`), GMEB (`$6.61M` / `0.160x`), DRAMB (`$19.64M` / `0.186x`) and AVGOB (`$17.80M` / `0.224x`). **Four of the five are common stocks.** The actual regularity is stated in the issuer note itself: **the five largest instruments on the book turn over `0.38x` against `0.92x` for the other 95** — large positions turn slowly, regardless of wrapper.

**Counter-example two: WDCB does less absolute volume than DRAMB.**

These two listed on the same day (2026-07-06), an identical 117-day window:

| Token | Kind | Market cap | 24h volume | Turnover | Range width |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| [WDCB](/en/stocks/bstocks/WDCB) | common stock | $2.63M | **$2.33M** | 0.884x | 2.005x |

**WDCB turns over 4.75 times faster than DRAMB and still does 36% less absolute volume.** Western Digital is one of the four single names on this line, a storage company with revenue in the tens of billions — and its token here holds `$2.63M`, 48th on the book. **For WDCB, "single names trade more than baskets" holds as a ratio and fails as an amount.**

So the claim has to be narrowed to something that survives: **not "names beat baskets", but "the attention in this theme sits on one name".** Outside SNDKB, MUB turns over `0.415x`, below the book average; DRAMB at `0.186x` is near the book minimum; and WDCB cannot even match DRAMB on volume. Only SanDisk is traded repeatedly.

## 7. Structure: what these five tokens legally are

All of this comes from the issuer record on this site, the same data behind [/stocks/bstocks](/en/stocks/bstocks):

- **Legal wrapper**: tracker certificate, ADGM / FSRA listed, issued by BTech Holdings Limited. The holder gets a certificate that tracks a price, not a share registration.
- **Voting**: none.
- **Dividends**: pass-through, but **a 30% US withholding deduction comes first, and the remainder is reinvested through a multiplier**.
- **Redemption**: available, KYC required, `$5` minimum.
- **Proof of reserve**: attestation, not a live on-chain proof.
- **US persons**: blocked.
- **Chain**: BNB Chain only. BSC deposits only, so a wrong-network transfer loses the asset.
- **Issuance model**: exchange self-issued; the only venue is Binance, operated by the issuer.
- **Grade on this site**: B.

Two of those matter extra for this group.

**DRAMB is a wrapper on top of a wrapper.** Roundhill Memory ETF is itself a fund, and the fund holds the stocks; the certificate tracks the fund price; the token represents the certificate. **Three nested layers, with the costs of the middle two borne by the token holder** — the ETF management fee comes out of fund NAV, and the certificate layer cost structure is not in this data. The four single-name tokens have two layers.

**"Dividends pass through" means something different for each of these five.** Some of the makers pay a dividend, so the "30% withholding then reinvest through a multiplier" rule genuinely applies to them; DRAMB tracks an ETF, which receives distributions from its constituents first and then decides what to pass to its own shareholders, **so the token layer receives something that has already been processed twice.** The current multiplier is not in this data — see section 9.

## 8. This snapshot was taken on a Saturday

The timestamp `2026-10-31T06:06:52Z` is a Saturday morning UTC. US cash equities closed on Friday at 20:00 UTC and do not reopen until Monday — **for more than two days after this snapshot, these token prices are the only prices these names have.**

There is an extra layer for this group: **two of the five underlyings do not trade in the United States at all.** SK Hynix is listed in Korea, and Seoul hours do not overlap US hours; the ETF behind DRAMB trades in the United States, but its constituents span US, Korean and Japanese markets. **"Closed" is not the same moment for all five of these tokens.**

This has already been quantified once on the same issuer: [one stock, four books](/en/blog/aapl-four-books-tokenized-equity-liquidity-en) lays the four tokenized versions of AAPL side by side on a Saturday and concludes that **a quote outside trading hours is a market maker opinion, not a market price**. The 24-hour window in this piece runs from Friday 06:06 UTC to Saturday 06:06 UTC and **contains one complete US session**, so it is not a pure closed-market quote; but part of that `$97.80M` in section 2 fell in hours when US equities were already shut.

## 9. What this method cannot compute

**First, no premium or discount.** The snapshot holds only the token price, with no reference price for the underlying stock or ETF. More to the point, the bStocks dividend mechanism is "deduct 30% US withholding, then reinvest through a multiplier" — **without knowing the current multiplier, the token unit price and the underlying share price are not comparable in the first place.** So no premium figure appears here.

**Second, no shares per token.** The snapshot carries no conversion ratio. SNDKB has 66,641 tokens outstanding at `$1155.20` each, and neither number converts into a share count without that ratio — a token price far above any of these companies actual share prices says the ratio is not 1:1, but this data does not say what it is.

**Third, nothing about the fundamentals of this theme.** This snapshot holds no company financials and no memory spot prices. When this piece says "the memory line", it means **the trading behaviour of these five tokens in this channel**, not a position in the industry cycle. Any judgement about where a supercycle stands is outside what this data can support.

**Fourth, the range-width windows are unequal.** These five are 142/142/114/117/117 days. The book comparison uses only the 46 instruments listed 100 days or more, and even there the windows differ by more than a month — the longer something is listed, the more easily it reaches an extreme.

**Fifth, market cap is CMC's definition** (price times circulating supply), and the book total of `$1,318.38M` is the sum of these 100 rows; the bStocks row on [this site's /stocks page](/en/stocks) shows an on-chain size of `$914M` — **two figures from a different source and a different moment, which will not tie out.** This piece uses the former throughout.

**Sixth, 100 of 105.** CMC reports 105 instruments; the snapshot holds 100. The missing five appear in no figure here.

**Seventh, turnover is 24h volume divided by market cap.** A small denominator inflates the ratio mechanically, so high turnover in the tail is first of all a consequence of a small market cap and **must not be read as better liquidity in the tail** — the two counter-examples in section 4 are a direct consequence of that rule.

---

**Conclusion.** Memory and storage is the most heavily funded theme on bStocks: five tokens holding `14.49%` of book value and doing `16.89%` of its volume. But the gap between those two shares comes entirely from one instrument — **SNDKB did `$97.80M`, the largest 24h volume of all 100, 66.3% of this group, and 97% more than the other four combined.** Take it out and the remaining four turn over `0.4360x`, a third below the book `0.6625x`.

And "the single name trades 6.83 times faster than the thematic ETF" does not survive a control: **AVGOB is a single stock at almost identical market cap and turns `0.224x` against `0.186x`, both at the bottom of the book; WDCB is a single stock and does 36% less absolute volume than DRAMB.** The main explanatory variable for turnover is position size, not wrapper — and the issuer own data says the same thing: the five largest instruments turn `0.38x`, the other 95 turn `0.92x`.

The sentence to carry out: **this channel did not put money into a memory theme, it put money into the SanDisk name.** For the other four tokens, trading activity bears no relationship to their standing in the industry.
