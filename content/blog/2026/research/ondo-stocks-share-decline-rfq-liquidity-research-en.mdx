## TL;DR

**Ondo is still the largest issuer by on-chain value ($984M as of 2026-10-16), and it is losing the lead.** Its share fell from 58% in February 2026 to roughly a third — not because it shrank, but because the market doubled while it stood still.

The real problem is not the size table but turnover. **On the same Circle exposure, CRCLON turns over under 1% in a day; the bStocks version does 27% and the xStocks version 29%.** Three tokens tracking one company, about thirty times apart in activity.

**My read: Ondo's $984M looks like a custody balance rather than live money. It leads on compliance narrative and instrument coverage, and neither substitutes for distribution.**

---

## The legal structure: the token and the share are different assets

The issuer is **Ondo Global Markets**, registered in the **British Virgin Islands**, renamed Ondo Stocks in July 2026.

Official material describes the tokens as fully backed by the corresponding share or ETF. The Broadridge partnership announcement draws the boundary more clearly:

- **The Ondo token and the share or ETF held as collateral are different assets**
- Token holders **do not directly hold shareholder rights in the underlying**
- Some governance works by holders **expressing views to the issuer**, which is not the same as voting the share

That framing is considerably more honest than "1:1 backed", and closer to the truth. **"Fully backed" describes a collateral relationship, not an ownership one.**

Ondo's secondary-market terms spell out several conditions worth reading one by one:

- The issuer gives **no guarantee that any secondary market continues to exist**
- **No guarantee that any particular token has sufficient liquidity**
- Secondary market prices **need not equal the issue price**
- Redemption requires customer due diligence and KYC
- The issuer **may refuse redemption** where it suspects a transaction fails legal or internal control requirements
- Holder recourse **may be limited mainly to the relevant collateral and what the offering documents provide**

The second-to-last point is substantive. **"May refuse redemption" means redemption is a service, not a right.** Grading has to separate that from "has a redemption channel" — it is what pins Ondo at B rather than A.

## The share narrative: the company says 70%, the data says a third

This is the claim to be most careful with.

Ondo states publicly that it holds **over 70% share** among tokenized equity issuers. On CoinGecko's basis the real figure is roughly **a third**.

The timeline:

| Date | Ondo share |
|---|---:|
| 2026-02 | **58%** |
| 2026-03 | 53% |
| 2026-08 | 34.9% |
| 2026-09 | 31% |
| 2026-10 | about a third |

Over the same period the market went from roughly $1B to $3.5B (rwa.xyz basis), while Ondo moved from the $1B TVL it reported in June to $984M now — **the market more than tripled and it barely moved.**

**That 70% should be treated as a marketing figure and kept out of any research conclusion.** Numbers like it usually rest on a narrow definition of "issuer" — counting third-party wrappers only, excluding exchange-issued tokens. When you meet one, go back to a neutral source.

## What the RFQ route costs

None of this means Ondo lacks liquidity. Its flow runs through **RFQ and mint/redeem channels**, quoted by market makers when a user initiates, rather than sitting in DEX pools.

The design is defensible: RFQ often executes large orders better than a shallow AMM pool, and avoids impermanent loss and MEV. Jupiter's screener shows `RFQ` for some Ondo tokens instead of a fixed dollar liquidity figure.

But the cost is real:

1. **DeFi composability is close to zero** — a token not in a pool cannot be an AMM pair, and integrating it into lending markets is harder
2. **Off-hours quote quality is uncertain** — RFQ depends on a market maker choosing to quote; spreads and price impact can widen on weekends and while the underlying is shut
3. **Ordinary AMM metrics do not apply** — you need quote response time, fill rate and slippage at increasing order size, and none of that is public

**And "can sit in a pool, can serve as collateral" is the only differentiation tokenized equities have over an ordinary brokerage.** Coinbase's tokens total just $7.5M, yet they already trade on Uniswap and Aerodrome, work as Aave collateral and can be borrowed against on Morpho. On that axis $7.5M has more optionality than $984M.

## The only issuer seriously returning voting rights

Credit where it is due: Ondo is the only large issuer working the voting problem properly.

Its 2026 custodial version will support proxy voting through **Broadridge** — the incumbent proxy-voting infrastructure in traditional securities, not a crypto-native improvisation.

It is also expanding geographically, working with SBI to bring Japanese equities on-chain.

**If proxy voting actually ships, Ondo's legal-structure grade needs redoing.** Robinhood has published a plan for 1:1 redemption plus voting; Ondo is taking the Broadridge proxy route. Whichever lands first may reset the pass mark for the whole category.

## A size figure that is easy to misread

Ondo's protocol TVL is around $3.56B, but **the overwhelming majority sits in Treasury products like USDY and OUSG, not stock tokens.** The equities line is currently a strategic loss leader bought with share.

By the same logic, comparing Securitize's $3.88B TVL to Ondo on "stock TVL" is a mismatch — that figure is mostly funds such as BUIDL. **Lock the methodology before comparing, or you end up counting a Treasury fund as an equities moat.**

The ONDO token itself: $0.368, around $1.79B market cap, roughly $103M of 24h volume. The market is pricing an RWA platform, not a category leader's premium.

## What I will watch

1. **Whether absolute TVL recovers above $1B** — below $800M confirms the lead has changed hands
2. **When Broadridge proxy voting is genuinely usable** — the only lever that re-rates its legal structure
3. **Whether CRCLON turnover escapes <1%** — if it stays there, "leader" means a custody ranking
4. **Whether it starts seeding DEX liquidity** — abandoning the pure RFQ route would be a clear strategic turn
5. **Whether more venues follow Bitget in delisting Ondo tokens** — losing distribution is more dangerous than losing size

---

**Bottom line.** Ondo leads on regulatory path, instrument coverage and the voting-rights question, and those are real long-term assets. But **a fall from 58% to a third says one thing plainly: asset coverage and a compliance narrative do not substitute for distribution.** The balance is there; the money is not moving through it.

For now I grade it B: issuer and underlying are clear, but redemption is gated by KYC and issuer discretion, and secondary liquidity is the weakest among the major wrappers. **If Broadridge proxy voting ships, that grade needs rebuilding. Until then, first by size is not first by quality.**
