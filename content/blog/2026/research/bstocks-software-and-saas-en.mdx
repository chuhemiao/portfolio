## TL;DR

**These five are negligible in size on this book and yet the least damaged on it — and the reason is not the software sector, it is that none of them carries leverage.**

- **The five hold `$10.68M` between them, 0.8% of the book, and do `$7.30M` of volume, also 0.8%.** SPYB alone (`$19.08M`) is 1.79 times their combined size.
- **All five rank in the best 27 of 100 on 30-day change**: ADBEB `+4.39%` (11th), CRMB `−1.12%` (19th), PLTRB `−2.02%` (22nd), CRWDB `−5.92%` (26th), ORCLB `−6.52%` (27th). The group median of `−2.02%` is 14.69 points better than the book median of `−16.71%`.
- **Turnover, however, is exactly average**: `0.6832x` for the group, `0.6624x` for the other 95, `0.6625x` for the book. **So this is not "nobody trades it", it is "nobody parks capital in it" — two different things.**
- **The counter-example is in the listing cohort.** ORCLB has the best 30-day change of the ten names listed its day, but six of those ten are leveraged ETFs, down between `22.36%` and `78.03%`. **Topping that cohort says mostly that it is unleveraged.**
- **Of the 18 instruments up over 30 days, the top two are −3x inverse ETFs** (SOXSB `+70.82%`, SQQQB `+24.94%`), and this group places exactly one (ADBEB, 11th). **What actually made money this month was short exposure; software merely avoided the hit.**
- **Turnover inside the group spans 12.7x**: CRWDB `2.322x` (15th on the book) against CRMB `0.183x` (97th). CRMB does `$91.8K` a day, second-lowest on the book.
- **This snapshot cannot produce a premium.** No underlying reference price, no dividend-reinvestment multiplier.

## 5. Turnover is average; market cap is not

**The easiest misreading of this group is treating "small market cap" as "thinly traded".** On this book they are not the same thing:

| Group | Market cap | Volume | Turnover |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| These five | $10.68M (0.8%) | $7.30M (0.8%) | **0.6832x** |
| The other 95 | $1,307.70M | $866.17M | 0.6624x |
| Whole book | $1,318.38M | $873.46M | 0.6625x |

**The ratio between this group and the other 95 is 0.97 — no difference at all.** Market-cap share is 0.8% and volume share is 0.8%; the two columns line up exactly. These five are not sitting untouched, they are traded normally for their size.

Inside the group, though, the spread is enormous:

Half of the `2.322x` at CRWDB is the `$727.1K` market cap underneath it, **so do not read it as "CRWDB is the most liquid name in this group"** — the largest absolute volume belongs to ORCLB at `$2.63M`, and its turnover ranks only 74th on the book.

## 6. Structure: what these five tokens actually are

All of it from the issuer record on this site, none of it invented:

- **The wrapper is a tracker certificate**, issued by BTech Holdings Limited as an ADGM / FSRA listed certificate — **not equity**.
- **No voting rights.**
- **Dividends are pass-through: a 30% US withholding deduction first, then reinvested through a multiplier.** This is what drives the first point in section 8.
- **Reserves are covered by attestation, not an on-chain proof of reserve.** Among peers, xStocks uses Chainlink proof of reserve, updated daily or on a 10% move, verifiable on chain; an attestation is not.
- **Redemption is available, requires KYC, minimum $5.** (Primary redemption at xStocks has a $5,000 minimum.)
- **US persons are blocked.**
- **BNB Chain only, Binance only, BSC deposits only — a wrong-network transfer loses the asset.**
- **The issuance model is exchange-self**: the issuer and the only trading venue are the same entity.

**This is the structurally most uniform of these groups: all five underlyings are US-listed common shares — no ETF, no ADR, no leveraged product, no private company.** That is worth stating on its own, because it is exactly where the conclusion of section 4 comes from: **half of this group holding up is a structural property, not a sector property.** The book holds 67 common stocks, 10 leveraged ETFs, 8 unclassified, 6 ADRs, 6 ETFs, 2 inverse ETFs and 1 private company, and all five of these fall in the first bucket.

**No voting rights has a very concrete meaning for these particular underlyings.** Governance fights at these five companies — dilution, dual-class structures, takeover offers — get settled by shareholder votes in public markets, **and these tokens appear in none of them.** That is not an accusation, it is the definition of the wrapper.

## 7. This snapshot was taken on a Saturday

The timestamp `2026-10-31T06:06:52Z` is a Saturday morning UTC. The US cash market closed at 20:00 UTC on Friday and does not open again until Monday.

This has already been quantified once on the same issuer: [one stock, four order books](/en/blog/aapl-four-books-tokenized-equity-liquidity-en) lined up four tokenized versions of AAPL on a Saturday and concluded that **a quote outside trading hours is a market maker opinion, not a market price**.

So read the 24-hour changes accordingly: that window (Friday 06:06 UTC to Saturday 06:06 UTC) **contains a full Friday US session**, so it is not a pure closed-market quote; but the snapshot itself lands inside the closure, and **for the two-plus days until Monday, these token prices are the only prices these names have.**

**This matters more than usual for this group**, because all five 24-hour changes are small (`+0.15%` to `−4.47%`). **On an instrument doing `$91.8K` a day, "barely moved" and "barely quoted" look identical in the data.** Read the `−0.79%` at CRMB with that in mind.

## 8. What this method cannot compute

**First, no premium or discount.** The snapshot holds only the price of the token and **no reference price for the underlying**. More decisively, dividends take a 30% US withholding deduction and are then reinvested through a multiplier — **without the current multiplier, token unit price and share price are not comparable in the first place.** No premium figure appears anywhere here.

**Second, no shares per token.** The snapshot carries no conversion ratio, so circulating supply converts into no claim at all.

**Third, the windows behind range width are not equal.** These five run 121 / 103 / 60 / 53 / 32 days; the shortest on the book is 5 days. Every book-wide width median quoted here is restricted to the 46 instruments listed 100 days or more — **only ORCLB and PLTRB qualify** (10th and 27th narrowest of the 46), and the widths of the other three (`1.422x` / `1.195x` / `1.186x`) cannot be compared against mature instruments.

**Fourth, for an instrument listed under 30 days, the "30-day" field does not cover 30 days.** Fifteen instruments on the book have been listed under 30 days and 11 under 20. The proof: APPB has been listed 5 days and its 7-day and 30-day fields are identical (`−16.11%` both), so the field is clipped to available history. **All five here have at least 32 days, so their own 30-day figures are genuine 30 days; but the book median of `−16.71%` and every ranking quoted here include those 15 clipped values** — five of which (LLYB, JPMB, BRKBB, UVXYB, TMFB) are positive and crowd the head of the ranking in section 3.

**Fifth, market cap is the CMC definition**: price times circulating supply. The book total of `$1,318.38M` here is the sum of these 100 rows, while the bStocks row on [the /stocks page of this site](/en/stocks) shows an on-chain size of `$914M` — **two numbers from different sources at different times, and they do not reconcile.**

**Sixth, 100 of 105.** CMC reports 105 instruments; this snapshot holds 100. The missing 5 are in none of these figures.

**Seventh, one chain.** All 100 exist only on BNB Chain — no cross-chain liquidity fragmentation, **and no redundancy either.**

---

**Conclusion.** These five enterprise-software instruments hold `$10.68M` on bStocks, 0.8% of the book and 44% smaller than one S&P 500 ETF; at the same time all five rank in the best 27 on 30-day change, with a median of `−2.02%` against the book median of `−16.71%`, 14.69 points better. Both facts are true, **but the second one has little to do with software.**

The evidence is the ORCLB cohort: it ranks first of ten, and six of those ten are leveraged ETFs down between `22.36%` and `78.03%`, while the two other unleveraged common stocks in the same cohort, AXTIB and CRWVB, fell `31.51%` and `38.18%`. **So the conclusion worth keeping is narrower: what this group shares is not an industry but a shape — a common-stock underlying, no leverage, and an underlying outside the eye of this particular storm.** The instruments that actually made money this month were two −3x inverse ETFs, which have nothing to do with software and nothing to do with falling less.

The third point is turnover. **The group at `0.6832x` is indistinguishable from the other 95 at `0.6624x`, and its market-cap share and volume share are both 0.8%.** So do not read "small market cap" as "thinly traded": these five are traded normally for their size, and the spread inside the group — CRWDB `2.322x` against CRMB `0.183x`, a factor of 12.7 — is far larger than any gap between the group and the book.
