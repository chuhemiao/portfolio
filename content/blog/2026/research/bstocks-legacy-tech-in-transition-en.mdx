## TL;DR

**The clearest thing in this group is a fact about the metric: turnover has no sign.**

- **INTCB turns over at `1.751x`, the highest among the ten largest instruments on the book** (2nd is SNDKB at `1.270x`), while its 30-day figure is `−47.60%`, it sits `−54.4%` below its since-listing high, and it is pinned at `2.0%` of its range.
- **IBMB, in the same group, turns over at `1.265x`, only 1.38x less than INTCB, and its 30-day figure is `+16.51%`** — 5th-best on the book and 2nd-best common stock, behind only LLYB at `+21.31%` — with an in-range position of `86.1%`, the 3rd-highest on the book, and only `−3.5%` from its high.
- **Two instruments in the same turnover band, 64.11 percentage points apart over 30 days.** So a high turnover number is neither attention nor exit: **it carries no sign.**
- **A second computable finding: one underlying, Intel, accounts for 14.1% of the book's volume.** INTCB's `$67.90M` is the 3rd-largest volume on the book and INTWB's `$55.54M` is the 4th; together they are `$123.43M`, while their combined market cap of `$40.22M` is only `3.05%` of the book.
- **The cleanest counter-example is AMDB.** Same listing day (2026-06-21), identical 132-day window, also a common stock, same industry — **width `1.565x` against INTCB's `2.245x`, so INTCB is 43.5% wider**, 30 days `−18.88%` against `−47.60%`, turnover `0.431x` against `1.751x`. **Those numbers belong to Intel itself, not to legacy tech and not to semiconductors.**
- **Group turnover of `1.6271x` is 2.59x the other 95 instruments (`0.6277x`), but that is an INTCB figure** — INTCB alone is 84.5% of group market cap and 90.9% of group volume. Strip it and `0.9529x` remains, still above the other 95 but at 1.52x rather than 2.59x.
- **This snapshot cannot compute a premium.** There is no underlying reference price and no dividend reinvestment multiplier, **so this report gives no premium or discount figure.**

## 2. Turnover has no direction

The group laid out by outcome:

| Token | Days | Since-listing high | Low | Range width | From high | In range | 30d |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **INTCB** | 132 | $142.07 | $63.28 | **2.245x** | **−54.4%** | **2.0%** | **−47.60%** |
| NOKB | 110 | $12.05 | $7.77 | 1.550x | −29.3% | 17.4% | −17.11% |
| QCOMB | 117 | $205.03 | $142.68 | 1.437x | −19.6% | 35.4% | −12.24% |
| **IBMB** | 110 | $265.33 | $199.23 | 1.332x | **−3.5%** | **86.1%** | **+16.51%** |
| BBB | 19 | $9.63 | $7.74 | 1.244x | −12.5% | 36.2% | +0.02% |

**The first and fourth rows are this entire report.**

**INTCB's turnover of `1.751x` is the highest among the ten largest instruments by market cap.** That is worth stating precisely, because high turnover on this snapshot is usually a small-denominator artifact (turnover = 24h volume ÷ market cap, so a small denominator mechanically inflates the ratio). INTCB is not that: its `$38.78M` market cap ranks 9th on the book, and the turnover figures of the top ten are SPCXB `0.458x`, CRCLB `0.326x`, GOOGLB `0.291x`, MSTRB `0.443x`, QQQB `0.327x`, SNDKB `1.270x`, MUB `0.415x`, NVDAB `0.889x`, **INTCB `1.751x`**, BNCB `0.432x`. **A top-ten instrument turning over 1.75 times its own market cap in a day.**

**And the outcome at that turnover level is near the bottom of the book:** 30 days `−47.60%` ranks 94th of 100. The six worse are KORUB `−49.66%`, MVLLB `−51.66%`, SOXLB `−51.90%`, SHAZB `−58.17%`, SNXXB `−61.18%` and INTWB `−78.03%` — **five of which are leveraged products.** In other words, **among unlevered instruments only SHAZB has fallen further than INTCB.**

**IBMB, in the same group, turns over at `1.265x`, only 1.38x less than INTCB.** The two are in the same turnover band. IBMB's outcome:

- **30 days `+16.51%`, 5th on the book.** The four above it are SOXSB `+70.82%`, SQQQB `+24.94%`, LLYB `+21.31%` and UVXYB `+20.12%` — two inverse ETFs, a pharmaceutical stock and a volatility ETF. **So IBMB is the 2nd-best 30-day common stock on the book, behind only LLYB.**
- **An in-range position of `86.1%`, the 3rd-highest on the book** (behind LLYB `92.5%` and WENB `87.7%`), while only 18 instruments on the book sit above the midpoint of their own range at all.
- **Only `−3.5%` from its since-listing high.**

**The two are 1.38x apart on turnover and 64.11 percentage points apart over 30 days.** That is this report's thesis: **turnover is an unsigned quantity.** It states how much changed hands in a day; it does not state whether that money was building a position or leaving one. For direction you have to look at where the price landed — INTCB at `2.0%` of its range, IBMB at `86.1%`.

## 3. One underlying, Intel, is 14.1% of the book's volume

That report's point was the denominator — high turnover is first of all a small-market-cap result.

## 4. The counter-example: AMD, listed the same day, is 43.5% narrower

Every prior report in this series ran the same machine: **compare instruments listed on the same day, so the window is identical.** All five members of this group have same-day peers, so the machine runs at full coverage here.

**AMDB is INTCB's most direct control:** same listing day, same 132 days, also a common stock, same industry, and Intel's direct competitor in x86. The result — **width `1.565x` against `2.245x`, with INTCB 43.5% wider; 30 days `−18.88%` against `−47.60%`, 28.7 percentage points apart; turnover `0.431x` against `1.751x`, 4.1x apart.**

**So the three figures in section 2 belong to Intel itself, not to legacy tech in transition and not to semiconductors.** Another company in the same industry, over the same window, listed the same day, is ordinary on all three dimensions.

**The same batch also holds a counter-example in the other direction: MSTRB's `2.162x` is only 3.8% narrower than INTCB's.** A bitcoin-treasury stock and a legacy chipmaker land at nearly the same range width over an identical 132-day window. **So the claim that legacy tech turnarounds are unusually volatile has no signature** — it fails against AMD in the same industry, and it collides with an instrument of a completely different type.

## 6. Structure: five instruments, one wrapper

- **The wrapper is a tracker certificate**, not equity. Issuing entity BTech Holdings Limited, ADGM / FSRA listed certificates.
- **No voting rights.**
- **Dividends pass through**: a 30% US withholding deduction first, then reinvestment through a multiplier (see section 8).
- **Reserves are attested, not proven on chain.** For comparison, xStocks uses Chainlink proof of reserve — updated daily or when reserves move more than 10%, verifiable on chain. An attestation is not.
- **Redemption requires KYC**, with a $5 minimum.
- **US persons are blocked.**
- **Single chain.** BNB Chain only, BSC deposits only; sending on the wrong network loses the assets.

That is a structural description, not an accusation. It carries one specific implication for this group: **dividends.** Several of these underlyings are long-standing payers (IBM, Qualcomm and Nokia all have long dividend histories), **and a 30% US withholding deduction followed by reinvestment through an undisclosed multiplier means a token holder receives no cash dividend and cannot see the price at which their share was reinvested.** For a position held mainly for dividend return that is not a detail — **it is also the root cause of the no-premium limitation in section 8.**

## 7. This snapshot was taken on a Saturday

The timestamp `2026-10-31T06:06:52Z` is a Saturday morning UTC. The US cash market closed Friday at 20:00 UTC and does not reopen until Monday. **Four of this group are US-listed common stocks and the fifth is an ADR, which also trades in the US session — so at the instant of this snapshot not one of the five reference prices exists.**

This has already been quantified once on the same issuer: [one stock, four books](/en/blog/aapl-four-books-tokenized-equity-liquidity-en) laid AAPL's four tokenized versions side by side on a Saturday and concluded that **a quote outside trading hours is a market maker's opinion, not a market price.**

The 24-hour change window used here (Friday 06:06 UTC to Saturday 06:06 UTC) **contains a full Friday US session**, so it is not purely a closed-market quote; but the snapshot itself lands inside the closure, **and for the two-plus days until Monday these token prices are the only prices these names have.**

One extra layer on section 3's figures: **the `$123.43M` traded across INTCB and INTWB happened in a window where an underlying reference price existed and then stopped existing.** This report cannot split that volume into a session half and a closed half — the snapshot holds only a 24-hour aggregate, with no tick data.

## 8. What this method cannot compute

**First, no premium or discount.** The snapshot holds only the token's own price and **no underlying reference price**. More importantly, the bStocks dividend mechanism is a 30% US withholding deduction followed by reinvestment through a multiplier — **without knowing the current multiplier, token unit price and share price are not comparable quantities.** That matters especially here, because several of these underlyings are long-standing dividend payers. So this report gives no premium figure; it is not computable, not forgotten.

**Second, no shares per token.** The snapshot holds no conversion ratio. IBMB's unit price of `$256.15` cannot be set against any share price directly.

**Third, BBB has been listed only 19 days, so its 30-day field covers its entire history.** Its `+0.02%` cannot sit alongside the other four 30-day figures, and its `1.244x` width is a short-window artifact. This report uses it only once, in section 4, against PENGB from the same listing day, and states there that the field name and the actual window disagree.

**Fourth, the range-width windows are unequal.** This group runs 19 / 110 / 110 / 117 / 132 days. Book-wide width comparisons are restricted to the 46 instruments listed 100 days or more — **four of this group are in that 46** (INTCB 35th, QCOMB 9th, NOKB 17th, IBMB 5th) and BBB is not. Even within those 46 the windows differ by more than a month.

**Fifth, market cap is the CMC definition**: price × circulating supply. The book total of `$1,318.38M` here is the sum of these 100 rows, while [this site's /stocks page](/en/stocks) shows bStocks at `$914M` of on-chain value — **two figures from different sources at different moments, and they will not reconcile.** This report uses the former throughout and states the timestamp everywhere.

**Sixth, 100 of 105.** CMC reports 105 instruments; the snapshot in hand holds 100. The missing 5 are in no statistic here.

**Seventh, legacy tech in transition is a layer this report drew itself.** The snapshot has no such field, and section 4 already supplies the disproof: Corning (GLWB, founded 1851) qualifies under the same standard and is wider than QCOMB, while a TSMC ADR (TSMB) does not qualify and is narrower than IBMB. **Drawn by someone else, the numbers in sections 2 and 4 would change.**

**Eighth, that 14.1% in section 3 is a single-day snapshot.** It is the aggregate of the 24 hours before 2026-10-31T06:06:52Z, **not an average over any period.** Intel carried a seventh of this book's volume on that day; this report cannot say whether that is normal or particular to the day, because the snapshot holds no historical series.

---

**Conclusion.** The most defensible finding here is about the metric itself: **turnover has no direction.** INTCB's `1.751x` is the highest among the ten largest instruments on the book, and it sits at `2.0%` of its range with a 30-day figure of `−47.60%`; IBMB, in the same group, turns over only 1.38x less and sits at `86.1%` of its range with `+16.51%`, the 2nd-best 30-day common stock on the book. **The two are 64.11 percentage points apart. A high turnover figure, by itself, does not tell you whether money is arriving or leaving — for direction, look at where the price sits in its range.**

**The second computable point: one underlying, Intel, accounts for 14.1% of this book's volume on 3.05% of its market cap.** The 3rd- and 4th-largest volumes on the book are the spot and 2x tokens of the same name.

**And the claim that legacy tech turnarounds are unusually volatile is rejected directly by AMD, listed the same day:** over the same 132-day window, in the same industry, also a common stock, **AMDB is 43.5% narrower than INTCB, 28.7 percentage points better over 30 days, and turns over 4.1x less.** Those three extreme figures belong to Intel itself.
