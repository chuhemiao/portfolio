## TL;DR

**The roadshow put two numbers side by side. At the midpoint of the price range, Anthropic's implied equity value is about `$968.4B`; its disclosed compute commitments are about `$900B`. The first is 35% to 52% below the valuations that circulated in October; the second is `$150B` above the widest figure compiled then.**

- **Valuation range `$916.1B` to `$1,020.8B`.** Price range `$70–78`, about `13.087B` shares after the offering. The midpoint is `35.4%` below the `$1.5T` the October piece used as a hypothesis and `51.6%` below `$2T`.
- **The multiple more than doubles depending on which revenue you divide by.** The midpoint is `12.0x` the October run-rate of `$81.0B`, `14.2x` Q3 annualized revenue of `$68.0B`, and `27.4x` trailing-twelve-month revenue of `$35.28B`.
- **Net loss `$104.04B`, operating loss only `$1.26B`.** About `$94.3B` of the gap is fair-value remeasurement of convertible notes, derivative liabilities and SAFEs, not operating cash burn; operating cash flow for the first nine months was `+$6.6B`.
- **Compute efficiency slipped in Q3.** Revenue over compute expense fell from `2.08` in Q2 to `1.72`. Using the roughly `$11.6B` Q2 revenue cited in the October piece, compute expense rose `77.2%` quarter over quarter against revenue growth of `46.6%`.
- **The next twelve months of commitments come to `$70.7B`, about `1.79` times Q3 annualized compute expense.** Holding the Q3 revenue-to-compute ratio on that spend would take about `$121.6B` of revenue, `50%` above the October run-rate.
- **Three labels in the draft do not reconcile**: growth multiples mix two conventions; compute commitments appear as both `$900B` and `$900.9B`; and the roughly `$5B` of short-term compute cannot all have landed in Q3.

> This piece is built on an external draft. The draft says it relies on the second amended S-1 of November 10, the IPO fact sheet, the founders letter and five customer case studies. The snapshot is `2026-11-11`; the final price is expected on November 18 with trading on the 19th, and the registration statement was not yet effective as of November 10. Figures are kept as the draft states them and the SEC filing was not reopened for this piece. Everything computable was rechecked; the comparisons and derivations in sections 2, 5, 9 and 10 are mine.

## 1. Offering terms: four different numbers answer "how much"

| Item | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Price range | `$70–78` | Midpoint `$74`; the final price may change |
| Base offering | `825M` shares | `801.35M` new plus `23.649M` secondary |
| Over-allotment option | up to `123.75M` shares | `15%` of the base; excluded from the share count below |
| Base offering size | `$57.75B` to `$64.35B` | Transaction value, not market value |
| New-share proceeds (midpoint) | about `$59.30B` | Before fees; about `$1.75B` of secondary goes to selling holders |
| Concurrent Class N placement | `$26B` | Strategic investors, generally non-voting |
| Expected net proceeds | about `$84.6B` | Gross `$85.30B`; the roughly `$0.7B` gap is fees |
| Common shares after offering and placement | about `13.087B` | The share count used for valuation |

Listing on Nasdaq under `ANT`. Timeline: first S-1 on October 26, first amendment on November 2, second amendment and roadshow launch on November 10.

**Transaction value, proceeds and market value are three different things.** `$57.75B` to `$64.35B` is what the shares sold in this deal are worth; what reaches the company is the new shares plus the placement, net of fees, about `$84.6B`; market value multiplies the full share count, covered in the next section.

## 2. What changed since the October piece

The [October 26 piece](/en/blog/anthropic-ipo-s1-ai-capital-structure-valuation-research-en) was written the day the first S-1 was filed. There was no price range then, so valuation could only be hypothesized from press figures. The amendment two weeks later pinned several of those numbers down:

| Item | October piece (press / draft basis) | November 10 amendment (this draft) | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Valuation | `$1.5T`–`$2T` (hypothesis) | `$916.1B`–`$1,020.8B` | Midpoint `35.4%`–`51.6%` lower |
| Compute commitments | "more than `$500B`", up to about `$750B` including deals in negotiation | about `$900B` | `$150B` above the old upper bound |
| 2026 Q3 revenue | about `$16.8B` (press report of investor communication) | `$17.0B` | Filed figure slightly higher |
| 2026 H1 revenue | `$16.29B` | derived `$16.242B` | `$0.048B` apart, within Q3 rounding |
| Net loss | H1 `$80.7B` | first nine months `$104.04B` | Q3 about `$23.34B` |
| GAAP operating loss | H1 `$0.693B` | first nine months `$1.26B` | Q3 derived `$0.567B`, `-3.3%` |
| Adjusted operating margin | H1 `+10%` | Q3 `+15%` | Still improving |

The interesting part is two changes moving in opposite directions: **the price the market will pay is well below October's talk, while the compute spend already signed is above October's widest figure.** Midpoint value `$968.4B`, commitments `$900B`; the latter is `92.9%` of the former.

That ratio has obvious basis problems — commitments span many years, carry conditions and termination rights, and are nominal rather than present value, while valuation is a one-time price on equity — so it does not mean the company has pledged its whole value to compute. But it shows the scale: **a large share of this company's cash flow for the next decade is already allocated to compute suppliers.**

Section 8's P/S table in the October piece is therefore superseded. That table had `22.3x` Q3 annualized revenue at `$1.5T`; on the same basis (Q3 annualized `$68.0B`) the midpoint is now `14.2x`. The qualitative analysis in its sections 3 and 7 still holds.

## 3. Revenue: recognized, run-rate and trailing twelve months are three numbers

| Period | Revenue | Note |
|---|---|---|
| FY2023 | `$41M` | |
| FY2024 | `$386M` | `9.4x` 2023 |
| FY2025 | `$4.585B` | `11.9x` 2024 |
| 2025 first nine months | `$2.545B` | |
| 2025 Q4 | `$2.04B` | Derived: full year minus nine months |
| 2026 first nine months | `$33.242B` | `13.1x` the prior-year period |
| 2026 Q3 | `$17.0B` | Single quarter |
| October 2026 | about `$81.0B` | Annualized run-rate, not realized revenue |

From about `$0.4B` in 2025 Q1 to `$17.0B` in 2026 Q3, quarterly revenue grew to `42.5` times its starting level.

The three measures have to be kept apart:

- **Trailing twelve months, realized**: 2025 Q4 `$2.04B` plus 2026 first nine months `$33.242B`, about `$35.28B`. This already happened.
- **Q3 annualized**: `$17.0B × 4 = $68.0B`. Assumes the Q3 level holds for a year.
- **October run-rate**: about `$81.0B`, `19.1%` above Q3 annualized. Assumes the latest month holds for a year.

The last two are extrapolations. In a period of fast growth they sit closer to the current scale of the business than the trailing figure, but they lean harder on growth not slowing.

## 4. Profit and cash flow: the two loss figures describe different things

First nine months of 2026:

| Item | Amount | Share of revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | `$33.242B` | |
| Compute and infrastructure | `$18.808B` | `56.6%` |
| Personnel | `$4.863B` | `14.6%` |
| Sales, marketing and partnerships | `$5.529B` | `16.6%` |
| Other operating and administrative | `$5.302B` | `15.9%` |
| Total operating expense | `$34.502B` | `103.8%` |
| GAAP operating loss | `-$1.26B` | `-3.8%` |
| GAAP net loss | `-$104.039B` | |
| Operating cash flow | `+$6.6B` | |

About `$94.3B` of the net loss comes from fair-value changes on convertible notes, derivative liabilities and SAFEs, `90.6%` of the total. The higher the company's valuation goes, the larger the book loss from remeasuring these instruments — **this part is the accounting shadow of a rising valuation, not cash consumed by operations.**

The adjusted number cannot be read alone either. Q3 GAAP operating margin was about `-3%`, adjusted `+15%`; the 18 percentage points of adjustments, stock compensation among them, are real costs that simply do not use cash. The prospectus also warns that as infrastructure spending such as Anthropic Direct expands, future operating and investing cash flow may come under pressure — **positive operating cash flow today does not show that free cash flow has settled.**

## 5. Compute: the efficiency ratio, short-term capacity and $900B of commitments

**Revenue over compute expense** was `0.40` in 2025 Q1, reached `2.08` in 2026 Q2 and fell back to `1.72` in Q3. The company credits the rise to algorithms, scheduling, a multi-chip fleet (AWS Trainium, Google TPU, NVIDIA GPU, AMD GPU) and infrastructure work, and attributes part of the Q3 decline to buying higher-priced short-term capacity.

This ratio is neither GPU utilization nor gross margin — training and inference compute share one denominator.

**Using the roughly `$11.6B` Q2 revenue cited in the October piece, quarterly compute expense can be split out:**

| | 2026 Q1 | 2026 Q2 | 2026 Q3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | about `$4.642B` | about `$11.6B` | `$17.0B` |
| Revenue / compute | about `1.39` (derived) | `2.08` | `1.72` |
| Compute expense | about `$3.347B` (derived) | about `$5.577B` | about `$9.884B` |

Q3 compute expense rose `77.2%` quarter over quarter, revenue `46.6%`. Q1 is backed out of the nine-month total of `$18.808B` by subtracting Q2 and Q3.

**The commitments**: about `$900B` in total, of which about `$451.6B` is expected by the end of 2030 and about `$70.7B` within the twelve months after September 30, 2026. Part of it is conditional or terminable.

Placing the `$70.7B` against the Q3 cost structure:

```text
Q3 annualized compute          $9.884B × 4    ≈  $39.54B
Next-12-month commitments      $70.7B         ≈  1.79x Q3 annualized
Hold the Q3 revenue/compute    $70.7B × 1.72  ≈  $121.6B of revenue
October run-rate               $81.0B          →  50% more needed
```

**This is a sensitivity, not a forecast.** It assumes the whole `$70.7B` is expensed over the next twelve months and the ratio stays at the Q3 `1.72`. If efficiency returns to the Q2 `2.08`, the same spend supports more revenue and the pressure eases a lot; if some commitments are capitalized facilities, the expense spreads over a longer period. But it frames the question: **over the next year, revenue has to grow half again beyond the October run-rate for the new compute not to compress margins.**

## 6. The strategic placement: suppliers are also shareholders

Alongside the IPO the company plans to sell `$26B` of Class N stock to Google, NVIDIA, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft and TSMC Partners. Class N is generally non-voting.

The seven sit across chips, memory, foundry and cloud — where the main recipients of the `$900B` in commitments are. **`$26B` is `2.9%` of the commitment total.** Suppliers taking a small equity stake in exchange for certainty on a large long-term order book is not unusual, but it raises three things to watch: whether procurement is priced at arm's length, supplier concentration, and money flowing from shareholders to the company and back to the same shareholders.

## 7. Customers and case studies: the edge moves from the model to the workflow

As of September 30, 2026:

| Metric | Size |
|---|---|
| Customers contributing at least `$100K` over the past 12 months | more than `11,900` |
| At least `$1M` | more than `1,900` |
| At least `$10M` | about `250` |
| Fortune 500 companies at the `$100K` threshold | more than `50%` |
| Global 2000 companies at that threshold | more than `30%` |
| Net revenue retention (NRR) | about `900%` |
| Q3 revenue outside the top five customers | about `79%` |

**A `900%` NRR cannot be read as a SaaS renewal rate.** It compares eligible customers' latest three months with the same three months a year earlier, and excludes fixed subscriptions such as Claude Pro, Max and Ultra. It says enterprise usage is expanding very fast; it does not say every existing customer will keep that pace.

Five case studies, with their numbers and the limits on reading them:

| Customer | Disclosed figures | Read with care |
|---|---|---|
| Legora (legal) | Over `1,500` law firms and legal teams; a 14-country, 31-institution study found lawyers save about `4.3` hours a week of non-billable work; first-pass review of `900` M&A diligence documents across four jurisdictions cut to half a day | Saved non-billable time does not necessarily become new revenue |
| Eli Lilly (life sciences) | Over 50,000 employees on Claude Enterprise, active use above `85%` in key functions; Claude Science pilot in October 2026 | Active use shows adoption, not output |
| Rakuten (multi-business) | Claude Code ran autonomous coding for over 7 hours, `99.9%` accuracy on some complex changes; new-feature cycle from 24 working days to 5 days | Neither the "some" sample nor the accuracy definition is disclosed |
| Salesforce (software) | Effective-output metric up `151%` year over year; 33 API migrations estimated at 231 person-days done in 13 calendar days | Person-days measure effort, calendar days measure elapsed time, team size unknown — **do not divide them into a multiple** |
| Walmart (retail) | Shopping agent Sparky weekly actives up over `110%` in a quarter; orders through Sparky carry about `35%` higher average value | Selection: shoppers who use an agent may already be higher-value; not causal |

The common direction is that **AI is moving from answering questions to completing tasks, and embedding into enterprises' existing permissions, audit and compliance systems.** Permissions, audit and human review — things outside the model — are becoming part of the competitive edge. Anthropic sells model, platform and applications at once, which is the advantage; the risk is that large customers build their own model routing across those layers and turn it into one replaceable supplier among several.

## 8. Governance and the founders letter: which decisions may depart from profit maximization

| Holder | Main rights |
|---|---|
| Founder LLC (Class F) | About `50.1%` of votes on general matters |
| Long-Term Benefit Trust (Class T) | Generally elects and removes a board majority |
| Class A (public offering) | One vote per share, within the structure above |
| Class N (strategic placement) | Generally non-voting |
| Class E (employees) | Votes on limited matters, no economic rights |

The founders letter makes two commitments that may cost near-term revenue: **no advertising in Claude**, and **possibly delaying releases when facing high-risk capabilities**, citing the restricted release of Claude Mythos Preview. The founders have each pledged `80%` of their personal Anthropic equity to charity, and the company is setting up a public-benefit fund equal to `5%` of its equity.

The letter sorts AI risk into four kinds: misuse, loss of control, authoritarian abuse, and economic disruption.

**For a Class A buyer both of these hold at once**: the special governance may keep long-term research and safety decisions from being held hostage to quarterly results, and public shareholders' influence on the board and strategy is genuinely limited. The governance structure itself belongs in the valuation — as a control discount, not just a statement of values.

## 9. Valuation: one price, 12x or 27x

| Assumed price | Implied equity value | On October run-rate `$81.0B` | On Q3 annualized `$68.0B` | On trailing twelve months `$35.28B` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `$70` | `$916.1B` | `11.3x` | `13.5x` | `26.0x` |
| `$74` | `$968.4B` | `12.0x` | `14.2x` | `27.4x` |
| `$78` | `$1,020.8B` | `12.6x` | `15.0x` | `28.9x` |

Excludes the over-allotment, future dilution, and the gap between equity value and enterprise value.

At the same price, realized revenue gives `27.4x` and the latest month extrapolated gives `12.0x`. **The more-than-double gap is what the market is paying for growth holding up.**

Three scenarios:

1. **Bull**: enterprise agent usage keeps climbing, model prices fall more slowly than unit costs, and operating margin widens.
2. **Middle**: revenue keeps growing but the pace normalizes, and the compute investment wave makes margins swing.
3. **Bear**: price competition cuts unit prices fast, long-term compute commitments go underused, and cash flow and valuation come under pressure together.

Current disclosures are not enough to assign probabilities to these.

## 10. Three places the draft does not reconcile

**1. Growth multiples mix two conventions.** The draft says 2024 "grew about 8.4 times" and 2025 "grew about 11.9 times". By the revenue figures, 2024 is `9.4x` 2023 (growth of `8.4x`) and 2025 is `11.9x` 2024 (growth of `10.9x`) — the first is stated as growth, the second as a multiple. The October piece says 2025 was "about `11.9` times 2024", which is right. Section 3 here uses "N times the prior year" throughout.

**2. Compute commitments are given twice.** Section 4.1 of the draft says "about `$900B`"; its final section says "`$900.9B`". Both may come from different places in the filing, but they cannot both be exact; this piece uses about `$900B`. A smaller one of the same kind: the midpoint valuation is `$968.5B` in the draft's section 4 and `$968.4B` in its section 9; `13.087B × $74 = $968.44B`, so the latter.

**3. The roughly `$5B` of short-term compute cannot all sit in Q3.** If it did, Q3 compute without it would be `$9.884B − $5B = $4.884B`, `12.4%` below Q2's roughly `$5.577B` — while revenue rose `46.6%` in the same quarter. That does not hold together. More likely the `$5B` is a multi-period contract amount or includes prepayment, with only part of it in Q3 expense. The draft calls it "related expense", which invites reading it as a single-quarter cost.

## 11. What this piece cannot compute

- **The SEC filing was not reopened.** All financial figures are as the draft states them; this round only rechecked arithmetic and consistency across documents.
- **The final price is not set.** Pricing is on November 18 and the share count may move with the over-allotment; every valuation here is a static calculation.
- **No present value or cancellable share for the commitments.** `$900B` is a nominal total; the draft does not say how much is conditional or terminable.
- **No breakdown of the adjustments.** The draft does not split the 18 points between `-3%` and `+15%`.
- **The quarterly compute split rests on the October piece for Q2 revenue.** About `$11.6B` comes from a press report of investor communication, not from this amendment; the Q1 `1.39` is derived from it.
- **The roadshow video and full slides were not reviewed.** The draft says that part was rebuilt from the official agenda and fact sheet.
- **The onchain pre-IPO products named after Anthropic are not `ANT` stock.** They carry no shareholder rights; see [this piece](/en/blog/anthropic-prestocks-anthropic-defi-infrastructure-token-value-capture-risk). The price range only gives them a reference anchor.

---

**Conclusion.** Anthropic has shown with realized revenue that frontier AI is a real and fast-growing market: `$33.242B` of revenue in nine months, positive operating cash flow, an `$81.0B` annualized run-rate. The roadshow price is also far more restrained than October's talk — `12.0x` run-rate at the midpoint.

But commercial success and return on capital are separate questions. About `$900B` of compute commitments is already close to the company's entire equity value; the next year's slice requires revenue half again above the October run-rate to avoid compressing margins; and the counterparties on those commitments are, through Class N, becoming shareholders too. **What sets long-term value is not how capable the models are, but whether cash flow can steadily outrun the compute bill already signed.** Even if AI creates enormous social value, a foundation-model company need not capture most of the economic surplus.
