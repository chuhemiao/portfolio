## TL;DR

**Coinbase's tokenized equities total about $7.5M — one one-hundred-and-thirtieth of Ondo, one one-hundred-and-twentieth of bStocks. On size it does not register.**

It is also **the only issuer whose tokens genuinely entered DeFi**: tradable on Uniswap and Aerodrome, usable as Aave collateral, borrowable against on Morpho.

**And "can sit in a pool, can serve as collateral" is the only differentiation tokenized equities have over an ordinary brokerage.** A token you can only buy on an exchange and hold has no advantage over a brokerage account — it just adds issuer credit risk and redemption limits on top.

**My read: on the question of why this category should exist at all, Coinbase's $7.5M argues the case better than anyone else's hundreds of millions. But it is far too small to show that the model holds under load.**

## Structure: a direct security, custodied by Alpaca

- Tokens: the **B20** series
- Underlying: **held 1:1 by Alpaca**
- Regulation: **ADGM**
- Chain: **Base**
- Size: about **$7.5M** (2026-10-16)
- US persons: **blocked**

The wrapper is a **direct security**, which under the SEC's four-way split in its Statement on Tokenized Securities (2026-01-30) sits ahead of the tracker certificates used by xStocks and bStocks, and ahead of Ondo's issuer claim.

Restraint is needed though — **"direct security" is a classification, not a guarantee.** I could not verify this product's redemption terms, proof-of-reserve mechanism, or how voting and dividends are handled. As in the Dinari piece: **until those are filled in, a better classification does not mean a better grade.**

## Thirteen contracts, four with supply

The first four launched on 2026-08-24: **NVDAc, METAc, AAPLc, GOOGLc**.

**Thirteen contracts were deployed; only those four carry supply.**

This is the cleanest example in the category of "deployments ≠ live instruments". In the Dinari piece I flagged that its 724 figure cannot separate the two — here Coinbase gives an explicit ratio: **13 : 4, about 31%.**

**Every issuer's instrument count should be read with that discount in mind.** The coexistence of 714 and 100+ for xStocks, and 395+ and 100+ for Ondo, almost certainly comes from the same place.

One more methodology conflict to flag: one source says "10 live", another says "13 contracts, 4 with supply". **I cannot reconcile those two, and the issuer's own disclosure should govern.**

## The only genuinely DeFi-native issuer

This is the heart of it.

On Base, Coinbase's tokens:

- trade through **Uniswap** and **Aerodrome**
- work as **Aave collateral**
- can be borrowed against on **Morpho**
- are priced by **Chainlink** oracles built for tokenized equities

Against the others' liquidity shapes:

| Issuer | Size | Where the liquidity lives |
|---|---:|---|
| Ondo | $984M | **RFQ + mint/redeem**, not in DEX pools |
| bStocks | $914M | **Binance order book**, BSC only |
| xStocks | $847M | CEX order books + **Solana DEX** |
| **Coinbase** | **$7.5M** | **Uniswap / Aerodrome / Aave / Morpho** |

xStocks has genuine Solana DEX depth ($37.7M on NVDA over 30 days), which I credited in that piece. But **"tradable" and "acceptable as collateral" are different things** — the second is what composability actually means.

**Only Coinbase's tokens reached the point of being accepted as collateral by lending protocols.**

What that buys: you can hold NVDAc's economic exposure, pledge it for stablecoins, and deploy those elsewhere. **No brokerage account can ever offer that, and it is the only coherent reason for this category to exist.**

## But $7.5M means it has never been stress-tested

The balance has to be stated.

At that size:

1. **Collateral caps on Aave and Morpho are almost certainly tiny** — liquidation depth, oracle latency and bad-debt risk in violent markets simply do not surface at this scale
2. **Liquidation while the underlying market is shut is an open problem** — US equities close on weekends, on-chain liquidation runs 24/7. If NVDAc is liquidated on a Saturday, where does the liquidation price come from, and how does the dedicated Chainlink oracle behave while the market is closed? **This is what I most want to know and the sources do not answer**
3. **DEX depth is unknown** — no pool depth or volume figures for Coinbase's tokens appear in the material

**So the accurate statement is: Coinbase has shown the route works technically, not that it holds at scale.**

## Company financials: the mirror image of Robinhood

| Metric | COIN (FY2026 Q2) | HOOD (FY2026 Q2) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Revenue | $1,220M | $1,308M |
| Net income | **−$359M** | +$561M |
| Operating margin | **−9.3%** | 53.3% |
| Diluted EPS | **−$1.36** | $0.62 |

Coinbase posted **GAAP losses in two of three recent quarters**.

**But the same conclusion as the Robinhood piece applies: this has no causal link to the tokenized equities business.** A $7.5M book is irrelevant against $1.22B of quarterly revenue. **COIN's loss cannot be used to argue its tokenized stocks are failing, and HOOD's profit cannot be used to argue the reverse.**

What the two share is this: **both have the cash and the licences, and both have invested approximately nothing.** The thing to watch is when either connects main app flow — that day rewrites the table.

## Base versus Robinhood Chain

Both issue on their own chain, and the two are not alike:

- **Robinhood Chain**: closed; the tokens circulate only inside its own app ecosystem
- **Base**: an open L2 with Uniswap, Aerodrome, Aave and Morpho already on it

**That difference is why Coinbase's $7.5M means more for composability than Robinhood's $151M.** Vertical integration is not itself an advantage; **putting the asset into an ecosystem that already exists is.**

## Risks and unknowns

| Item | Severity | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| Too small to test | High | $7.5M cannot demonstrate the model under load |
| Liquidation while markets are shut | High | On-chain liquidation is 24/7; the pricing source is undocumented |
| Redemption terms unseen | Medium | Whether redeemable at all, KYC, minimum and timing unverified |
| No proof of reserve | Medium | Alpaca holds 1:1, but no public PoR mechanism |
| Rights handling unseen | Medium | A direct security should preserve dividends and votes; no explicit statement found |
| US persons barred | Medium | In line with peers |
| Conflicting instrument counts | Low | "10 live" and "13 contracts / 4 with supply" cannot be reconciled |

## What I will watch

1. **Actual collateral size and liquidation history on Aave and Morpho** — the only real evidence the model survives pressure
2. **Liquidation rules while the market is closed** — weekend pricing, or a pause; this decides whether it can be serious collateral
3. **How far supply spreads past four instruments** — when the remaining nine contracts activate
4. **Whether main app flow is connected** — as with Robinhood, the order-of-magnitude line
5. **Whether it applies for a TSV licence** — a direct-security structure should find "identical rights" easier to satisfy

---

**Bottom line.** By size, Coinbase is close to negligible on this table. **By the question of why the category should exist, it is the only issuer with a decent answer** — the value of a tokenized stock is not 24/7 trading (perps do that better and at far greater scale) but the ability to enter a DeFi balance sheet as collateral.

**Coinbase proved the technology, not the scale.** The others are far larger, and their tokens sit in exchange accounts and custody balances — **which differs from a brokerage account only by the issuer credit risk stacked on top.**
