## TL;DR

**This is the smallest of the 20 groups by market cap, the second-highest by median turnover, and not one of its five names escaped the 30-day column.**

- **Combined market cap `$4.67M`, smallest of the 20 groups** (the next smallest is pharma and consumer at `$6.92M`). Combined volume is `$11.23M` — **0.4% of the book by value, 1.3% of it by volume.**
- **Aggregate turnover `2.4053x`, 3.63 times the book rate of `0.6625x`; median member turnover `2.587x`, second-highest of the 20 groups** (the single-stock 2x group is `2.601x`).
- **Four of the five rank in the top 16 of the book by turnover**: ALABB 3rd, DELLB 9th, PENGB 14th, STXB 16th. The second piece, [the denominator trap](/en/blog/bstocks-single-stock-2x-etfs-en), already put the `5.109x` of ALABB in the book top five — **what is new is not that one figure, it is that three of its neighbours are in that top 16 too.**
- **All five are down more than 20% over 30 days** (`−21.36%` to `−33.10%`), something only 46 of the 100 instruments managed; four sit below the 20% mark in their own range against a book median of 22.4%.
- **The counter-example is GLWB.** It is the only member listed more than 100 days (117), ranks only 37th by turnover, has the shallowest 30-day fall in the group but the deepest distance from its high at `−39.0%`, and a range width of `1.770x` almost exactly equal to the mature common-stock median of `1.768x`. **It looks nothing like the other four in any column.**
- **PENGB has been listed 19 days, so its "30-day" `−33.10%` covers at most 19 days.**
- **This snapshot cannot produce a premium.** No underlying reference price, no dividend-reinvestment multiplier.

## 5. Counter-example: GLWB is different in every column

**The table above invites "this group behaves as one". Pull GLWB out and that claim loses half its force.**

| Dimension | GLWB | The other four |
|---|---|---|
| Days listed | **117** | 19–95 |
| Turnover rank | 37th | 3rd, 9th, 14th, 16th |
| 30d | **−21.36%, shallowest in group** | −22.84% to −33.10% |
| From high | **−39.0%, deepest in group** | −29.2% to −34.8% |
| Range width | **1.770x, widest in group** | 1.563x to 1.651x |
| 7d | +0.41% | −7.27% to +3.62% |

**The range width of GLWB at `1.770x` is almost exactly the median of `1.768x` across the 28 common stocks listed 100 days or more.** In other words, **the only member of this group with a full window has the volatility range of an entirely ordinary common stock**; the `1.563x` to `1.651x` of the other four are narrower, and their windows are 19, 60, 88 and 95 days.

That is the half of section 4 contributed by the window. The longer an instrument has been listed, the easier it is to print an extreme — so:

- **"Range widths in this group cluster tightly between 1.56 and 1.65" cannot be said**, because that is four short windows plus one long one, and the long one happens to be the widest.
- **"This group sits deeper below its highs than the book" can be said**, but note that the `−39.0%` at GLWB is built out of 117 days of history, so its high is considerably older than the highs of the other four.

There is a second counter-example, this one against section 3: **the highest-turnover name is not the most volatile one.** The range width of ALABB at `1.595x` ranks only 7th of the ten names in its own cohort:

## 6. Structure: what these five tokens actually are

All of it from the issuer record on this site, none of it invented:

- **The wrapper is a tracker certificate**, issued by BTech Holdings Limited as an ADGM / FSRA listed certificate — **not equity**.
- **No voting rights.**
- **Dividends are pass-through: a 30% US withholding deduction first, then reinvested through a multiplier.** This is what drives the first point in section 8.
- **Reserves are covered by attestation, not an on-chain proof of reserve.** Among peers, xStocks uses Chainlink proof of reserve, updated daily or on a 10% move, verifiable on chain; an attestation is not.
- **Redemption is available, requires KYC, minimum $5.** (Primary redemption at xStocks has a $5,000 minimum.)
- **US persons are blocked.**
- **BNB Chain only, Binance only, BSC deposits only — a wrong-network transfer loses the asset.**
- **The issuance model is exchange-self**: the issuer and the only trading venue are the same entity.

**All five underlyings here are US-listed common shares** (Seagate is incorporated in Ireland and trades as a US-listed share), with no ETF, no ADR and no leveraged product among them. That connects to section 4: **none of the drawdown in this group contains leverage decay; it is the fall of the underlying plus whatever spread this venue adds.**

**The $5 redemption minimum has a concrete meaning here.** These five market caps run `$494.4K` to `$1.79M` while the redemption threshold is `$5` — **a low threshold is not a wide path**: redemption requires KYC and goes through the primary channel of the issuer, and the secondary market is a single venue (Binance). **When the entire market cap of an instrument is under `$500K`, "a redemption channel exists" and "how much can be exited at a reasonable price" are two separate questions, and this snapshot can only answer the existence of the first.**

## 7. This snapshot was taken on a Saturday

The timestamp `2026-10-31T06:06:52Z` is a Saturday morning UTC. The US cash market closed at 20:00 UTC on Friday and does not open again until Monday.

This has already been quantified once on the same issuer: [one stock, four order books](/en/blog/aapl-four-books-tokenized-equity-liquidity-en) lined up four tokenized versions of AAPL on a Saturday and concluded that **a quote outside trading hours is a market maker opinion, not a market price**.

So read the 24-hour changes accordingly: that window (Friday 06:06 UTC to Saturday 06:06 UTC) **contains a full Friday US session**, so it is not a pure closed-market quote; but the snapshot itself lands inside the closure, and **for the two-plus days until Monday, these token prices are the only prices these names have.**

**This matters especially here, because this is one of the highest-turnover groups on the book.** The 24-hour changes are `+1.46%` (GLWB), `−1.87%` (STXB), `−4.16%` (DELLB), `−4.73%` (PENGB) and `−4.88%` (ALABB). **In a period when the underlying is already closed, those percentages measure buying and selling on this venue, not the pricing of new information about the underlying** — and since this is the most active group, that venue-local price discovery is a larger share of these numbers than of anyone else.

## 8. What this method cannot compute

**First, no premium or discount.** The snapshot holds only the price of the token and **no reference price for the underlying**. More decisively, dividends take a 30% US withholding deduction and are then reinvested through a multiplier — **without the current multiplier, token unit price and share price are not comparable in the first place.** No premium figure appears anywhere here.

**Second, no shares per token.** The snapshot carries no conversion ratio, so circulating supply converts into no claim at all.

**Third, the windows behind range width are not equal, and this group is the one most affected.** The five run 117 / 95 / 88 / 60 / 19 days — **only GLWB makes the list of 46 instruments listed 100 days or more** (24th narrowest of them). The widths of the other four cannot be compared against mature instruments, which is what the whole of section 5 is about.

**Fourth, for an instrument listed under 30 days, the "30-day" field does not cover 30 days.** Fifteen instruments on the book have been listed under 30 days and 11 under 20 — **PENGB has 19, so its `−33.10%` does not correspond to 30 days.** The proof sits on another instrument: APPB has been listed 5 days and its 7-day and 30-day fields are identical (`−16.11%` both), so the field is clipped to available history. **In the section 4 table, the complete windows are GLWB, DELLB, ALABB and STXB.**

**Fifth, market cap is the CMC definition**: price times circulating supply. The book total of `$1,318.38M` here is the sum of these 100 rows, while the bStocks row on [the /stocks page of this site](/en/stocks) shows an on-chain size of `$914M` — **two numbers from different sources at different times, and they do not reconcile.**

**Sixth, 100 of 105.** CMC reports 105 instruments; this snapshot holds 100. The missing 5 are in none of these figures.

**Seventh, one chain.** All 100 exist only on BNB Chain — no cross-chain liquidity fragmentation, **and no redundancy either.**

**Conclusion.** This group shows up at two extremes at once: **it is the smallest of the 20 groups by market cap (`$4.67M`) and the second-highest by median member turnover (`2.587x`)**, with four members in the top 16 of the book by turnover — no other group contributes four names to that list. The second piece covered the `5.109x` at ALABB; **what this piece adds is its neighbours: DELLB 9th, PENGB 14th, STXB 16th.**

In the 30-day column not one of the five escaped: all down more than 20% (only 46 instruments on the book are), four sitting deeper than `−30%` below their highs, all five in the bottom half of their own listing cohort and two the worst in theirs. **But this group was not hit hardest — eighteen instruments fell further than PENGB, mostly leveraged ETFs. What characterizes it is uniformity, not depth.**

And that uniformity needs a discount of its own. **GLWB is the only member listed more than 100 days and it looks nothing like the other four in any column**: 37th on the book by turnover, the shallowest 30-day fall in the group, the deepest distance from its high, and a range width of `1.770x` almost exactly the mature common-stock median of `1.768x`. **The narrower ranges of the other four (`1.563x`–`1.651x`) are produced by windows of 19 to 95 days, not by being calmer.** The one clean conclusion left is this: **for their own size, these five are traded unusually often, and in this particular month not one of them got away.**
