## TL;DR

**These are the five companies the whole AI narrative rests on, and in this channel they are the quietest instruments on the book.**

- **Of the six narrowest ranges among the 46 instruments listed 100 days or more, three are in this group**: NVDAB `1.286x` (3rd narrowest), TSMB `1.322x` (4th), AVGOB `1.350x` (6th). The only things narrower are two broad index ETFs.
- **Group turnover of `0.6234x` sits below the book `0.6625x`.** Three of the five are in the bottom quartile of the book by turnover: AVGOB 92nd, TSMB 85th, AMDB 79th.
- **One name is the whole sector.** NVDAB holds `$43.80M`, `12.1%` more than the other four combined (`$39.08M`), and did `$38.93M`, `3.05 times` their total (`$12.75M`). **Remove Nvidia and the remaining four turn over `0.3261x`, under half the book figure.**
- **But "semiconductors are simply narrow here" is wrong.** Over an identical 110-day window starting the same day, MRVLB — another fabless chip designer — has a range width of `1.888x`, `40%` wider than AVGOB `1.350x`; and SNDKB, listed the same day as NVDAB, is `2.432x`, `1.89 times` NVDAB. **Narrow is a property of these five names, not of this industry.**
- **A narrow range does not mean it held up.** The AVGOB low is `74.1%` of its high, and it is sitting at `6.5%` of that range, `−24.2%` from the high. **Narrow says it fell slowly, not that it did not fall.**
- **Two of the five track ADRs** (TSMB, ARMB). An ADR is itself a depositary receipt, so these two tokens are a token over a certificate over a receipt — one layer more than a US common stock.

## 6. Structure: two ADRs mean one more layer

All of this comes from the issuer record on this site, the same data behind [/stocks/bstocks](/en/stocks/bstocks):

- **Legal wrapper**: tracker certificate, ADGM / FSRA listed, issued by BTech Holdings Limited. The holder gets a certificate that tracks a price, not a share registration.
- **Voting**: none.
- **Dividends**: pass-through, but **a 30% US withholding deduction comes first, and the remainder is reinvested through a multiplier**.
- **Redemption**: available, KYC required, `$5` minimum.
- **Proof of reserve**: attestation, not a live on-chain proof.
- **US persons**: blocked.
- **Chain**: BNB Chain only. BSC deposits only, so a wrong-network transfer loses the asset.
- **Issuance model**: exchange self-issued; the only venue is Binance, operated by the issuer.
- **Grade on this site**: B.

Two of those matter extra for this group.

**TSMB and ARMB carry one layer more than the other three.** TSMC is listed in Taiwan and Arm is a UK company, and what trades in the United States for both is an ADR — a receipt issued by a depositary bank representing some number of foreign shares. So the chain for these two tokens is **foreign shares, depositary bank, ADR, ADGM tracker certificate, BNB Chain token**: four layers, against three for NVDAB / AVGOB / AMDB, whose underlyings are US-domestic common stock. **Each extra layer adds a fee set, an operating rule set and a counterparty**, and none of that is in this data.

**That 30% records only one deduction.** The issuer record states "deduct 30% US withholding, then reinvest through a multiplier". For a US common stock that is the whole story; for an ADR, the receipt may already have been through a source-country step when it received the foreign distribution, and depositary banks typically charge a service fee as well. **This data records the US step and says nothing about what happened before it** — this piece does not guess at rates, because the snapshot has none.

One related point about voting: most of Arm free float is held by SoftBank, and TSMC governance differs from a typical US company. **None of that reaches the token holder, because voting at the certificate layer is "none"** — structurally, governance differences between the underlying companies are flattened out entirely by this wrapper.

## 7. This snapshot was taken on a Saturday

The timestamp `2026-10-31T06:06:52Z` is a Saturday morning UTC. US cash equities closed on Friday at 20:00 UTC and do not reopen until Monday — **for more than two days after this snapshot, these token prices are the only prices these names have.**

One detail worth stating for this group: **the reference market for both ADRs is still the US market.** ADRs trade in New York, so the "closed" moment for TSMB and ARMB is the same as for the other three, unlike a stock listed directly in Seoul or Taipei. But their **underlying companies** have their own sessions in Taiwan and the UK, and a gap between an ADR price and the local foreign price exists by construction — a layer entirely invisible in this snapshot.

This has already been quantified once on the same issuer: [one stock, four books](/en/blog/aapl-four-books-tokenized-equity-liquidity-en) lays the four tokenized versions of AAPL side by side on a Saturday and concludes that **a quote outside trading hours is a market maker opinion, not a market price**. The 24-hour moves here (NVDAB `−1.97%`, AMDB `−3.20%`) run from Friday 06:06 UTC to Saturday 06:06 UTC and **contain one complete US session**, so they are not pure closed-market quotes.

## 8. What this method cannot compute

**First, no premium or discount.** The snapshot holds only the token price, with no reference price for the underlying stock or ADR. More to the point, the bStocks dividend mechanism is "deduct 30% US withholding, then reinvest through a multiplier" — **without knowing the current multiplier, the token unit price and the underlying share price are not comparable in the first place.** So no premium figure appears here.

**Second, no shares per token.** The snapshot carries no conversion ratio. AMDB has 20,585 tokens outstanding at `$510.80` each, and neither number converts into a share count without that ratio. **An ADR carries a ratio of its own** (how many foreign shares one receipt represents), also absent from this data, so TSMB and ARMB sit two unknowns away from any share count.

**Third, no cause for the narrow ranges.** This piece can say the price range of these five tokens is narrow; it cannot say the underlying stocks moved little, because the snapshot holds no underlying price series. Range width is an observation on the **token side**, shaped at once by underlying volatility, market-maker behaviour and the liquidity of this channel, and those three cannot be separated.

**Fourth, the range-width windows are unequal.** These five are 142/110/132/110/110 days. The book comparison uses only the 46 instruments listed 100 days or more, and even there the windows differ by more than a month — the longer something is listed, the more easily it reaches an extreme, which makes the NVDAB `1.286x` harder to achieve over the longest window, not easier.

**Fifth, market cap is CMC's definition** (price times circulating supply), and the book total of `$1,318.38M` is the sum of these 100 rows; the bStocks row on [this site's /stocks page](/en/stocks) shows an on-chain size of `$914M` — **two figures from a different source and a different moment, which will not tie out.** This piece uses the former throughout.

**Sixth, 100 of 105.** CMC reports 105 instruments; the snapshot holds 100. The missing five appear in no figure here.

**Seventh, turnover is 24h volume divided by market cap.** A small denominator inflates the ratio, so the market caps of the four high-turnover instruments in section 4 (IBMB, ASMLB, QCOMB, SMHB) all sitting under `$2.5M` has to be read along with them — **their high turnover must not be taken as better liquidity elsewhere in the industry.**

---

**Conclusion.** The five semiconductor design and fab tokens hold `6.29%` of book value and did `5.92%` of its volume — in this channel, the five companies the whole AI narrative rests on are traded less than their size would imply. They are also the narrowest price ranges available: **among the 46 instruments listed 100 days or more, and excluding the two broad index ETFs, three of the four narrowest single instruments are members of this group.**

But that profile does not belong to the semiconductor industry, only to these five names. **MRVLB, listed the same day over an identical window and also a data-centre chip designer, is `40%` wider; SNDKB, listed the same day as NVDAB, is `1.89 times` wider; and the two lowest-turnover instruments on the book, AMATB and COHRB, are both semiconductor-chain companies and neither is in this group.** Dispersion inside the industry beats dispersion between industries.

Two readings travel from this. **First, a narrow range has to be read next to the position in range** — AVGOB has the 6th narrowest width on the book and is sitting at `6.5%` of it: it fell slowly, it did not avoid falling. **Second, this group having "average turnover close to the book" is one instrument doing the work** — remove NVDAB and the remaining four print `0.3261x`, under half the book.
