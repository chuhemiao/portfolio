## TL;DR

**The easiest mistake to make with this group is to write up the quietness of two instruments as the quietness of a sector.**

- **VSTB turns over at `0.208x`, the sixth-lowest of all 100**, on `$105.3K` of daily volume, which ranks 98th. **GEVB turns `0.307x` on `$409.0K`, ranking 95th.** Those two really do barely move.
- **But the group as a whole turns over at `0.8832x`, above the `0.6611x` of the other 95.** The other three sit at or above the book average of `0.6625x`: FLNCB `2.197x`, USARB `1.632x`, BEB `0.680x`. **"This channel has no interest in infrastructure stocks" is backwards at the group level.**
- **The coldness of VSTB comes from its listing batch, not from power.** Of the five instruments listed on 2026-10-19, TMFB (a 3x Treasury ETF) at `0.215x` is only 3.5% above it and BRKBB (Berkshire) at `0.223x` only 7.0% above. **Three instruments with nothing in common but a listing date.**
- **And that runs against the pattern in the book.** The 15 instruments listed in October turn over at `1.143x` as a cohort, the highest of any month. VSTB and GEVB both listed in October and sit at the slowest end of the book.
- **FLNCB has the fifth-widest range on the book at `3.044x`**, and the second-widest of any common stock. SOXSB, listed the same day (2026-07-28), is a **3x inverse** ETF at `2.695x` — **identical window, and the unlevered one is wider.**
- **Four of the five are worse than the book median of `−16.71%` over 30 days**: `−26.55%` / `−29.11%` / `−33.18%`. Only GEVB (`−4.94%`) and VSTB (`−9.88%`) look better, and they have been listed 5 and 12 days — **those figures are their entire history and cannot be compared with anyone else's 30 days.**
- **This snapshot cannot produce a premium.** It carries no underlying reference price and no dividend-reinvestment multiplier, **so this piece publishes no premium or discount figure at all** — not an omission, an impossibility.

## 2. Two instruments barely move

Start with the part that holds.

- **VSTB turns over at `0.2082x`, the sixth-lowest of all 100**, with 24h volume of `$105.3K` ranking 98th — above only TMFB at `$91.6K` and CRMB at `$91.8K`.
- **GEVB turns over at `0.3071x`, the fifteenth-lowest**, with `$409.0K` ranking 95th.

**What is worth noticing in that table is not that VSTB is sixth, but what surrounds it.** First and second (AMATB, COHRB) carry market caps of $9.02M and $8.44M, fifth (DRAMB) $19.64M and ninth (AVGOB) $17.80M — **their turnover is low because the denominator is large.** VSTB, TMFB, BRKBB and CRMB all sit below $600K; **their turnover is low because the numerator is small. There genuinely is almost no trading.**

So all section 2 establishes is one sentence: **the absolute volumes of VSTB and GEVB really are in the tail of the book.**

## 3. But the group as a whole turns faster than the book

Split the group from the other 95:

| Bucket | Market cap | 24h volume | Turnover |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| These 5 | $8.36M (0.6%) | $7.39M (0.8%) | **0.8832x** |
| Other 95 | $1,310.02M | $866.07M | **0.6611x** |
| All 100 | $1,318.38M | $873.46M | 0.6625x |

**The group turns over 33.6% faster than the other 95.**

The reason is the other three instruments: **FLNCB at `2.197x` (20th-highest on the book), USARB at `1.632x` (25th-highest), and BEB at `0.680x`, slightly above the book figure of 0.6625x.** Three of five sit above the book average.

So "this channel has no interest in infrastructure stocks" **cannot stand as the conclusion for this group**. On this snapshot the accurate statement is:

- **two instruments have almost no trading** (VSTB, GEVB),
- **two instruments rank in the book's top 25 by turnover** (FLNCB, USARB),
- **and together the group is more active than the book.**

Lay out the internal spread: **the `2.197x` on FLNCB is 10.6 times the `0.208x` on VSTB.** A five-instrument group with an order of magnitude inside it — at which point "group turnover" describes no member.

## 6. Structure: all five share one wrapper

- **The wrapper is a tracker certificate**, not equity. The issuing entity is BTech Holdings Limited, ADGM / FSRA listed certificates.
- **No voting rights.**
- **Dividends are passed through**: a 30% US withholding deduction first, then reinvestment through a multiplier (see section 8).
- **Reserves are covered by attestation, not an on-chain proof of reserve.** Among comparable issuers xStocks uses Chainlink proof of reserve, which updates daily or on a 10% move in reserves and can be checked on-chain. An attestation cannot.
- **Redemption requires KYC**, with a $5 minimum.
- **US persons are blocked.**
- **One chain.** BNB Chain only, BSC deposits only, so a wrong-network transfer loses the asset.

This is a description of structure, not an allegation. It carries one concrete implication for this group: **the 24h volume on VSTB is `$105.3K`, while the redemption threshold is $5 with KYC.** Structurally there are two ways out — sell on the venue, or redeem through the primary route — **and on the venue route, the entire day of trading in this instrument is $105.3K.** That does not say redemption is unavailable. It says that at this size, the difference in width between the two routes matters more than it does on a head instrument.

## 7. The snapshot was taken on a Saturday

The timestamp `2026-10-31T06:06:52Z` is a Saturday morning UTC. The US cash market closed Friday at 20:00 UTC and does not open again until Monday. **All five underlyings here are US-listed, so at the moment of this snapshot not one of the five reference prices exists.**

The same issuer has already been measured on this point once: [one stock, four books](/en/blog/aapl-four-books-tokenized-equity-liquidity-en) laid the four tokenized versions of AAPL side by side on a Saturday and concluded that **a quote outside trading hours is a market maker opinion, not a market price**.

The 24-hour window here (Friday 06:06 UTC to Saturday 06:06 UTC) **contains one full US session**, so it is not a pure closed-market quote. But the snapshot itself lands inside the close, and **for the two-plus days from there until Monday, these token prices are the only prices these names have.**

One extra layer for this group, and an important one: **GEVB has been listed 5 days and VSTB 12.** The 30-day figure the snapshot gives GEVB, `−4.94%`, is identical to its 7-day figure, because before that it did not exist; the `−9.88%` on VSTB is likewise its entire history. **Neither figure belongs in any 30-day comparison** — including a comparison with the other three members of this group.

## 8. What this method cannot calculate

**First, no premium or discount.** The snapshot carries only the token price and **no reference price for the underlying**. More fundamentally, the bStocks dividend mechanism takes a 30% US withholding deduction and then reinvests through a multiplier — **without knowing the current multiplier, the token unit price and the underlying share price are not comparable quantities**. So this piece publishes no premium figure. That is an impossibility, not an oversight.

**Second, no shares per token.** The snapshot has no conversion ratio. The 1,548 GEVB tokens in circulation cannot be converted into any number of shares without it.

**Third, the range-width windows are not equal length, and in this group that is severe.** This group runs 5 / 12 / 88 / 95 / 95 days, **a 19-fold spread**. Book-wide comparisons use only the 46 instruments listed 100 days or more — **not one of these five is among those 46**, which is why the widths in section 5 are compared only against instruments listed the same day and never enter the book-wide medians by type.

**Fourth, market cap is the CMC definition**: price × circulating supply. The book total of $1,318.38M here is these 100 rows added up, while [the /stocks page on this site](/en/stocks) shows $914M of on-chain value for the bStocks row — **two figures from different sources at different moments, and they will not reconcile.** This piece uses the former throughout and timestamps it everywhere.

**Fifth, 100 of 105.** CMC reports 105 instruments, the snapshot holds 100, and the missing 5 are in none of these statistics. **That matters more for this group than most**: the book holds only two power instruments, and if any of the missing 5 is a utility, the sample-size conclusion in section 4 changes.

**Sixth, turnover = 24h volume ÷ market cap.** The lowest-turnover table in section 2 already shows that the same ratio can arise two ways — a large denominator or a small numerator — **so a turnover ranking cannot be taken as an activity ranking.** It has to be read next to absolute volume.

**Seventh, "the physical bottleneck of AI" is a layer this piece drew.** The snapshot has no industry field. Grouping generation, storage and rare earths together rests on what each company does, not on any classification from the issuer or from CMC. **And this layer holds only 5 samples in this snapshot, 2 of them listed less than two weeks — so only 3 are actually usable for comparison.**

---

**Conclusion.** The most honest conclusion for this group is a correction. **The volumes of VSTB and GEVB really are in the tail of the book** (98th and 95th), but "this channel has no interest in infrastructure stocks" is backwards at the group level: **the group turns over at `0.8832x` against `0.6611x` for the other 95**, because FLNCB at `2.197x` and USARB at `1.632x` both rank in the book's top 25. The five differ by 10.6-fold internally, at which point the group average describes no member.

**And the coldness of VSTB does not even belong to the label "power."** TMFB (a 3x Treasury ETF) and BRKBB (Berkshire), listed the same day, are only 3.5% and 7.0% above it, and the only thing the three share is a listing date — **while the 15 instruments listed in October turn over at `1.143x` as a cohort, the highest on the book.** The genuinely clean comparison is GEVB: listed the same day as APPB with market caps 1% apart and volumes 2.7 times apart.

The last point runs the opposite way from "nobody wants them": **the `3.044x` range on FLNCB is the fifth-widest on the book and the second-widest of any common stock, 11.5% wider than a 3x inverse ETF over the identical window.** A sector can simultaneously have no trading and extreme volatility — **volume and volatility are two separate questions, so ask them separately.**
